Counterintelligence Programs

During May, 1967, a leaflet was mailed to about 40 individuals in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, area. Recipients were labor union leaders, churchmen, members of the American Society of Friends, and the American Legion Posts in vicinity of the residence of Arthur Edelman. Some of the recipients were associated with Edelman in promoting demonstrations against the war in Vietnam and the leaflet was designed to present the membership and leadership role of Edelman and his wife, Beth, in the Western Pennsylvania Communist Party (CP).

Arthur Edelman, after being made aware of leaflet, announced it was time to come out in the open. An informant advised that Arthur Tuden, a college instructor in Pittsburgh, who was active in the antiwar movement, commented he was considering being more circumspect in his dealings with Edelman. Subsequent to circulation of leaflet, Edelman claimed the organization Trade Unionists For Peace in Pittsburgh was on verge of collapse because of friction among its leaders.
