SANITATION WORKERS STRIKE, MEMPHIS, TENNESSEE, RM. Racial Matter

RE MEMPHIS TELS, MARCH TWENTY EIGHT, NINETEEN SIXTY EIGHT.

FOLLOWING IS SUMMARY OF MARCH TWENTY EIGHT ACTIVITIES;

ON MARCH TWENTY EIGHT, LT. E. H. ARKIN, INSPECTIONAL BUREAU, MEMPHIS POLICE DEPT., ADVISED THAT THE MASS MARCH TO BE LED BY REV. MARTIN LUTHER KING, JR., IN SUPPORT OF SANITATION WORKERS

STARTED AT AROUND ELEVEN AM CST WITH ESTIMATED FIVE THOUSAND WORKERS, MANY OF WHOM WERE NEGRO TEENAGERS. MARCH STARTED AT CLAYBORN TEMPLE, LOCATED HEART OF NEGRO AREA, AND KING JOINED MARCH AFTER IT PROGRESSED ONE BLOCK. HE WALKED WITH MARCHERS TO VICINITY BEALE AND MAIN STREETS, MADE NO SPEECHES PRIOR TO MARCH, MADE NO KNOWN UTTERANCES DURING MARCH, AND BY TIME HE REACHED ABOVE POINT RAMPANT BREAKING OF STORE WINDOWS AND LOOTING WAS BEING PERPETRATED BY MARCHERS, WHEREUPON KING AND A FEW ASSOCIATES RAN TO A NEARBY CAR AND LEFT MARCH, PROCEEDS TO RIVERMONT MOTEL WHERE THEY REMAINED THROUGH AFTERNOON AND EVENING. BY NOON, POLICE HAD BROKEN UP CROWD, BEING FORCED TO USE TEARS, BUT SPORADIC LOOTING HAS CONTINUED THROUGH AFTERNOON
