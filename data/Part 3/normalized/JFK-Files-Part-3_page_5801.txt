Senator Church concurred in this arrangement; however, subsequently on August 16, 1975, Mr. Lilliff advised that Senator Church had reconsidered his agreement to the aforementioned procedures as a result of persuasion by the BCG Staff. Lilliff, at that time, indicated a letter recording Senator Church's change of attitude had been drafted. To date, no such formal acknowledgement has been received by this Bureau and no interviews have been attempted in the interim.

In his October 15, 1975, communication, Senator Church again raises this issue. Although he does note that the matter was originally discussed with you ' some weeks ago," he makes no mention of the agreement reached at that time. Apparently, as some sort of precedent, he does cite the fact that the FBI did make available for use and interview the names of Internal Revenue Service (IRS) personnel who cooperated with the FBI in certain COMLLRDP activities. We do not consider release of those identities on our part to be a concession with respect to confidentiality of sources in general since we specifically stipulated that we considered IRS personnel to be U. S. Government officials acting in their capacity as such.

It is clear that the merits of this issue have not changed since the agreement reached between yourself and Senator Church on August 14, 1975. I feel very strongly that the issue of confidentiality of sources is vital to the continuing effectiveness and integrity of the FBI in its investigative operations.
