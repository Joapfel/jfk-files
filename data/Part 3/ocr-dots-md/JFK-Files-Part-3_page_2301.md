Dear Mr. Chairman:

You will recall that by letter of January 27, 1970,
from Senate Majority Leader L. I. Ransfield and an
minority Leader Rush Scott, we were advised of the Senate's plan to conduct an investigation
and study of intelligence activities by Government agencies. The scope
of the study was defined in Senate Resolution 21 of the 94th Congress.
The letter specifically requested that we not destroy or otherwise
dispose of any records or documents which might have a bearing on
the subjects under investigation or relating to the matters specified
in Section 2 of Senate Resolution 21. That section of the Resolution
described the Senate's extensive interest in the domestic intelligence,
as well as foreign counterintelligence, activities of Executive Branch
agencies, including the FBI.

In accordance of that request of the leadership of
the United States Senate, I immediately issued instructions to all
offices and divisions of this Bureau establishing a moratorium on the
destruction of all records of whatever description. In retrospect, I
now see that the moratorium is need not have been as all-encompassing
as that, but this was done to assure that there could be no question of
my intention for the FBI to comply fully with the request with regard
to the preservation of relevant records in which the Senate will develop
an interest.
