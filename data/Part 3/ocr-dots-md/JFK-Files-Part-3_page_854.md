## 2. Guidelines and Procedures Currently Used by the FBI and the Department of Justice in Determining Whether a Proposed National Security Electronic Surveillance Falls Within/Without the Keith Decision

The Keith decision applies solely to a domestic organization (and its members) "with no significant connection with a foreign power." The issues are what constitute a "domestic organization" and "significant connection."

The Department of Justice has issued the FBI no formal oral or any written guidelines on these issues.

The reason is that the standard to be applied is a "facts and circumstances" test in each case in the light of the Supreme Court's language in the Keith case and the Department of Justice's position as stated by Mr. Maroney before the Subcommittee.

The Supreme Court in Keith said that while it was "attempt(ing) no precise definition" the scope of its decision was limited to a "domestic organization...composed of citizens of the United States...which has no significant connection with a foreign power, its agents or agencies." The Court also recognized the difficulty in distinguishing "between 'domestic' and 'foreign' unlawful activities directed against the Government of the United States where there is collaboration in varying degrees between domestic groups or organizations and agents or agencies of a foreign power."
