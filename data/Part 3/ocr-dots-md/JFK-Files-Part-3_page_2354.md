With reference to the fomenting of racial strife and student disorders in the United States by Cuba, our investigation of this matter is a case in point. He arrived in the United States in August, 1967, to assume duties at the CMUN. He left the United States on February 20, 1969, at the State Department's request based upon the results of our investigation of him. At the time of his departure, he held the rank of Counselor, second in order of diplomatic precedence at the CMUN.

Our investigation of this matter determined that he was a principal link between the Cuban Government and leaders of black extremist and New Left student groups in the United States. He gave advice and counsel to such leaders, including this matter and its association with the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC), furnished Cuban propaganda materials to them; and arranged visits to Cuba for unknown purposes by many of them.

During early May, 1968, while in Washington, D. C., ostensibly as a representative of the Cuban Government to the Pan American Health Organization, this government instructed this agency to "continue to follow Plan A-5," which was then agitating in Washington, D. C., following the serious racial disorders here of the preceding month. The significance of these instructions to the government is unknown; however, an SDS plan of similar terminology resulted in an undisciplined mass protest and violence at Columbia University, also during April, 1968.

The intelligence functions of this agency at the CMUN appear to have been taken over by this agency, but superior to this in the CIS hierarchy.
