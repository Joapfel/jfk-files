MEMORANDUM FOR THE FILE

SUBJECT: January 30, 1964, Meeting of the President's Foreign Intelligence Advisory Board

The President's Foreign Intelligence Advisory Board convened in regular meeting at 9:00 a.m. on January 30, 1964, at the Board's offices, Room 297 Executive Office Building. Present were Chairman Clifford and Board members Gray, Langer, Murphy, Doolittle, Pace, and Land (Dr. Baker was unavoidably detained in New Jersey and joined the meeting later in the morning); and Messrs. Coyne and Ash.

In executive session, Chairman Clifford gave Board members a fill-in on the brief talk he had with President Johnson a few days after the President took office on November 22. Chairman Clifford had taken the occasion to relate to the President some of the highlights of the function performed by the Board for President Kennedy, and the composition of the Board. Although President Johnson gave no indication that he had previously known of the work of the Board, he expressed appreciation for the effort which the Board had made on behalf of President Kennedy. The President said that he was comforted to know that this group of outstanding citizens is giving attention to the important subject of intelligence, and he asked Mr. Clifford to inform the Board members that he wished them to continue with this important task.

The President's Secretary, who was present, also attended. He stated that he was unable to attend because he was on leave. He did, however, speak to Chairman Clifford a short time before leaving. Chairman Clifford indicated that he was very pleased with the results of the meeting and that he believed that there was a high probability of a settlement being reached. He also suggested that a working group be established to work out details of the settlement. This working group was to consist of Mr. Clifford, Mr. Deane, Mr. Johnson, Mr. Kennedy, Mr. Murray, Mr. Doolittle, and myself. I was to act as the representative of the President. I was to have a say on all questions relating to the terms of settlement. I was to be given a copy of the draft agreement before the meeting ended and was to have ten days in which to approve or reject it. If I rejected it, then I was to have another ten days in which to propose alternative terms. If I did not respond within those twelve days, then I was to be considered as having accepted the terms of the draft agreement. If I did not respond within the two-week period following the receipt of the draft agreement, then I was to be considered as having accepted the terms of the draft agreement with modifications. If I did not respond within the additional ten-day period, then I was to be considered as having accepted the terms of the draft agreement with modifications.
