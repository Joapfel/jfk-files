Martin Luther King, Jr.

12/17/75

This informative memorandum records unsuccessful efforts to pinpoint the typewriter on which an anonymous letter, apparently directed to the subject, was typed. The original was located in papers left behind upon the retirement of former Assistant to the Director William C. Sullivan.

Examination of the questioned document by the Laboratory established that the typing was done on a Royal typewriter with pica-style type. We checked available inventory records maintained by the Administrative Division, as well as our own records in INTD, to locate all Royal typewriters that were in the then Domestic Intelligence Division (DID) around 1964. This search was based on the suspicion that the note was typed by Sullivan on a typewriter accessible to him in 1964, when he was Assistant Director of the DID.

Our checks revealed that only four typewriters of the type sought were likely in the DID in 1964. All four are presently in INTD, and three of them were readily eliminated as not identical to the one that produced the questioned document because all three have closed “4s,” whereas the questioned document has an open-ended “4.” The fourth typewriter, serial number KMG 13-4320913, is maintained in room 4410 and charged to the IS-2 Section. It was examined by Supervisor F. M. Devine of the Document Section of the Laboratory, who advised that it is not identical to the typewriter that produced the questioned document.
