During July 1971, a conversation was intercepted, and when pieced together with previously monitored conversations and other background, enabled us to apprehend, without injury or incident, two Black Panther Party members wanted for the murder of a policeman.

Physical surveillance of a meeting to plan the murder of Black Panther Party rivals, the meeting site having been learned of via electronic surveillance, resulted in the apprehension of two fugitives. The apprehension caused a gun battle; however, the electronic surveillance information allowed for advance planning, which cut the risk to arresting officers and bystanders.

Electronic surveillance of the Students for a Democratic Society Headquarters, Chicago, Illinois, provided information on plans for the “Days of Rage” violent demonstrations in Chicago during October 1969. This advance information, relayed to Chicago police, enabled them to anticipate, to some degree, destructive activity and to concentrate their force where needed.
