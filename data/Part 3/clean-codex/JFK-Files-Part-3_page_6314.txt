March 2, 1965

Memorandum for Mr. Tolson

Mr. DeLoach (DeLooch)

The Attorney General called and advised that he had talked to Senator Long. Last night, Senator Long's committee was looking into mail covers, etc. The Attorney General stated he thought somebody had already spoken to Senator Long and said he did not want to get into any national security area and was willing to take steps not to do this. The Attorney General stated that Mr. Fensterwski was present for part of the meeting, and Mr. Fensterwaki had said that he had some possible witnesses who are former Bureau agents and, if they were asked whether mail was opened, they would take the Fifth Amendment. The Attorney General stated that before they are called, he would like to know who they are and whether they were ever involved in any program touching on national security; if not, it is their own business, out of which we would want to know. The Attorney General stated the Senator promised that he would have a chance to look at the names if he wanted to, personally and confidentially, and the list would have any names involving national security deleted. He would tell the Senator how many, but no more.

The Attorney General stated that the Postmaster General is going down there this morning, almost as soon as he, the Attorney General, thought would be helpful to Chief Inspector Montague of the Post Office Department.

The Attorney General stated that Senator Long also said he is not going to propose legislation to overthrow mail covers, as he thought they served a useful purpose, but he also thinks that control should be tightened. I stated I thought there was great laxity in the matter of mail covers and in the matter of tapping telephones.
