Federal Bureau of Investigation, U.S. Department of Justice, Communications Section.

September 26, 1961.

Teletype. Urgent, 9-26-61, 8:33 p.m. TCH.

To Director, FBI, 92-2914, 92-2918.  
From SAC, Pittsburgh, 92-236, 92-228.

Gabriel Mannarino, AKA, AR; Samuel Mannarino, AKA, AR. On the evening of September 25, a PCI advised that gambling of all types has been suppressed in New Kensington, Pennsylvania, to a point beyond anything in his recollection for the last twenty-five years. Numbers, sports bets, and horse bets are down to about twenty-five percent of the normal of one month ago, and card, craps, and barbout games are extinct. Many gamblers in New Kensington fear they will not be employed for a long time and also fear, since they are now aware of the extensive IRS investigation, that they may be indicted federally.

PG 615-C advises that on September 25, Willie Sams, the Mannarino “Brains” in gambling operations, consulted with an attorney in a room in the Carlton House, Pittsburgh, due to Sams’ belief that the IRS is investigating him and may make a successful case against him. Both subjects should be considered armed and dangerous.

Correction: Last line, word 1 should be “subjects.”

End and acknowledgment please.

WA, 8:37 p.m. OK FBI WA RAC.

Tuesday, September 28, 1961.
