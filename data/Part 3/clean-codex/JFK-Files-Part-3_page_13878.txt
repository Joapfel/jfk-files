The next morning he was able to send a letter to his son in New York, describing the events of the previous night. He wrote: “I am so grateful that you are not only well, but also asleep at home when I should be up all night and worry about you.” The letter ended with an appeal for help from God, which the young man did write, and the response came as he had expected.

“O God,” he said, “if there is any power in this world which can save your life, show it now. Save yourself, or else we both will be lost. I have not said anything, because I thought I should lose my breath if I tried to speak. But you must know what I was going to say, and if I had been a man of words, not a man of action, I should have spoken differently. And then you would not have known half of it. For though we were apart for only two days, you have changed. You have become a different person. It has come upon me suddenly, like lightning. It has not been away for long; but it has been so strong and strong, that it seems to have taken all the force out of you. I never was so weak. I have not been able to do what I ought to do. I have been so feeble. You have gained strength which I have not. And I feel so weak and feeble and powerless. And it is so hard to think of going on living. And then there is no help in living. There is no hope. God help us all.” The letter ended with a call to God for help.
