Counterintelligence Programs

Headquarters sent to Southern offices having substantial Klan activity a supply of three different postcards to be mailed anonymously to a selected number of known Klan members. These postcards contained a caricature of a member of the Ku Klux Klan (KKK) in his hood and robe. One attacked the Klan's secrecy of membership, and the other inferred that the Klan leaders were using Klan money for high living, both sensitive points in the Klan.

Numerous mailings were made, some of which received newspaper publicity, thereby causing disruption in the Klan membership. Specific individuals to whom mailings were made are not identified in Headquarters files.
