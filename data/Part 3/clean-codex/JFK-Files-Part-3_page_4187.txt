FBI Washington, D.C. FBI Memphis. 12:56 a.m., urgent, March 29, 1968.

To: Director. From: Memphis (157-1094).

Sanitation Workers Strike, Memphis, Tennessee. Racial Matter.

Re Memphis teletypes, March 28, 1968. Following is summary of March 28 activities: On March 28, Lt. E. H. Arkin, Inspectional Bureau, Memphis Police Department, advised that the mass march to be led by Rev. Martin Luther King, Jr., in support of sanitation workers started at around 11:00 a.m. CST with an estimated five to six thousand workers, many of whom were Negro teenagers. The march started at Clayborn Temple, located in the heart of the Negro area, and King joined the march after it progressed one block. He walked with marchers to the vicinity of Beale and Main Streets, made no speeches prior to the march, made no known utterances during the march, and by the time he reached the above point, rampant breaking of store windows and looting was being perpetrated by marchers, whereupon King and a few associates ran to a nearby car and left the march, proceeding to Rivermont Motel, where they remained through the afternoon and evening. By noon, police had broken up the crowd, being forced to use tear gas, but sporadic looting continued through the afternoon.
