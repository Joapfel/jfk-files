9. Photostat of several thousand checks from various Klan units, payable to Shelton or Alabama Rescue Service, were secured from the House Un-American Activities Committee file. I found only one of these checks which was not endorsed “For deposit only” to Alabama Rescue Service account in Tuscaloosa. A check dated 3/28/64 payable to Robert M. Shelton, in the amount of $50.00, on the account of White Patriots, P. O. Box 7094, Montgomery, Alabama, was endorsed by Shelton and the Auto-Rate Stone in Tuscaloosa. The amount was entered in receipts on the Klan books as a donation from White Patriots of Montgomery. Shelton stated that the $50.00 was for four repairs on his automobile which were used for Klan business.

10. Transcript of the testimony of Ernest H. Merriwether, President, Dixie Engineering Corporation, Mobile, Alabama, was secured from the House Un-American Activities Committee file together with photostats of eight $500.00 checks payable to Shelton during 1963 and 1964. Merriwether was not contacted personally on advice of U. S. Attorney Robert Jansen, South Carolina Judicial District of Alabama. Merriwether has several tax suits pending in Mobile at present.

Merriwether testified that he paid Shelton the following checks:

7/14/63: $500.00  
9/24/63: $500.00  
11/6/63: $500.00  
11/13/63: $500.00  
Total: $2,000.00

1/8/64: $500.00  
2/13/64: $500.00  
3/13/64: $500.00  
4/22/64: $500.00  
Total: $2,000.00

Shelton reported $500.00 from this source on his 1963 return and $1,500.00 on his 1964 return. He contends four of the $500.00 checks “bounced.” The ledger sheets on his personal account at the First National Bank of Tuscaloosa and the deposit tickets to this account disclosed eight $500.00 deposits to the account from Dixie Engineering and only one debit to the accounts for $500.00 for a returned Dixie Engineering check.

D. L. Thomas, accountant for Dixie Engineering, stated that all eight checks to Shelton were posted to their disbursements journal and postings to this journal were made from checks after they cleared the bank.

The eight checks in question were written on six different bank accounts. The banks were contacted and their records indicate all eight checks were eventually paid. However, Shelton’s five exemptions and a standard deduction for the year 1963 will offset his reported income plus the additional $1,500.00 on his 1963 return.
