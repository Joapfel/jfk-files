Cline, presumably reflecting the Director’s wishes, said that the Checklist that day should cover military activity in South Vietnam, the fighting in Laos, Soviet ICBM developments, and problems in the Berlin air corridors and Venezuela.

Around the middle of December, all members of the White House staff, using the Checklist, tried to fix some intelligence problems. When McCone stopped seeing the President daily, the White House staff neglected itself.

They tried to get the President to read the Checklist, but were largely unsuccessful, although McGeorge Bundy was able to do so at one oral briefing, Checklist in hand. On 16 December, Gen. Clifton told OCL’s John Heidemann, “If we can’t penetrate this sort of wall today or tomorrow, we’ll just have to try something else.” In this cloudy atmosphere, the sun came out occasionally. On the 18th, Clifton said that the staff had finally managed to get the President to read “the books”—probably two or three issues.
