Subject: Trip to Congo

Dear Stan,

Just a brief heads up on the Congo business. I arrived on the
Monday following Porter and of course it was a holiday so didn't
and was not contracted until the following day (Tuesday). By this
time I had an agreement ready and in the hands on the Compense:
Air Force or Wednesday. On Thursday we met with the minister of
the cabinet and he indicated all was in order and that we could
be sure he signed the following day (Friday). On Friday nothing
but now we created out Saturday, again nothing happened so there
was nothing to do but wait until Monday. By that time I had talked
with Behaia and got a free ticket on them from Leopoldville to
Brussels so that I'd have more flexibility. On Monday there was
still no action and again move them the benefit of the doubt by
remaining until Tuesday when I could have gotten a 207 flight on
Monday night direct to Brussels. On Tuesday I left Leopoldville in
the afternoon, proceeded to Bozoville and Mba waited 10 hours
for the delayed K.I. flight (DC-7), finally leaving at 3 AM and ar-
riving in Frankfurt at 1900 that night.

As I told you, Major Reiner is an Austrian who acts as an ad-
viser to the Compense Air Force. Major Pierrott, commander of the
air force is young and ambitious. At the present time there is
nothing but a shell and instructors are badly needed. There are,
according to Pierrott, 8 AT-6's and 7 Tiger Moths on order which
will give them a total strength of 9 of each aircraft... They expect
to put a total of 120 cadets into training. I talked alone the
lines of a package deal on training but this didn't sink in. They
just want to get started and so in to detail later. As you can well
imagine, the government structure in the Congo is very shaky and it
will be a long time before the II. & III. forces leave and things settle
in a normal manner. For example, there could be a change in the
govt., including Pierrott, any way and this could be followed by
any number of changes in the weeks following. Any contract we sign
could be thrown out by a new government. In addition there is also
a good possibility of renewed violence which might throw everything
in a turmoil.

Without going in to detail, following are the more important
points of the agreement:

1. Air Force retain us for purpose of hiring personnel for pur-
pose of training mechanic and pilot personnel.

2. Air Force pays transportation of personnel from point of hire
to Leopoldville and return plus air travel done in the Congo.

3. Air Force pays (to the bank in Ely) $1,000.00 for my experience
each payment to be made within 20 days after signing Agreement.

4. Initially, 3 mechanic and 3 pilot instructors will be hired
and Air Force will accept persons of German, Swiss, French or English
nationality

5. Pay of personnel will be responsibility of Air Force and
mechanics will receive $770.00 or $650.00 blue room. Pilots will
receive $870.00 by blue flying-day which is figured at $50.00 for
each 25 hours flown (they anticipate pilots will fly 25 hours per
month) or $750 blue room blue flying-day. In addition, all personnel
will be furnished their meals except on weekends when they are
not working.
