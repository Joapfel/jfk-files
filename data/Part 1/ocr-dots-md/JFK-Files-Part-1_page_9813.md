Subject: LIRING/4 Report of News Concerning her Father in New York.

1. At 1600 on 22/3/68 Rowton visited LIRING/4 at her residence to discuss with her a report she had received from a mutual friend concerning her father. Earlier in the day LIRING/3 had informed Rowton that LIRING/4 had received a disturbing piece of news to the effect that someone had approached her father in New York City and told him to break off all contact with LIRING/4 in order to avoid jeopardising his residence in the U.S. According to LIRING/3, LIRING/4 was convinced as a result of this report that Rowton, and possibly with the knowledge of LIRING/3 himself, had arranged this approach to preclude any uncontact L/4 might have had with her father that might jeopardise her mission to Havana. Rowton assured LIRING/3 that he had take no such action and agreed to talk to LIRING/4 to reassure her also since according to LIRING/3 she was very upset and her confidence in both Rowton and LIRING/3 himself shaken.

2. Rowton reassured LIRING/4 when he saw her but, of course, could not furnish proof of any kind that he had not intervened. He asked several times the identity of the source, but LIRING/4 seatfastly refused to divulge it, saying only that a lawyer friend of her father had written to her stating that the reason her father had not communicated with her over the past three months and had not answered her letters and telephone calls was that "they" had told him not to maintain any contact with his daughter at the risk of losing his residence in the U.S. Rowton asked whether the lawyer's name was Fortuondo (a lead furnished by LIRING/3) but L/4 would not confirm or deny this, saying simply that she would look into the matter herself. She said that she suspected Rowton since he was the only person to whom she had given her father's address in New York and she could not imagine why any other person would have a motive to contact her father in that way. She added that if her father had just given her some sort of explanation she could understand, but acting the way he did indicated that someone had terrorized him into complete silence. Rowton again insisted that he did not in any way contact her father, and would never have done so without first consulting her. He surmised that possibly her father's lawyer had counseled him to break off contact in connection with the application her father was making for a visa for LIRING/4's brother on the ground that the American authorities might deny the visa if they knew of LIRING/4's previous employment in the LIRING offices.

Wallace B. Rowton.
