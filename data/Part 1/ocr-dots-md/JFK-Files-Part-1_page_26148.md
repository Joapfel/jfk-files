AMEASSADOR FREEMAN ATTACHED BY SIEMPRE FBIS No. 85, 30 Apr 64

Mexico City Correspondent's Dispatch in Spanish to PRENSA LATINA Havana 1910 GNT
23 April 1964 (FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY)

(Excerpt) Mexico City--In an article in the Mexico City magazine SIEMPRE on 23 April, Victor Rico Galan writes that U.S. Ambassador Fulton Freeman made a statement to the press about Cuba and Brazil. "In a violent style that seems to come from the caverns of the John F. Kennedy Club," he (criticized) the constitutional government of Jose Ouelart and the Cuban Revolution. He talked like a soapbox orator, as it issuing a political proclamation, forgetting that Mexico maintains friendly relations with the Cuban Government and does have very close relationships with the deposed Brazilian President. Mr. Freeman behaved like a savage guest who spits on his host country.

"This is no great cause for surprise. U.S. ambassadors are constantly interfering in our domestic affairs as if it were the most natural thing. They feel as if they were on conquered ground, and not less like ambassadors than proconsuls who care no consideration or explanation to anybody, as if this country belonged to them and they enjoyed boundless privileges."
