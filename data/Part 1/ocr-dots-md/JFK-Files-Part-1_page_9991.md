> 'Nothing is the letter. Could you give me a copy as soon as possible? This is very important. You have done a very wise thing in coming to see me about all this; much wiser than you could realize.'

L/3: The letter is typewritten on my typewriter and unsigned, but I'm sure anyone who knows Crozco could recognize the style.

Cap: Yes. Could you send it by hand with Jacobo? Whatever you do, please do not telephone and talk about anything sensitive, or use names.

L/3: I could send my wife tomorrow morning, or possibly even later tonight.

Cap: Right, but send it as soon as possible.

L/3: Just one other thing. I or anyone to get in touch with an old friend of mine, Drs. Penafrío. I understand that she is in town, and I might be able to contact her through you. That I suggest is this, what you give her my name and telephone number and ask her to call me at her convenience.

Cap: Fine. I'll do what I can. In any case I'll be in touch with you. Again please don't leave town until you have talked with me again on this matter.

5. Rowton made a date with L/3 for 1030 the following day (9/8/67) to pass to L/3 a copy of the Crozco letter. It will be recalled that he gave the original of the letter to Abelaric Curbelo. Incidentally L/3 stated that no mention of the Cubans was made during his conversation with Capoano.
