COSTA RICA

BE SURRENDERED TO ACCEPT CUBAN LEANS

Panama City, 3 Mar in Spanish 0295 GMT 19 Mar 76 PA

[Text] San Jose, Costa Rica, 10 Mar--The fate of exiled Cuban leader Orlando Bochacó has been complicated by the fact that no government in the hemisphere is willing to grant him territorial autonomy.

Meanwhile, Bochacó began today his 7th day on a hunger strike which he is staging to sell attention to his cause, and is accepting only food in his cell in the prison of Cartagena, next to San Jose, where he is being held.

Bochacó was deprived of his freedom when his presence and illegal entry into Costa Rica were proved. He used a Chilean passport with a false name to enter the country shortly before U.S. Secretary of State Henry A. Kissinger's official visit.

Bochacó's deportation has been ordered, but no government has shown any willingness to receive him. Today, Foreign Affairs Minister Consuelo J. Pacio negotiated with a country not previously consulted--one of the latest to be approved--Pacio explained. The results of these negotiations were negative.

Apparently, Bochacó's guerrilla background influences these propositions.

PACIO ON CUBAN-COSTA RICAN RAPPROCHEMENT

Panama City ACAM in Spanish 0036 GMT 23 Mar 76 PA

[Text] San Jose, Costa Rica, 22 Mar--(ACAN-EPE)--Costa Rican Foreign Minister Consuelo J. Pacio this afternoon rejected criticism by newspapers who have said he is propitiating a rapprochement with the Cuban regime. Pacio said that the Castroite adventure in Angola and the proletarian internationalism doctrine it represents have thrown cold water on the possibility of renewing relations with that island.

The minister thus responded to a newspaper editorial which noted the dangerous inclination of the Foreign Ministry toward reconciliation with Cuba at a time when, according to the commentary, the Havana regime has decided to embark on an expansive and aggressive foreign policy.

Pacio rejected this criticism and freed his ministry from all responsibility for what officials from other ministries said or did after their trip to Cuba. Some have praised the system in effect there.

The foreign minister commented that the adventure in Angola and the theory advanced by Cuba--according to which it is prepared to back insurrections provoked by communist movements--are very much taken into account by Costa Rica and are considered to be enough reason for cooling the possibility of a renewal of relations with Havana.

Pacio also said that he did not share the opinion of the New York TIMES which commented that Jamaica and Guyana have become beachheads for Cuban penetration in the hemisphere.
