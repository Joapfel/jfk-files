ANALYSIS OF THE MARKET

The primary function of the shipyard will be the construction of 20/200-ton boats from steel and wood. This will include shipliboots, lobsterboats, barges, light cargoeships, and passenger and patrol launchers. The secondary function of the Combine will be to provide repair and maintenance service to all boats of this class within the Caribbean of equal or better quality than that offered in Mexico, Trinidad or the United States.

To a degree, the shipyard will attempt specialization in the construction of 35-ton shipliboots for favorable competition in the world market. Construction will be attempted from steel and from wood. Construction from wood will be advantageous because of the procurement of wood in large part, if not all, from the local sawmills in Bluefields. Another advantage of construction from wood is a plan for production in mass. Construction in which prefabricated sections of boats will be constructed. It is possible to visualize in the near future not only the date of fully-equipped shipliboots but also prefabricated parts for those shipyards which do not have the necessary machinery, wood, and other items.

Domestic

The East Coast and Central American Markets! There are only a few countries in America which possess the facilities for construction of boats off the shrimpgebust season. These countries are Peru, Mexico and the United States. The production in Peru is small and relatively insignificant, and its influence in the Caribbean is slight. Mexican boats are constructed from good material, but because of poor labor, together with antiquated designs, they are subject to enormous structural fatigue in heavy seas.
