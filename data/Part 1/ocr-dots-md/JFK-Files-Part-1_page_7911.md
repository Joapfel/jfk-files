On Subject's second day in Mexico, Mr. and Mrs. Mechisporenko, Subject's former Stolat C/O in Mexico, came to visit him at the Hotel Nacional. Subject deliberately kept his son away so that Mechisporenko would find it difficult to have private words with him. However, Mechisporenko came to visit Subject every day thereafter until Subject's departure, bringing gifts of records, Xavier, etc., when Mechisporenko found the opportunity the first thing he told Subject was that he heard Subject had been a "rebel" and had been difficult in Mexico. Subject, in his half-serious manner, poohphoed the idea that he was a rebel and told Mechisporenko earnestly that he didn't have the time, effort or inclination to indulge in the cooperation that was expected of him. He gave Mechisporenko the old line about friends "hasta muerte", but that he couldn't and wouldn't put out the old effort. Mechisporenko said that was too bad, because he must want to present Subject to his chief and now there was no point to it. Subject shrugged this off. Mechisporenko asked Subject if he would be willing to use his office as a letter-drop. Subject said, reprovingly that that was not a very smart idea- since agents writing to his firm and to him should then know of Subject's connection with the Soviets and he would thus be exposed. Nevertheless, Mechisporenko told Subject that they hadn't forgotten about the "branch office" and that Subject shouldn't feel bad about the delay in setting it up. Subject ridiculed that idea, saying that it was the Soviets who should feel bad; so for him, he couldn't be bothered in the future with the trips and time involved. Mechisporenko, still not cheated, asked if Subject would provide information on all changes in USA regulations for travel in Central or South America. Subject was said he had given this information to the Soviets in the past. Mechisporenko then asked Subject to continue to give such information to Valentin.

Subjectelicitedfrom Mechisporenko that he was working in the Latin American Section of the Foreign Ministry, and that he was allowed to come and go as he pleased to visit Subject. In the context of telling Subject that he saw them Subject was not getting along with his Soviet friends, Mechisporenko said that he could be coming back to Mexico next year.

One day while Mechisporenko was in the hotel room, the phone rang and Subject picked up the phone. A female voice asked for "City." Subject banteringly replied in Russian that "City" wasn't around, but wouldn't he do. In the ensuing conversation Subject found out that the caller was a 25 year old student, her address and telephone number, and made a date to meet her the following day at the Latin Station, just off the Metro. Mechisporenko was also in favor of Subject making the date, and as a matter of fact: came the next day to take Subject's son to the circus so that Subject would be free. /Comment: Subject said that he never did go to meet the girl, but told Mechisporenko that he had gone and the girl had not shown up. Subject was of the opinion this was not a provocation by the Soviets, but he shied away because he did not want to be involved in anything indiscreet./

When Alexander Prikodnoy dropped in unexpectedly at Subject's office on 3 December, while Subject's wife and son were present, the Soviet asked when he could have lunch with Subject, and the latter put him off telling Prikodnoy to call next week. /Comment: Prikodnoy did not call./
