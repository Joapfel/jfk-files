10: At 4:00 p.m. on Sunday, 19 September, Jackson returned to the room in the Columbus Hotel, bringing with him Senior Evia and Captain Verezon. Jackson introduced us as Mr. Bruton, a pseudonym I had supplied. He told them that I was a representative of the US Government and that we were interested in the documents in Verona's possession. He assumed then that the information which they might give me would be treated as highly classified material, and he then left the meeting. It developed that Captain Vezon could not speak any English (incidentally, Jackson does not speak Spanish), so that it was necessary to converse with his through Evia, who acted as interpreter. Evia is fluent in English and Spanish.

11. The substance of the information supplied by Verezon concerning the documents in his possession is contained in the second part of this report. Verezon appeared to attach a very high historical value to the contents of his documents and seemed anxious to make them available to US authorities. He claimed any desire for a fee and merely said that he wished to retain the original, since he considered that they constituted an authentic account of the truth of the Communist movement in Cuba, but that we would be entirely welcome to photocopy or otherwise copy the contents of his files.

12. At the conclusion of the interview, I thanked the two Cubans for their courtesy and cooperation and told Senior Evia that I would get in touch with him later in the week either directly or through Mr. Jackson.

13. I asked herewith a letter to Evia confirming our interpretation in the Verezon documents and asking him to notify me when it is expected that they will begin arriving, so that we may attempt to facilitate their entry. You will note that I have signed the letter with the name "Bruton" which is how I was introduced to Evia and Verezon by Jackson. I think the letter should be sent in Washington rather than in New Orleans, so as to allay any suspicions that Evia may harbor the idea that I am not actually a government representative. You will note that I have suggested that Evia write to us in New Orleans in care of Miss Jean M. Faton at Post Office Box 2016. We will, of course, assume that this is a letter-drop.

14. In view of the fact that I refer by implication to the Customs Department, I suggest that you hold the letter to Evia until you or Ciesliedau have discussed the matter with Mr. Enderick, who is the superior of Mr. Wyatt, the supervising Customs Agent in Miami. I suggest that the letter be sent air mail, unregistered. I have left the date blank and I request that you insert it before mailing. If you wish to change the letter in any way, please do so. Evia does not, of course, know any handwriting, so that the letter can be signed by anyone.

William P. Burke
