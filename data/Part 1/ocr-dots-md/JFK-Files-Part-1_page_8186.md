5. Tichborn (P) has several operational ideas which should be of interest to the clandestine services. One about which he feels very strongly and which he stressed when talking to 10/6 (Students) was that he feels a great deal remains to be done in the student and professor fields. Among other related suggestions, he feels that more politically promising students should be brought to the U.S. for study. In order to accomplish the objective of calling such potential leaders to our cause, he feels a thorough spotting program should be undertaken and that the criteria for selection of such individuals should be based on this potential rather than on past academic records per se which is the current basis for selection in current exchange programs. He feels, and rightly so, that good grades not necessarily synonymous with leadership qualities. In Latin universities some students spend some time on their political activities than they do on their academic work, and it is among this group that the potential political leaders are found, hence, a new set of criteria are necessary in order to satisfy our requirements and interests. Tichborn (P) also feels that professors could be recruited to assist in such spotting and also that there should be an expanded exchange of such individuals in the U.S. Our efforts along these lines would not conflict with those exchange programs already in effect since our objectives would be different and the type of student or professor involved would be of a different calibre and nature. 10/6 felt that Tichborn (P) has much potential along these lines and was very much interested in his idea per se. The only difficulty they foresee for the present is that they have no organization in existence under which such an activity would be undertaken and hence, if it were agreed to follow up on this, some kind of a private institution or organization would have to be established and this would require at least six months.
