4. LILANG/3, at his meeting with Rowton the following morning (19/3/68) reported that when he arrived at the place designated he found the Guatemalan Cliva waiting with neither of them saw Hersaval. Cliva, after a wait of about 45 minutes, suggested that they give up and go to a cafeteria, Copa de Leche, on San Juan Batrán. L/3 stated to Rowton that while they were waiting they were joined by another Guatemalan, a young man of about 22 years of age and rather stout, who accompanied them to the cafeteria. The older Guatemalan then showed L/3 a Guatemalan passport in the name of Miguel Angel Cliva Castañeda which he said was false but which enabled him to travel in Mexico. The younger man stated that he had legal documents but did not volunteer his name to L/3. In the course of conversation L/3 learned that the older Guatemalan, Cliva, formerly worked in the advertising business in Guatemala City and that the younger man was familiar with electronic equipment and radio communications problems. The older Guatemalan apologized to L/3 for not having been able to bring along the other FAR colleague, to whom he referred as "Rodolfo" and again promised to introduce him to L/3 the following evening. He promised to telephone L/3 at 1400 the next day to confirm the meeting.

LILANG/3 then told Rowton that the two Guatemalans then escorted him to the Hotel del Valle on Calle Independencia (Rowton recalls that Dr. Bustamante resided there during his stay in Mexico City several months ago). L/3 told Rowton that he met two other Guatemalans there in the lobby and commented that they talked rather freely in front of him, discussing at one point even the illegal acquisition of a revolver needed by one of them. In the course of conversation L/3 stated that he learned that César Montes is now back in Guatemala and that the FAR is planning an April offensive. All the Guatemalans expressed concern over the increase in the power of the "Mano Blanca" and blamed it for the kidnapping of the Guatemalan Archbishop. They also commented that the military in Guatemala were disenchanted with the "puppet president", Montenegro, and could be expected to increase the terrorism from the right in order to place Montenegro in an impossible position and eventually justify his removal.

6. L/3 commented to Rowton that he noted that at no time did any of the Guatemalans mention Cuban aid or assistance. In fact one of them at one point s-jd that he had tried unsuccessfully to obtain a visa for travel to Cuba; some thirty days previously.
