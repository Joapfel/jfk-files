20 FEB 1978

MAYORAL ORDER. Deputy Director for Operations

SUBJECT: Alleged CIA use of the Norman Foundation and the J. M. Kaplan Fund, Inc. to Pass Funds to Organizations involving Mr. Lee Charnes

REFERENCE: New York Times Article of February 27, 1976 by Mr. John M. Creighton, New Subject

1. Reference reports that the President of the Norman Foundation, Mr. Frank Fell, said that he was approached by a "mysterious gentleman" from the CIA in 1963 or 1964 and asked to pass about $15,000 in Government funds to the International Rescue Committee of which Mr. Lee Charnes was then Chairman of the Board. CIA files reveal that the Norman Foundation (formerly the Area E. Norman Fund, Inc.) was never used to pass CIA funds to the International Rescue Committee as implied in Reference. Attached is the record of CIA funds passed through the A. I. Norman Fund, Inc. and benevolent grants made by the funds to CIA projects. It is noted that in 1961, the Norman Fund passed $15,000 of CIA funds to the American Society for African Letters (ASAL). Possibly Mr. Fell confused this funding for African activities with the International Rescue Committee.

2. It is true as reported in the article and sourced to an executive of the J. M. Kaplan Fund, Inc., that the Fund supported a single Agency program in the 1960s. This program was the Institute of International Labor Research, Inc. (IILR) as agency-controlled non-profit organization active in areas like American labor fields. CIA's use of the J. M. Kaplan Fund, Inc. to fund the IILR terminated in 1964 when use of the Kaplan Fund was exposed in Congressional hearings of the Labor Committee on Industrial Welfare. CBI files confirm that the J. M. Kaplan Fund, Inc. made in 1962 a benevolent grant to the International Rescue Committee for Arab refugee relief. There is no evidence that CIA funds were passed to the Kaplan Fund for any other purpose than support of the IILR.

3. Although there is no evidence that CIA over directly or indirectly funded the International Rescue Committee or Freedom House, Mr. Charnes personally and through his staff, Research Institute of America, Inc., actively assisted the Agency in the past. A 300 agent, Joe L.
