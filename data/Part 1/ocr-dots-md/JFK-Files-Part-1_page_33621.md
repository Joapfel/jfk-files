Convert to MarkdownSince this is an honest letter, and not just pro-forma appreciation by a retiring President, I must also, unfortunately, include some observations that are not so pleasant.

I remember very early in my work with COFE, that you taught me the political meaning of the word "polarisation", and since then I have had ample opportunity in the Hungarian National Council to see polarization at work.

COFE has grown in the past year into a large organisation engaged in serious work, and frequently proving its effective-ness behind the Iron Curtain. To put it another way, during the past year COFE has evolved from just a hope into a professional weapon in the war against the enemy. Therefore, activities which a year ago were deplorable but pardonably, are today inextricable and intolerable.

Admiral Hillar agrees with me completely on this point, and intends to take such action from time to time as may be necessary to cover similar activities when they occur.

However, I recognise that it is not enough for COFE to take unilateral action. We must be assisted by you and those of your associates who understand who the real enemy is, and are willing to subordinate fratricidal strife, polarising attempts, and dialogal activities.

I fully realise that these are nothing but big words unless I can illustrate them more specifically.

I have in my possession enough illustrations to fill a volume, but I would like to select three in particular to mention in this letter, not because I have any particular desire to single out the individuals concerned for censure, but rather because what they have done not only illustrates the point I am trying to make, but also reveals an attitude of mind which is practically incommunicable to an American working for a great cause.

The first case that I have in mind is that of Mr. Purne Hagy.
