The text of the message was translated into English and sent back to the Soviet Embassy. The English translation indicated that the subject of the letter had been discussed in a previous meeting, and that there were no longer any secrets between the two countries.

The contents of the letter are as follows:

**1. Valentin Sergeyevich KOMRUSHIN was seen entering the Embassy at 0919 hours on 4 October in the company of KOMRUSHINA, driving an automobile with license "76 DD." Other than the conversation mentioned in paragraph 4 above, he was not seen again until 1324 hours when he exited the Embassy alone. It is not known when KOMRUSHINA left the Embassy on foot.**

**2. Oleg Sergeyevich KOROLEV was seen entering the Embassy on 4 October 1969 at 0840 hours driving a dual-control car from a local driving school. The car and instructor then departed. KOROLEV entered the main Embassy building and was not seen again until 1349 hours, when he exited the Embassy on foot.**

**3. Valentin Sergeyevich LOGANOV was seen entering the Embassy at 0850 hours the morning of 4 October 1969. He was seen exiting the Embassy in his car, license plates "31 DC," at 0913 hours and was seen entering the Embassy again at 0939 hours in the same vehicle. At 1016 hours he was seen exiting again in car "31 DC" with LOGANOV. The above is brought to your attention because all of these Soviets are known KGB officers (except FEDIN, who is carried as Suspect KGB), and all have at least passable English speaking ability.**

**4. Attached is a photograph of Conrikh Ivanovich KUZNETSOV, who might have been the driver for car "33 DD," in which Subject was snuggled out of the Soviet Embassy on 4 October 1969. Since his wife, Yelizaveta Ivanovna KUZNETSOVA, may have been with him, her photograph is attached. Also attached is a photograph of Yevgeniy Grigorievna EKOLIOVA, wife of Inva Fedorovich MOKHLOV, who might be identical with the woman who not Subject at the gatehouse as he entered the Embassy on 4 October.**

**5. With reference to the emergency instructions for Subject on the last page of Reference B, CAS/MEX reports that they were not briefed concerning this emergency means of communication.**
