(SEVENTH DEPARTMENT - June 1955-January 1960)

Compromise of [WILLERFORD] (1959)

NOSENKO's Information

Frederick WILLERFORD, an American Negro, was [BARRETT'S] roommate during the [1959 exhibition] in Moscow. Initially he was a target of the Moscow City KGB Directorate which believed [WILLERFORD] to be an FBI agent and had been collecting information on him. Unaware of the interest of the other KGB element, VOLKOV and YEFREMOV had made [WILLERFORD]'s acquaintance, and NOSENKO had arranged for compromising photographs to be taken of [WILLERFORD] and YEFREMOV in a Moscow hotel room. NOSENKO watched [WILLERFORD] and YEFREMOV through a two-way mirror and was able to describe the scene in detail. It was only after these compromising photographs had been obtained that NOSENKO learned of the Moscow City KGB Directorate's interest in [WILLERFORD]. NOSENKO's own department and the Moscow City Directorate then made a joint request for operational approval to recruit [WILLERFORD], but [WILLERFORD] was refused because of the ban on recruitment attempts at the time. The case file was retained by the Moscow KGB organization, and NOSENKO did not know whether [WILLERFORD] was approached subsequently.

Comment: [WILLERFORD] was interviewed by the FBI in July 1964 and "advised emphatically that he had never been compromised by anyone nor did he feel that he ever could be compromised by anyone except through the use of drugs, since he felt that he was 'too smart' and most Soviets that he has ever met are aware of his pro-American sentiments". [BARRETT] has reported that [WILLERFORD] had visited VOLKOV in his hotel room and was friendly with him. [BARRETT] said that he had often wondered if [WILLERFORD] was a homosexual because of his manner and bearing.
