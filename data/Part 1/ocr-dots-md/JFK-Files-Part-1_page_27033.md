SECRET

ATL-4-75

14 January 1975

MEMORANDUM FOR RECORD

John R. Peterson
Atlanta Resident Office

Response to Call-in to Agency Headquarters/Initial Contact (Telephoneme)
with John Daniel Williamson, Carrollton, Georgia

1. On 7 January 1975 Albert S. Coleman of the Washington Field Office called on his way to pass on information that he had just been given by Dale Zeilmer, DCN Headquarters, regarding a call received by the Agency duty officer on New Year's Eve from one John Daniel Williamson of Carrollton, Georgia. Williamson identified himself as a Methodist minister and gave his address on Route #2, Box 3546, Carrollton, Georgia, Phone (404) 954-0443.

2. In his call to Agency Headquarters, Williamson said that he had a friend who had information on the impending overthrow of a foreign government. The information regarded illegal shipment of arms from the US to the foreign country. Williamson said that his friend had wanted to try to sell the information to the foreign country concerned but that when the friend told Williamson about it Williamson prevailed on him to let him (Williamson) bring it to the attention of the proper government agency, resulting in his call to our headquarters on New Year's Eve. Williamson asked that someone from the Agency go put in contact with him.

3. On the afternoon of 7 January 1975, after verifying through the phone operator that Williamson was listed in the Carrollton area (no actually lives at Koopville, Georgia, on Eubal Road, but the Koopville phone are handled under the Carrollton exchange), we called his number. We were informed by a young male voice, apparently his son, that Williamson was indeed a Methodist minister, but that he taught school every day during the week in the high school at Franklin, Georgia, except on Saturdays when he worked all day at a hardware store at Rockmart, Georgia. We were told that he preached on Sunday morning. We were not told what he does on Sunday afternoons. Since he was not to be at home until after 5 PM, we called him from our own home that some evening.

4. Williamson vowed to be expecting our call although we had not identified ourselves during the earlier call. He gave us essentially the same information furnished during his New Year's Eve call. He said that the friend about whom he called is a 'former parishioner and lives in the Carrollton area.' He preferred not to provide us with the friend's name and address. The information, however—that which we were able to ascertain—bundled on the up-and-up, as far as Mr. Williamson's part in it was concerned, for he seemed to be a sincere and well intentioned individual. When we tried to at least learn the name of the foreign country involved, Williamson said that his friend would not give him that information.
