Subject: LIVING/4 Report of Status of Request for Re-entry Permit.

1. Rowton met with LIVING/4 at her request at 1630 on 23/3/68 at theenny's Restaurant (Reforma I. Diana) to discuss the status of her visa re-entry) request. She stated that she had just received a letter from her friend Doctor Yolanda Diaz, transmitted through Jorrín and given to her by the latter that same morning. The translation of the text of the letter is as follows:

"Año del Guerrillero Heróico"
La Habana.Febrero 9/68

Sra.
México,D.P.

Dear Mr. - I complained to you last night when we spoke (on the telephone) the processing of your entry is being held up because of a number of things that have to be done which in his view of the priorities you know exist result in its not being done as rapidly as one would wish.

I don't think you should be discouraged and rest assured that as soon as it comes through, we'll advise you, especially since Abreu is going to follow it through. Kisses to your child and regards
to Carlos.

/s/ Yolanda

2. The letter, a copy of which was made by Rowton, also contains a P.S. from Abreu, a translation of which follows:

I'm taking an interest in your matter. As Yolanda explains red tape takes time. I received your cable and letter.

3. LIVING/4 called to Rowton's attention the length of time (almost six weeks) for the letter to reach her despite the fact that Abreu himself most probably forwarded it through the pouch. L/4 commented that when she re-marked this to her friend Catalina Cepero, the latter stated that she had received a letter in the pouch the same day but it was dated in December, 1967, and another from Yolanda dated February 9, 1968.

4. LIVING/4 also reported that she had finally managed to talk with her father on the telephone, and, although he appeared uneasy and somewhat abrupt (seco), she was reassured that he was in reasonably good health. She finally assured Rowton that she no longer suspected him of any foul play and agreed that her father was apparently acting on the advice of his lawyer in connection with the visa request of her brother (to emigrate to the U.S.) that was being processed in New York.

WB Rowton
