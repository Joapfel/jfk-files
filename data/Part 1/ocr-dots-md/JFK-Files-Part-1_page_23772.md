In his early talks with the London Station and again in his talks with BODICAST and TIFFANY the Agent has indicated some degree of disentanglement with the organizations and personalities, if not the ideology, of the left-wing factions with which he had been so intimately involved. He has never gone so far as to renounce his birthright and citizenship, nor does he consider himself disloyal after several years of professional anti-PEPARDE activity. He intimates that he would someday like to return home, though he doubts he would be allowed to live down his past and start a new career in PEPADE.

19. On the basis of information available to us we could not say that S/VI is or ever was an ideologically committed Marxist-Leninist. He might say that he has matured over the years a moreering prejudice against the "in" group, both domestically and internationally. He has been an "out" most all of his life, and he has shown a strong attachment to the causes of the downtrodden Cubans, the colonially oppressed Africans, the organizationally subdued "young Marxists" of orthodox communist and other leftist movements, etc. There is no question that in his sympathy for such causes he overstepped the bounds of reason and good sense, but it is doubtful that he actually committed any reasonable offense. One has the feeling that S/I really sought to hedge his bets on the revolutionaries by putting out feelers to KUHAK.

20. There is a strong case for the argument that hard cash plays a significant role in motivating S/I to cooperate with KUHAK at this point in time. All reports emphasize that he was not just broke, but deeply in debt, when he began talking to London Station. Subsequently he has been receiving what amounts to a salary at a rate somewhat in excess of average for a European journalist, let alone a professional small-time revolutionary. Since he has had no valid cover story for anything other than the first advance(s) from COPERMA, we cannot be sure that he has let his wife, his in-laws, or his immediate circle of associates know the extent of his newly-acquired affluence. He is quite capable of thinking big operationally and has blustily stated that it takes money to "move" in Algiers and that "poverty is no virtue in the 'Third World'".

21. But S/I does not appear to be an out and out mercenary. There must be other factors than money alone that would lead him to cooperate with KUHAK. It could well be that he is reacting philosophically to an outlook on life that let his doain despite years of devoted cultivation. In the long debriefings he rarely, if ever, mentioned anyone who he felt had really accomplished anything as a professional left-wing revolutionary. There are indications that he regards his activities over recent years as somewhat un rewarding, though there is little indication that he didn't enjoy much of what he did or that he doesn't consider the cause of the national liberation movements a just and honorable cause. He may, simply, have reappraised the results of some years of revolutionary activity with a somewhat jaundiced eye and decided it was time for a change.

22. Certainly S/I builds a convincing case about the reasons for his break with LIEH C, but it must also be noted that ideology is not enough then. If anything, it would seem he was reacting to a breach of contract and perhaps wounded pride. The net result seemed to be a well of resentments against one individual, which resentment fermented into spite and determination to "get even." If this assumption is granted, then the letter he had published in ZEARTH and the manuscript he submitted to ENEMESIS are both explainable and explain such. It was his involvement with these publications that triggered HUHAK's operational interest in his, and we initiated contact at a period when he was, perhaps, backing in (what he probably considered) the success of his attack on LIEH A.
