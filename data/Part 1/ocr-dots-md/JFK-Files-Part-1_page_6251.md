CHRISTMAS
MESSAGE

As another year draws to its end, we find before a world new in many aspects but sadly stagnant if not actually retrogressive in some of life's most vital facets. Humanity is a year older but is it not still engaged in a relentless search for the true meaning of its being?

December is a thoughtful month in the lives of men. It is the hour of retrospect, of questioning, of reorganization and projection into the future. Above all, December is a yearly reminder of an idea and a way of life for men. A new and revolutionary order born twenty centuries ago, comes vigourously to the forefront for in the long months of toil we have not forgotten the cause and the goal of our existence.

No time is perhaps more adequate to ask this question: Have men achieved for the world a lasting peace? Each year the same unconditional, negative answer meets the question. Not only does mankind live far removed from peace but each new day embarks upon roads that can only lead farther and farther away from it into world of national and international wars.

Two causes exist in the reality of our world, one in the moral order, the other in the realm of social and political entities. Each complements the other and both are necessary to the attainment of stability in the society in which man must live.

Men would have peace in today's world but do nothing to preserve the forces of internal peace in each man. Man, the family, society: the elements capable of achieving for the world a real and positive peace are totally disunited and into each has been sown the seed of distrust and of fear.

There has been much jiggling of doctrines and ideologies, instead of being a source of confidence and strength

by which men may rule their lives, have served only as a source of fear and hatred among nations and races and even amongst men in the same community.

Morally, our world has chosen to forget the command: "Love one another as I have loved you".

Socially, convenience and self-interest have made this world blind itself to the fact that, "Peace can only be the consequent to the establishment in each Nation and in the world of regimes or systems of government based on justice and the freedom of the individual."

Today world is not a very adequate breeding ground for peace. Colonialism, imperialism, rightist and leftist totalitarianism thrive throughout the world stifling in man his longing for liberty and equity.

We as young men and as Cubans have a duty and right to our land and to the world. Our land, we firmly believe, will know peace only when we have returned to our brothers the right to think and to speak freely, abolishing the rule of terror and injustice that today shackles our people.

The world faces much its same task but on a vaster scale. Each man must be given the freedom of choice and the justice which are his right.

To promise and to speak of peace without the noble purpose and the firm decision of fighting tirelessly for its attainment is a cowardly crime.

Peace, as all worthwhile things, has a high price and its achievement entails a struggle. Those who would have peace must willingly take up the battle against those forces at work in our world which debianize man and make him a prey of fear. No season is as appropriate as Christmas to reaffirm in each of us the determination to carry on this struggle until true peace has been given to man.
