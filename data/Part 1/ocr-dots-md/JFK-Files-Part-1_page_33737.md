REPLY: Mrs. Mary Augustino represented the New York office at the meeting in the absence of Mr. John F. Leich, who is in Europe attending the European Negotiation Conference.

Czechoslovakia:

It was rescheduled that at the meeting on December 26, 1951, discussion of the Czechoslovak problem had ended on a note of optimism. Miss Augustine advised that this optimism had been obliterated due to certain unfortunate circumstances which had developed in negotiations between the Czechoslovak Council majority of 17 and the Slovak minority of 13. Miss Augustine indicated that one of the principal stumbling blocks in the Czech situation is the question of the co-equal chairmanship in the new setup of two persons, one appointed from each group. She further advised that a body of 20 persons divided equally between the two operating groups would not as an end have contributed to establishing a new Czechoslovak National Council, and that, whereas the US wished to appoint Mr. Šenk as one of the two chairman, they had at the same time expressed their opinion that Dr. Cowky would not be acceptable to them as the other co-chairman, or indeed, that he should not exercise any major function. Such an ultimatum appeared unacceptable to the 17 who consented that negotiations could not be carried on under these circumstances.
