11. Mr. F. W. Sumner, in his address to the members of the House of Representatives of Hawaii, November 8, 1925, says:
> The great bulk of our people are laborers. They work for the benefit of their employers, who are the employers of the employers.
> 
> As a rule, the average laborer gets 70 cents a day. This is enough to feed a family of four persons, if they eat three meals a day. But it would take a family of five to get through one day with food and water.
> 
> This is true also of the laborers on the railroads. If you go to the railroads at night, when the men are allleep, you will find them sleeping by the side of the track. If you look down the train as it goes along, you will see that it has already passed you. It does not stop to let you in, however, and if you try to stop it, it will break up your train. You can not even stop it if you do stop it. It breaks up the engine and runs away, and you have to wait until it comes back again.
