USTRANS REPORT #222, I lps

9 Jan 63

Subj: Americans/ Mare Schleifer/Richard Gibson/Afrique Revolution

PADA: Home of Jean Marie Boeglin (Jeanson Network), 7 January 63

1. Boeglin scheduled an informal discussion between himself, Schleifer, an idle leader from the Union of South Africa, and Yves Mathieu (phonetic) at the Boeglin/Mathieu apartment. I was invited to participate, and to translate for Schleifer who speaks no French. The South African didn't appear, but we were joined later in the evening by a French lawyer Maurice Lim who works in Eline Vacante and by François Lim, another Jeanson network character.

2. Boeglin has accepted me as being very leftist, "objective", and perhaps even communist—an impression I have matured with him. Thus, his acceptance of us as a like-minded person gave confidence to the others.

3. The evening was a discussion of the Cuban and Algerian revolutions. Schleifer was more or less the oracle, having been in Cuba twice, once for two months Dec 60/Jan 61 and recently for nine months. Personally, I felt Schleifer is so extremely left that he is a communist in any thing except politics and the party membership. He is intelligent, reasonable well, and is obviously well-read, being able to give quotes from books by title of the principal communist authors, especially Stalin. He is also funny in that he seems to accept U.S. positions as being propaganda positions and does not assess the true ones for Russian positions. At the same time, he gave us an impression of sincerity, quite a bit of criticism for Russia, and an obvious search for the truth. He didn't blink an eye when Mathieu assumed that we were all"Communist, Socialist, and hoped for the eventual implementation of socialism even in the U.S." If he is not a socialist, he is an extreme left fellow traveler.

4. The principal conclusions of the evening were that the Algerian revolution has proceeded along the path of socialiste much faster than the Cuban revolution did, but that the Cuban revolution will reach true socialisme before the Algerian revolution because of the limiting factor of the Edouard Screen, the Cuban army was the spearhead of the revolutionary reforms in Cuba, but the Algerian army has not been due to the political crisis, a defect which is being corrected at the moment as plans are now being made for the Algerian army to assume the same position here, Russia and the U.S. after the Cuban breach have implicitly or explicitly made an agreement to keep out of Algeria in particular and Africa in general in favor of France and other European dominions in order to keep from having a further stand-off as in Cuba; Russia is not ready for nor does it want further revolutions in Latin America or Africa—as opposed to Asia—since it is not ready to back such revolutions in the face of American or European opposition to the limit, and thus she can only lose face and the position of monopoly of socialist leadership which she wishes to retain. Schleifer made some interesting statements that Morgan had been killed by Castro because he took funds from the CIA to finance a counter-revolution.

I caused a flurry of dropped files during the evening, especially Mathieu—which was later forgiven because of my tolerance—objectivity—than I described the lower middle-class farm of my family with its 80 hectares, tractors, combine, etc., and declared communism couldn't work in such a wealthy atmosphere. In this way I agreed that perhaps in the U.S. no more effective education and distribution of land could be envisaged.
