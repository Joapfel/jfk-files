It is probable that the FSP, as a political party, will no longer carry the weight and res- pect it has in the past, at least until economic conditions in Cuba grow much worse, or until the present East–West tension diminishes considerably. Until recently, two possible solutions to the FSP’s present predicament lay in its uniting with the Ortodoxo Party or with the PAU. Concerning the Ortodoxo Party, it is known that the leftist groups within that party (Vice-Secretary AMEZ, FAU RADA, N.A.I.R., and others) are militating for a pact with the communists (see copy of recommendation from E. T. CRAIG to the Ambassador, dated January 23, 1952, copy of which is attached), but, so far, have been checked by the anti- communist elements within the Ortodoxo Party. Until recently, conservative anti-BATISTA elec- tives in Cuba stated with conviction that BATISTA would join with the Communists in a selfish attempt to gain the Presidency. However, BATISTA, in two articles appearing in Cibagi, leader 2 and 13, 1951, closed the door on more of this kind by stating the only way the FSP and the PAU could unite would be if the FSP would publicly state that, in case of an international conflict, the FSP would side with the United Nations and against Russia.
