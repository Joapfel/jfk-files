SUBJECT: LIPEAT Project Renewal

1. It is requested that the LIPEAT Project be renewed for the period 1 July 1960 through 30 June 1961 for a total of $98,901.00. Last fiscal year the project was approved for $123,830 of which $73,884 was obligated. The approved programmed figure was $90,000 for Fiscal Year 1961. The extra $9,000 required would come from the following programmed projects:

*   LIMIT - $2,000
*   LIVERY - $1,000
*   LIFEBY - $6,000

2. Subsequent to the preparation by the Station of the attached Project review and renewal request for LIFET, the following situation developed in Mexico which has directly affected the entire LIPEAT Project. At a meeting with the Chief of station on 25 June 1960, LIFETER asked the station and LIPEAT to immediately begin covering the Soviet, Polish and Czech Embassy under LIVERY. LIVERY suggested that the Mexicans transfer the Spanish and that the station forward the tapes to Washington for translation of the Russian, Polish and Czech portions. The Chief of Station had no alternative but to express great interest in the coverage of targets of such importance. In order to protect the security of the LIPEAT operations, the station stopped the LINEICK and LIMER (LIVERY had not been in operation for the past six months for security reasons) coverage on 22 June, prior to the LIVERY activation. It was determined that LIVERY would cover 5 Soviet and 2 Czech lines. The task continues to be processed by LIFET TER and LIPEAT. The LIVERY monitors extract from the Spanish only the most important points for the daily reports which are passed to LIVER Y. LIPEAT has been told we will inform him of pertinent information obtained (ostensibly from headquarters processing) of the Soviet and Czech text for passing to LIVERY. LIPEAT will continue its unilateral coverage of Polish and other targets of interest.

3. The Station does not anticipate any problems connected with the exploitation of leads since LIVERY has evinced no interest in this matter. In view of the lack of language capabilities by the Mexicans and their ability to control what is passed (ostensibly from Headquarters), the Station sees no problems arising from the present arrangement.
