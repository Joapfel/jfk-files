Dear Sirs,

This is to acknowledge with thanks receipt of
your informative letter of April 29, 1953 which arrived
by steamer mail. We regret that an earlier reply has
not been possible, but the time elapsed has been well
spent in giving your latest report and your proposal
of March 5, 1953, the serious consideration which
they deserve. We might suggest that your future corre-
spondence be sent to us by airmail.

The executive board of the Foundation has studied
your report of April 29, 1953 and appreciated your
comprehensive response to our request for further in-
formation. The objectives and contemplated activities
of your group certainly seem to be highly consistent
with the general aim of the Foundation and as such
would merit careful consideration of financial assis-
tance so you have indicated would be necessary.

As above indicated, your proposal of March 5,
and report of April 29, have not with the synthetic

are the indications of strong local support in the

poration subscription, plus an almost equal amount in
subsequent donations for the ("P.F.C.") organization.
However, we would like to know to what extent the
1947 figures, which you cited in your letter of April
29, might serve as a realistic basis for a 1953
estimate of support which might be obtained locally.

If, in the light of information at hand and
any additional information which you may furnish on
the personnel, resources, and needs of your group,
your proposal should prove to constitute the feasible
and desirable activity which it now appears to us,
we will endeavor to lend our support to you in every
way possible.

The foundation will not be in a position to
frame a definitive answer to your proposal until we
are in receipt of, and have reviewed, your promised
estimate of printing costs for ("P.C.").
