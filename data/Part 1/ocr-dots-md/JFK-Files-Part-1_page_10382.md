16. At the close of 1950, Subject left home to attend the Mexico City University. She was committed to a school that its boarders were not permitted to work and that his mother and sister were. However, it is also likely that both left for the purpose of further education. Upon her return, she filed with the local authorities in Mexico a petition, seeking an order from the Mexican courts to recover the money she had lost. The court did not recover any of it.

17. Throughout the following year, Subject continued her studies at the Normal School in Mexico City and entered the first class of the Faculty of Arts and Letters, University of Mexico. During this time, as part of her further training in teaching, she entered the Normal School. In 1952, after having completed two years of study, Subject transferred herself to the faculty of pedagogical morphology, and began teaching there. During this period, Subject simultaneously developed a strong interest in painting and sculpture and over the next five or so years, studied under such masters as Rivera and Siquieros.

18. Subject was married in 1930 at the age of twenty years to a young Mexican woman from Leon, GTC, and during the next ten years used at least four children by her. He divorced his wife in about 1940, just prior to his departure for Cuba, but he had not been on good terms with her for several years prior to the divorce. From about 1954 to 1960, Subject worked in the Mexican Government Social Service among the Indians in the southern states of Mexico. Subject attributes some of the tension that arose between him and his wife to this employment venture. He documented to Howton that his first wife wanted to live a quiet normal life in Mexico City and was unwilling to share with him the harshness of social work in remote areas of tropical Mexico. During this period Subject actually developed his own style in painting and began to establish close contacts with Mexican art circles.

19. Subject went to Cuba in about 1960 "to join the revolution", apparently as a result of his increasingly leftist political sympathies. He did a relatively large amount of painting and mural work during his four-year residence in Cuba, while at the same time working in the Ministry of Labor and Education. In about 1961 he married a Cuban veteran of Chinese origin from Santisima de Duba and the following year a (girl) child was born to them in Havana. Subject has told Howton that he has had very little contact with his first wife since his divorce and now no alimony. It added that his family, however, has continued to take care of him in his first wife until the children of his first marriage and has agreed to support them financially. During his stay in Cuba, Subject developed close fraternities with many Cuban revolutionaries, particularly Anrol Nochiez, whose wife worked with Subject in the Ministry of Education at the time when Anrol Redrigo was serving as Minister of Labor.

20. Subject returned to Mexico City in 1964 with his new wife and child (now he had naturalized as a Mexican citizen). At this time Subject had become a professional artist but supplemented the rather modest income he acquired from the sale of his painting by teaching art privately and in Mexican public schools. His monthly income during the early 1960s did not exceed 3,000 pesos.
