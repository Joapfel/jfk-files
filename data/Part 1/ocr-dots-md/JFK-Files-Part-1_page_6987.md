1931. Already a Harvard graduate and communist, he became a professor at Oklahoma University. (CC files)

1935. He made a trip to Cuba, where he and other left-wingers were arrested and expelled. During this period he continued to direct communist activities in Oklahoma and made arrangements for him to visit Mexico any trips to Mexico to confer with Mexican communist leaders. (CO files)

1940. He cashed a check drawn on the Bank of Foreign Trade, Moscow. This was not discovered until 1941; see next item.] (CO files)

1941. Congressional investigation of known subversive activities in Oklahoma uncovered the check-cashing, his 1935 visit to Cuba, association with leftist individuals, articles to left-wing publications, membership in left-wing groups like Civil Liberties Committee, etc. (SRS, memo from Herman Horton to Bureau of Security and Consular Affairs, 10 August 1940)

8 September 1941. Dismissed from Oklahoma U. while on sabbatical. Reinstated 11 Feb 1942 but with understanding to resign at termination of sabbatical. (SRS, Herman Horton memo)

1942. Late in the year, Elizabeth Bentley was introduced to him by Jacob Colos, according to her 1943 testimony. (CI/SRC, CO, SRS)

1943. As of this time he worked for OSS in Washington D.C., despite his known communist activities and sympathies. He was (or became before the end of the war) head of the OSS Latin American Division. (Washington Post, 1 Sept 1946, attached). According to Elizabeth Bentley's testimony and book ("Out of Bondage"), he supplied Colos and the communist secretariat with information from his OSS office. (SIG,SRS)

1948. Elizabeth Bentley testified that she had met him in late 1942 through Jacob Colos (known Soviet agent) and that while Halperin was employed by the OSS in wartime Washington he gave information to Colos. (SIG)

Autumn 1949. He began teaching at Boston University. (SRS, Horton memo).

Late 1952 or early 1953. By this time he was director of Latin American Regional Studies at Boston University. He was summoned to testify before the Jenner Committee in Boston, since Bentley had named him as one of her sources. He refused to say whether he was a communist. (CC files)

Later in 1953. He was suspended by certain U.S. authorities in disagreement with an impartial U.S. committee which had decided not to fire him for the time being. (CC files)

28 November 1953. The Malperins took off for Mexico. (SRI, Horton memo)
