How, why, and to what extent can Fidelismo affect the Alliance para el Progresso? Some observers feel that Castro is indeed the real (if not de jure) father of the Alliance; that without his cataclysmic emergence, the United States would have continued to ignore the poor-coutry republics of the south. (In the 1950's only 3% of our foreign aid went to the 21 nations which comprise Latin America. Laos alone received more aid than all of these countries combined.) But, more importantly, the Cuban premier has aroused the expectations of the heretofore sullen masses throughout the hemisphere, and their respective leaders have become acutely aware of the rumbling discontent. Some of these politicians would no doubt welcome a sudden disposal of the Castro anarchy, but none of them would have participated in a move to openly chastise Cuba at the Punta del Este conference. As a matter of fact, "Che" Guevara managed to accomplish a few minor triumphs despite the cool reception he got from most of the delegates. His post-conference meetings with President Frondal of Argentina and ex-President Quadros of Brazil are not to be taken lightly.

Although the United States managed to obtain all of the desired signatures on the final declaration at Uruguay, it would be naive for us to suppose that we can once again count on the heretofore solid backing of Latin America.
