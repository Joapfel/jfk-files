Schirra Ponders Settling in Perth

PERTH, Australia (AP)— American astronaut Walter M. Schirra Jr. says he is seriously considering settling in Perth when he is through with outer space.

Navy Capt. Schirra and Air Force Col. Frank Borman orbited around the capital of western Australia today, meeting political and civic leaders and other residents of the "City of Lights."

Perth is the site of a tracking station for U.S. space flights and puts on its lights when American astronauts fly over the area at night. Schirra during his six-orbit flight Oct. 3, 1962, radioed the station that he had spotted the lights during his second orbit.

Death Asked for Three

At the opening of the trial in La Cabana prison fortress, prosecutor Jorge Segura asked the death penalty for Cubelas, former Maj. Ramon Guin, Jose Gonzalez Gallaretta and Alberto Blanco Romariz.

He asked 30-year prison terms for Juan Alsina Navarro, Guillermo Cunil Alvarez and Angel Herrero Veliz.

Guin is charged with knowing about the plot, concealing it and spying for the CIA.

Cubelas testified that Artime persuaded him to join the plot during a meeting in Madrid on Christmas Day, 1964. He said that in February, 1965, Artime gave him a high-powered telescope and a silencer for his rifle, and he agreed to shoot Castro from a distance of about 800 yards.

'In Hands of Enemy'

"This was a cowardly act," he said. "I fell in the hands of the enemy."

Cubelas said the meeting was arranged by a Cuban resident of Madrid and by Odon Alvarez de la Campa, a former counselor to the Cuban embassy in the Spanish capital who defected in March, 1965.

About 500 persons, including Cuban and foreign newsmen, attended the session of the trial.
