The Soviet Union, after the successful liquidation of the Western Front, will turn its eyes to Latin America, where it can find new opportunities for exploitation. The American people, who have suffered so much under the Soviet yoke, are likely to welcome the opportunity to throw off Soviet control. As the Soviet Union gains strength in South America and Central America, its reach will extend farther into the Western Hemisphere, and it will become more and more difficult for the United States to compete in the race for markets and investments. At the same time, the Soviet Union will seek to increase its influence in South America byímming links with the Western powers and by promoting brotherly ties between the Soviet Union and the various Latin American countries.

In this connection, the United States should remember that the success of the Soviet drive through Latin America depends not only upon the ability of the Soviet Union to gain access to Latin America through such means as military intervention and economic pressure, but also upon the ability of the United States to resist the efforts of the Soviet Union to secure entrance into the Western Hemisphere through the countries which have already joined the Western World. It is important for the United States to bear in mind that the security of the Western world is closely interrelated with the security of the Latin American countries, and that the interest of the United States in these countries must first of all be considered before any other policy can be planned or carried out.
