# Chapter VI

1. In this chapter Hamilton levels some criticism at Ambassador Bonsai, whom he describes as inane and whom he calls "fumbling Phil" (pp. 94-95)

2. Hamilton describes Jim Noel's arrival at the Miami Base, following the severing of diplomatic relations with Cuba, as "unwelcome", because he felt him to be "basically either soft on Communism or at least terribly confused about the issues". (p. 96). Hamilton offers his criticism of Manuel (Manoel) Ray because the latter had stayed with Castro too long and was now sounding a theme of "the revolution betrayed" which was finding favor at the White House, State and CIA.
