ANWORLD Meeting in San Juan, P. R., 2-5 May 1964

SUBJECT: The Nature of the American Commitment

1. Alluding to certain charges levelled against CIA in a recent publication on the Bay of Pigs affair, whose substance was that the Cuban military leadership had been served with erroneous intel estimates by their CIA advisors. I voiced my hope that the U. S. team servicing AMWORLD would never be subjected to similar censure (without implying justification for charges made in the instance of the Bay of Pigs). I reiterated that it was our strong desire to be absolutely forthright with our Cuban counterparts, especially in stressing that our target intelligence had by its very nature to be of limited usefulness, especially when it came to predicting the extent and nature of Cuban defense measures. As an example, certain radar sites had struck us as highly vulnerable to external raids, but that this estimate might not necessarily be borne out if put to the test of an actual operation.

2. In the general content of pacts fulfilled, I once more pointed out to AMTUL-19 that the American commitment to AMWORLD was a tenacuse one and that I would never be able to give an estimate of U. S. Government intentions vis-a-vis AMWORLD other than what can be deduced from the events of the preceding day. An an example, official American reaction to the widening repercussions of a limited AMWORLD success in lifting Cuba was simply unpredictable. AMTUL-19 hastened to assure us that AMBIDAY-1 was indeed fully cognizant of the circumstances I had outlined and that this sense of urgency in embarking operations was partly a reflection of a sense of insecurity engendered by the official American posture in respect to Cuba.

Henry D. Neckaher
