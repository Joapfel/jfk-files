SUBJECT: Cover Review for Harold R. NORMAN

1. The leading factor that has been responsible for the maintenance of a suitable cover arrangement for NORMAN has been the flexibility and adaptability of NORMAN himself. Without this person's fundamental ability to mold his cover around his personal qualifications, the notional cover story would not have been adequate in Mexico. However, to date, this cover has provided the added backstopping needed to permit NORMAN to give a convincing appearance of being gainfully employed and with no apparent inference to OTOKE connections.

2. When NORMAN originally was assigned to Mexico City, he stopped in Dallas, Texas en route here in order to visit the cover address of his supposed cover concern so that he could be aware first hand of the details surrounding this company. In November-December 1958 he became acquainted with several Texans who were considering business opportunities in Mexico and - although they were not from Dallas - was able to successfully convince him of the legitimacy of his business. In this case, as is usual with his general cover story, he also showed them published articles in Bohemia and Boys Life, written under his true name, to further illustrate his varied means of financial support.

3. As stated above, the notional cover firm alone would probably not satisfy NORMAN's cover requirements, but when combined with his actual writing activities, it furnishes a fairly well-rounded background story. Thus emerges the basic necessity for this type of cover: some large grains of truth mixed tastefully with the nebulous aspects of a notional story.

4. The exact manner by which NORMAN achieves this impression is as follows:

a. He mentions that his cover firm has sent him into Mexico to investigate the prospects of establishing a Mexican affiliate. NORMAN's absence from Mexico (once in July 1958 for consultation in Washington and again in January 1959 on TOY to Cuba) are thus explained as added surveys of this nature outside of Mexico - thus providing the pattern of flexibility in movement.

b. To implement his income, NORMAN has taken up free-lance writing and can prove his accomplishments by exhibiting his published articles. His home has office space that is surrounded by popular literature, manuscripts and other aids that a writer would normally possess.

c. To further cover for his source of income, NORMAN has let it be known that he owns property in Costa Rica and in Cuba (places where he was previously assigned - the Costa Rican property is actual) and that rentals provide him with monthly income.

d. NORMAN does not attempt, however, to mix with the American communist in Mexico, but prefers to lend the general impression that he wishes to get "down to grass roots" in dealing with the native environment. His residence is situated in a middle to upper class neighborhood largely settled by Mexican families. He then creates the impression of a slightly "bohemian American writer who has adopted his life during long experience in Latin America, to local conditions."
