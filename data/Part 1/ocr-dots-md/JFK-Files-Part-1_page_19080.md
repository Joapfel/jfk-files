5. -- It was said before, and has been again said, that the right of the slave to go to the country of his choice, and of the master to take him there, is secured by the Constitution. But the Constitution does not say, as it does in the Bill of Rights, that the right of the master to take his slave to some other state is an absolute one. On the contrary, it says, that the right is subject to such limitations, and to such conditions, as the master may have fixed. These conditions are, that the slave shall not escape, and that he shall receive the same kind of treatment which he received in the state in which he then resided. Now, this is exactly what the master means when he takes his slave from him in a different state, and carries him off to some other state, where he may be sure of his own safety. He knows that, so long as he has the slave in his hands, he may carry him away as he pleases. But he knows also that, as soon as the slave becomes free, he will run away, and take his master into another state. It is for this reason, that the master is allowed the right of taking his slave to some other state, and of keeping him there, so long as he remains in the hands of the master. This is precisely the right which the master has in the first place, and which, in the second place, he has in return, to take his slave from him in a different state, and to keep him there, as long as he remains in the hands of the master.

In conclusion, we may say, that the right of the master to take his slave from him, is not an absolute one. He may take him away, provided he leaves him free to return, and to take him back in the same state in which he left it. It is only when he is not found in the state to which he was taken, and when it becomes known that he is still in the state in which he was taken, that the master has the right to bring him back to his owner. But in that case, he may, if he likes, force the slave home again, and take him back, provided he goes in the same state in which he was taken.
