MEMORANDUM FOR THE RECORD

SUBJECT: Edward G. Tichborn (P) - hernia

1. On 1 February Edward G. Tichborn advised me that in lifting his bag out of a taxi on his return trip from Cuts, he suddenly felt a pain in the Groin and was afraid that he had a hernia.

2. On the way to the airport he asked me if there would be any chance of the Government picking up his medical expenses in the event that he actually had a hernia which required any substantial treatment. I told him so that time that I did not know what the Government's position would be, but that I would check into the matter and let him know.

3. On 2 February, I contacted Mr. Vernon Turner, SCA/DEC, who explained the circumstances to him. At that time, I also pointed out that as far as the provisions of his contract were concerned, it was stated that he was not an employee of the U. S. Government and while it did not make any specific reference to medical payments while he was working for us, it did not necessarily preclude such payments.
Mr. Turner agreed that although the contract states he was not an employee of the U. S. Government, in actual fact during the period he was doing our business, he was paid on further to explain to Mr. Turner that in all probability we will call upon Mr. Turner to do future services for us until that time when he was being paid; it was doubtful that this arrangement can be square with what he would take in his regular law practice. Mr. Turner stated that there were a number of ways that we could alleviate his situation should it become necessary.

My understanding is that I said the facts of a case - not the words in a paper - would probably be the basis for determination of employee status. If we are in doubt to pay in this case a contribution payment - not necessarily related to medical expenses - would probably be the solution. V.T.
