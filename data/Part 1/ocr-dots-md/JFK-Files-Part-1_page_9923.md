11 August, 1957

Subject: LURING/3 Meeting with Mercader Pereira.

1. At his meeting with Rorten at 1100 on 14 August, LURING/3 reported that he had learned from Ernesto Tappiano that she was Mercedes Pereira, his Spanish revolutionary friend, was living at Diagonal San Antonio 4, in apartment 6. ( According to 1/3, his interest in Pereira was revived by a comment made to him by hoises Cronco Leal on 31 July, 1947, to the effect that Mercedes Pereira was in Mexico).

2. LURING/3 continued that upon learning her address he called on Mrs. Pereira unannounced at 1030 on 13 August with LURING/4 and fortunately found her in with her 17-year-old son, Arrando. He found the apartment almost completely unfurnished, except for a table and several chairs and a nucleus of bedroom furniture. According to 1/3, Mrs. Pereira explained that she and her son had just returned to Mexico and were organizing their household on their very limited resources. She said that her son had found work in a factory and that she hoped to locate a job as soon as her Mexican immigrant papers came through.

3. She and her son welcomed LURING/3 and /P after so many years, but Mrs. Pereira confessed later in the conversation that she was expecting 1/3 to visit her since she had already received word from Ernesto Tappiano. She then recounted to 1/3 and 1/P the vicissitudes and turmoil of her life since she had last seen them in Havana (in 1964). She stated that her very confused citizenship status had continued to plague her in Havana and, when she finally decided to leave in order to settle the inheritance left by her/husband in Spain, she had great difficulty in leaving Cuba. She explained that many obstacles were put in her way, despite all the work she had done for the revolution, and she was forced to leave in effect as a "gusanita". She said that her mother and sister, Sofia, were still in Havana, and added that her sister is still working at the Czech Embassy. She also stated parenthetically that her sister, Sofia, was hoping to be transferred to the Czech Embassy in Mexico City in the near future, where she had worked prior to going to Havana.

4. Mrs. Pereira stated that she was greatly disappointed with the Cubans and felt that the Tricontinental had done more harm than good by trampling its intention of helping all Latin American revolutions. She commented that the situation in Guatemala, which she had recently visited as a tourist with Spanish documents, was very bad for the revolutionaries.
