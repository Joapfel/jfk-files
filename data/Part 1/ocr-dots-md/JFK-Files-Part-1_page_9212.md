Dear Bill:

Good to hear from you, even though your letter,
as usual, too brief for my appetite. From "our mutual
friend 'Billia'" you know more about my activities than
I do about yours.

My collaborateur is working like a friend, with:
the assistance of a sub-friend (female) who darts up pictures
by the hundreds (literally). Last night I worked with him
until 2 o'clock in the morning (with a brief intermission
for a trize fight on his television set). In that long
stretch we went through over 400 pictures which he had just
combined out of various lagoons in us. Out of this batch we
discarded about one-third, tossed away a couple of dozen
other substitutes for the real thing which the new haul
corrected, and filled the present 40-odd chapter folders
(provisional) with the cream of the crop.

As you will gather from the above paragraph, the illustrated book on the man and his international network --and the forces all over the world arrayed on his side and against him-- ain't no ordinary picture album. It will be, I am quite certain, a major work. There is little point any attempting to describe it in a letter, but I am quite confident that it will be read widely--and even more important--looked at, with absorbed interest this year, next and ten years from now. That is more important, it will make convincing, I am certain, what no words, written or spoken, can ever make completely convincing.

As far as the "date when it will be wrapped up"--
I can only say that I am acting the role of the slave-driver
and the speedup man right now. It is, however, a delicate role,
which could have undesirable results if overcome.
But both my main collaborator, who is certainly one of
the best men in the country in the field--and the best man for
this particular book, is giving it every free moment, as is
his fantastic research as a instant. But a good picture book--as
I told you immediately when we discussed the idea originally,
is as much a job as any good book. I think we have broken the
back of the job, and I am confident that the product will excel
your fondest dreams. As far as I can estimate the date, I think I'll
be in a better position to do that in about three weeks, when
the haul will be substantially in, trax and when we will have
finished down our present mountainous collection to the essence.
I should imagine that when our friend the engineer returns
from his present task, our collaborator and I will be able to
give him a fair chance. At that point we'll have to settle
with him to do or ask other technical questions in connection with the
actual printing job. The largest single question we see is this:
Price of box versus gravity and lastin: value.
