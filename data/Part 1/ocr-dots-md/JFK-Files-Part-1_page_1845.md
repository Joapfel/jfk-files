## Chapter VIII

1. This chapter describes more conversations regarding internal problems within the Tropes, one of whose members is trying to bring in Manuel Ray (p. 149-153).

2. Hamilton then tells of a meeting with Bissell who said that he was under White House pressure to broaden the political base of the Tropes. Evidently former Venezuelan President Betancourt, former Costa Rican President Figureas and Puerto Rican Governor Munoz Marín were insisting on the inclusion of Muscle Ray (p. 146). Schlesinger and Goodrich were also backing Ray. Hamilton mentioned that the Cubans did not like Ray because of the lateness with which he broke from Castro (p. 147). Bissell noted that the Joint Chiefs of Staff wished to send an evaluation team to the training camps to appraise the state of readiness (p. 148).

3. Tracy Barnes takes Hamilton to see Schlesinger at the White House where the letter is working on his famous White Paper on Cuba (p. 151). It was there that Hamilton learned that Secretary of State Rank had veto'd the original point of invasion as too "noisy." Ambassador Stevenson joins the meeting and asks Barnes if everything was going well. Hamilton interprets this remark as indicating that the Ambassador knew about the invasion plans, which the letter had subsequently denied. Hamilton also mentions the fact that Barnes had kept a memorandum of his briefing of Stevenson before the invasion (pp. 152-153).
