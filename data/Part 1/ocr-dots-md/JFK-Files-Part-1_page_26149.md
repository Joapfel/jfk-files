Convert to Markdown"The surprising thing is that nobody has protested the insult to Mexico has been passed over in absolute silence. Worse things will obviously come. No sooner does he arrive in Mexico than Mr. Freeman arranges the right to shout anything he pleases, because Washington has become i.p.a.r.t. with Mexico's independent policy, a.c. the Texan style of the Johnson-Mann team: (isn't) alien to the relative tolerance of the Kennedy 'intellectuals.' Free-a comes to exert pressure violently and announces at the start that he is ready, for anything. It is the policy of international gangsterism that requires the conduct of a bull; in those who pursue it."

Further on, the writer says: "Either these saloon ruffians are stopped short or nobody will be able to stand them. That is, if, it is serious that nobody has raised his voice to teach Freeman some manners, and that, on the contrary, General Olachua, secretary of defense, should have paid him honors with all his general staff without daring to point out to him that this is an independent country, and that proconsuls and viceroys are not allowed here.

"Every U.S. ambassador who comes to Mexico makes his predecessor look good by comparison, but at least in the beginning all try to make themselves liked. But not Fulton Freeman. He comes with sullion looks, raising his voice so nobody can be mistaken. He shows his hand right at the start and announces that he is not going to try to convince anybody but will give orders. This indicates quite clearly that the State Department thinks it has been the patient and people circumstances are ripe for acting tough and violently."
