The third case I would like to mention concerns Mr.
Zolten Pfiffer.

After Mr. Lesserffy made his selections for the New York desk, Mr. Pfeiffer got in touch with him to protest against the fact that in selecting Mr. Gellert, Mr. Lesserffy had been unfaithful to the Wall Street Party, which because of Mr. Lesserffy had formerly been a member of that party, expected a clean sweep of the New York desk, irrespective of functional competence. Mr. Pfeiffer further stated — and this is the really important point — that because of the appointment of Mr. Gellert, Mr. Pfeiffer would have to "attack" Mr. Lesserffy from now on, wherever and whenever possible; in short, a declaration of war.

Again, I would like to understand Mr. Pfeiffer's
mental processes. Who does he think he is? Where does he think
he is? This is a very serious world, and not aorfolf Trial
operaetta. Mr. Pfeiffer is not in Budapest conducting a political
campaign against a standard member of a left-wing party. Mr.
Pfeiffer is in New York, an associate of ABCI, with every
opportunity to have his opinion heard and his influence felt.
But after a decision has been made, in good faith and after
long consideration, to employ a man because of his competence
to do a job, it is Mr. Pfeiffer's responsibility as an associate
of the same organization to help that man do his work, and
not to initiate in declarations of war in order to disrupt the
operation.

I am sorry to have had to go into this unpleasant detail,
but after a year of patiently observing and listening, I feel
that I have earned the right to speak plainly.
