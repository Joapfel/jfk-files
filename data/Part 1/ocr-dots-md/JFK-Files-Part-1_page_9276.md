Re your MS. P is in bed with some kind of favor. He got dick today to take the MS to Lloyd McCaffery, chief of research for Republican "hat'! C'ee, who will give it to the Col. Fellers who wrote the "How to Beat the Russians" article in the current "Colliers".

Elliston (Washington Post) has had the MS, at any rate of the last chapter. P rang kay halle@, his sec'y, to ask her to tell him it should get all possible publicity.

Don't worry too much (except in your capacity as a tax-payer, etc.) about the sales. Or Mr. Elliston reading it means more than the thousands reading the "Worlds in Collision" tripe,—which even Churchill can't dis-lodge from A on the East Seller list.

Be Slave-Labor: J suggests possible use, lacking photos, of drawings such as the enclosed from Sept. 13 NY Times Book Reports by Korolkov (Stalin Prize Winner!) and said to be artist of very high calibre. Don't throw it away. Wait & see. A number of his drawings appeared in *Life* a year or two ago; I've seen them, but there is no clue to the date, so you'd have to ask *life*.

Important: J says "Ask Littauer to tell Jenson about it". Of course Littauer will know to what content you can sound out two different publishers simultaneously, should you already have approached Doubleday or another.

Jensen's still being on *life* ("leave of absence") sounds fine, but Littauer will know whether he (Picture Press) could handle production & advertising as well as Doubleday?

Scratch my stuff about a possible Miss photo in my screed of Sept. 15; had no time to proof-read it...

Enclosed what look to me offhand 3 or 4 highly possible quotes from Karsakov & Zinoviy, from frontispiece of Murder in Moscow.

Think that Korolkov idea is worth keeping in mind. You could play up the angle: there are no photos, and they bleat about the "worker's paradise" and refuse all G inspection committees, make survivors swear to say nothing about the camps. It is Joe's fault there are no photos on this page, but this gives an inkling of what it is like, etc.

Korolkov being a Stalin prize-winner adds greatly, it seems to me.
