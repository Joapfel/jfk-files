Subject: LIII/3 Report of Meeting with Carmen Lozada.

1. At 2100 on 16/2/68 LIII/3 reported to Roston that he had met with Carmen Lozada at about 1130 on 17/2/68, at the square in Coyacán. He explained that on 15/2/68 their mutual friend, Carmen Molina, had contacted Carmen Lozada by telephone and had learned that the latter planned to return to Mexico the following day (Friday, 16/2/68). Carmen Lozada had told her on the phone to give her regards to her friend (LIII/3) but to ask him not to meet her at the airport since she would be accompanied by her daughter and would be set by her parents on arrival in Mexico. LIII/3 therefore contacted her through Carmen Molina on the morning of 17/2/68 and made the date to meet her near her parents home.

2. L/3 continued that as soon as we saw Carmen Lozada he knew at once that something unpleasant had happened during her visit to Havana. He told Roston that she had apparently lost five or six pounds, was dressed in a slovenly manner and gave of appearance of a distraught person, almost on the verge of insanity. Her manner and subsequent conversation, often incoherent and half hysterical, confirmed in L/3's mind that she was suffering from severe strain and depression. L/3 told Roston that he understood from her remarks that she had found the Havana airport, Havana, Cuba and the Cubans in general "detestable". She exclaimed at one point: "C dio a esa isla zaldita!" She added that she was on the point of becoming an enemy of Cuba (diverse gusana). In the next breath, however, she stated that she had to return "en pocos días" as soon as treatment had been arranged for her daughter (suffering from asthma). L/3, pretending to think that she had patched up things with her husband, Enrique, asked whether she had been reconciled and found that she really loved her husband after all. She exclaimed, according to L/3, in a half hysterical voice that she hated her husband and everything her represented.

3. L/3 continued that despite his repressed questions Carmen Lozada refused to give him details of what actually had happened to her. She repeatedly stated that she was under "intense, horrible pressure" that she could not explain. When L/3 commented that L/4 was preparing to return to Havana, Carmen Lozada burst out again with "Qué horrible!" but again did not explain. In view of Carmen Lozada's obvious state of psychological and physical disintegration, LIII/3 told Roston that he felt it best to terminate the meeting at the first opportunity (about 1630) and leave as gracefully as he could. He added that when he left he looked back to see her standing in a daze for a full five minutes before walking off. WDL
