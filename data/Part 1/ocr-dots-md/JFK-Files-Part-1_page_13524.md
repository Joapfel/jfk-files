All the information I have been able to gather suggests that the combination of Munoz Marín/Figueroa, as far as Washington is concerned, is unbearable and that Arttime has little choice but to align himself with their way of looking at things--or at least to pay lip service to it.

3. Another angle I would like to explore is President Botancourt. The more I read of his accomplishments as president, the more impressed I am. What would you think of having Arttime, armed with a letter of introduction from Figueroa, pay Botancourt a call before he relinquishes the presidency. Artime's approach would not be that of a supplicant or solicitor of support, but that of a young man making a pilgrimage, sitting at the feet of a revolutionary who has been successful, absorbing knowledge of how it was done. He could ask Botancourt, after he is out from under the daily grind of government, to address himself to the problems of post-Castro Cuba. He could invite him to chair a seminar attended by the MRR leadership. The objective would, of course, be to involve Botancourt in the affairs of the MRR, without putting his name on the masthead and without asking him to contribute anything but reflections of his lustre as a Latin American leader who bequeathed his country a constitutional and democratic regime. Undoubtedly Artime will have to cast off ideological ballast in order to accommodate his reluctant sponsors (Figueroa, Botancourt), but Havana "vaut bien une messe." As a minimum objective, Artime would in any case have to strive to neutralise the latent animosities of the moderate left, denying Manolo Kay an unchallenged ascendancy in monopolizing its support, which he can probably get for the asking.

4. A welcome by-product of the resuscitation of JMHART, is "Combate," an influential periodical with strong readership appeal in Latin America among elements of the moderate left, edited by Monje.
