Chapter X

1. This chapter opens with an unsuccessful internal revolt against President Ysidoras in Guatemala and indicates the desire of the Cuban Fremont and some of the Cuban Brigades in Guatemala to come to his assistance. Hamilton relayed this request to Washington, where it was considered by Bendez, Barnaas and Bissell, and presumably higher levels, and turned down (p. 189). Ysidoras succeeded in putting down this revolt. Hamilton notes that he subsequently learned that several companies of the Brigade had disbursed orders, made a show of force at a rebel stronghold and helped stifle the uprising (p. 187).

2. In said-March 1961, Hamilton was summoned back to Washington to a meeting with Bissell, Barnaas, Jake, Bendez and JamesNoel. Barnaas pointed out that the White House wanted a broader base in the Fremont, and how important it was to the White House to bring Manole Ray into the picture. Hamilton pointed out that the Cubans disliked Ray and that a large percentage of the Cubans accepted the Fremont. (Moe/indicated that Ray was not as bad as Palmael, while Hamilton listed the Cubans objections to Ray's program of Castrolam without Fidel and the length of time it took Ray to become dissatisfied with Castro. Furthermore, Ray's program did not include returning to the Cuban Constitution of 1940 which the others all supported (pp. 185-189). As a result of the desire to force Ray into the Fremont, Hamilton resigned his position in Miami and was reassigned (p. 189). (Jim Noe and Cane replaced him (p. 190)).
