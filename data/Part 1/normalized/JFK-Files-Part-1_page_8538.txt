(Massachusetts Institute of Technology, a second university in the city.)

Classes of 21 November: Bednarek and Gogarty studied from 1930 to 1970. At 1700 Komiyan entered together with Komiukin. Komiukin possessed good, fluent English although his grammar is quite rough. He speaks with a decidedly British accent. Komiukin commented that he had served in Burma (approximately from 1952-65) and more recently (1965-67) had been working in Moscow. Komiukin is married and stated that he had been in Mexico some four months. Komiukin appears to be between 35-40 years of age, blond hair, trim, athletic appearance, approx. 6 feet tall and weighing between 165-170 pounds.

Classes of 23 November: Bizerim and Concharino studied from 1930 to 1970. CONCHARINO commented that his wife and two children were ill with the flu. KABANSEV and KOMMUKIN arrived at 1700 and studied until 1800. During the lesson a slight argument or difference of opinion arose between KOMMUKIN and KAZINTSEV over what the official Soviet position was during the 1964 Cyprus crisis. (I/I could not recall the substance over what they differed on). I/I then queried them as to what the official position was on the present crisis. They both replied that they didn't know as yet what the "position of the USSR was on the current Cyprus dispute. The exchange reflected the obdurate, somewhat dogmatic, character of KOMMUKIN. I/I characterized KOMMUKIN as possessing very strong and fixed ideas. KOMMUKIN did not appear to be awed by KAMISEV's position and spoke openly and forcefully to him. Upon departing I/I spoke with LOGNOV regarding the possible employment of an English teacher for LOGNOV's children. LOGNOV commanded that a Soviet English-speaking teacher/employee thus now speaking his son. I/I had recently met a man named through William (?) PLIT, Director of the British Institute.
