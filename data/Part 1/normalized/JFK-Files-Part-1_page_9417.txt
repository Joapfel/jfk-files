Mr. McKnight concurred, thought we needed to work out the distribution problem by use of a committee which would take charge of publication and distribution - in many languages. Buttes added that we should use Germans and German facilities to see that manuscripts and photos are made available.

(At this point I suggested to Buttes that there would be a question of sponsorship of material.)

McKnight and Butles agreed that Lasky should be contacted and put in a position of discussing the matter with Neillson who is returning to Paris tonight,

Neillson added he would like to get together with Cody and other State people in Paris.

At McKnight's suggestion, Butles said he would communicate with Lasky and ask him to come to Paris to talk about the matter with Neillson.

Following are actual quotes:

'Neillson: "I don't know a great deal about this Cultural Congress, is it apt to produce a major offensive against the USSR and against the peace offensive?"

McKnight: "Yes, I think so. They are a very important bunch of people."

Kelorek: "They are big names... Very high calibre, very impressive; their discussions could be dynamite in putting over telling ideas."

Neillson then pointed out that ECA was going to be in the business of using very important organizations of all kinds in Europe (gave some Italian references) but thought that because of the temporary character of ECA such a thing as development of the Cultural Congress should be turned over to State or CIA to handle and continue. (Somewhere previously I had asked Butles, in order to make a point, whether the Congress was going to be self continuing - Bruce said he thought so.) Neillson said we should develop a full head of steam on the proposition and then should not drop out, should be continued by State through Lasky, or by CIA. (Actually was a plea for CIA to take notice of the Congress and take it over.)
