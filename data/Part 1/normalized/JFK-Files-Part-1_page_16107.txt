When war breaks out anywhere in the world, former OSS man Mitch WerBell is eager and able to supply weapons to either side — or both. And he's only one of several high-flying operators in a dark and growing trade

Newsfront

By GORDON ST. JOSEPH

It is entirely possible, as claimed, that none of America's high-ranking officials received any advance warning of the outbreak of hostilities which ended in Israel's "blitzkrieg" victory over her Arab neighbors. But at least one American authority on modern warfare did. He is a ruddy, monstrosely large, red-bearded, one-eye OSS operative named Mitchell Livingston WerBell III. Two days before the Middle East fighting began last June, he left his Georgia regimental arm an hour before daybreak, drove the 25 miles to Atlanta airport in 19 minutes and boarded a jet for New York, carrying a large, square valise which had been custom-made for him in Germany.

In Manhattan, WerBell checked in at the City Square hotel, went to a double room still carrying the valise, and set it between his legs while he made several phone calls to members he located up in a slim leather notebook. Precisely at noon, he registered the elevator and pushed the "lobby" button with the architraved coat of someone also likely certain that a chauffeur-driven limousine is waiting downstairs - which he was, which it was.
