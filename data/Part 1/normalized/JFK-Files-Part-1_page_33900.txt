In reply refer W-1968 to:

Dear friend,

On one of the matters which we discussed on Wednesday, i.e., the matter possible in Mr. Hughes' letter, we require some assistance from you. We have gone over this topic thoroughly and find that this is the situation.

The number of indigenous people which know will agree to include in their plan on our behalf is very limited.

This being the case, we require from you a compilation of names of indigenous people, not to exceed thirty, whom you would consider indigensible for the re-establishment of an operation similar to that of Kenneth's firm in another area, in time of real heat. It is possible, but not absolutely certain, that we can arrange to include this group of thirty in our provisions for time of trouble.

Moreover, we feel that it would be unwise for us to approach the Department of Defense officials at this time.

Therefore, suggest that you approach the Department of Defense directly and independent: with no reference to the fact that we have an interest in the matter as a whole, and with no reference to our interest in any part of it (e.g. the matter thirty above). We feel that General Clay and other influential citizens who are concerned with your organization may be of assistance in the direct approach which we have recommended.

I need not assure you that this cause is one of special interest on our part; nor do I mean to stress the practical considerations within the assisting department which compel us to declare our own limitations and to suggest the direct independent approach, for you are, I am sure, the first to understand a problem of this type.
