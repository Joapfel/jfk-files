4. Nevertheless, there are several mystifying aspects to the LITEPO report. It is quite incredible that the LITEPO airport team would know NOVICKY'S name, and equally incredible that they could confuse this name with that of PROCHEAZKA. They listed NOVICKY's full name, correctly spelled. Whore then, if NOVICKY had not actually arrived, did they get his name?

5. Secondly, it is most unusual, and the only instance the Station can recall, for only one courier to make the trip to Mexico. They have always traveled here in pairs. (NOTE: It has been the case recently, however, for the couriers to travel to Mexico via Cuba and leave via Cuba, instead of traveling via Air France through New York, then to Havana, return to Mexico and depart Mexico via Air France again.) Further, PROCHEAZKA's name is not on the list of known career couriers or audio technicians and sweepers of Book Dispatch 6423, dated 21 June 1968. A further curious departure from normal procedure was that according to LIENVOY (7 and 8 June 1968) reports, the courier PROCHEAZKA did not stay at the Czech schoolhouse but at the Ambassador's residence. Per LIENVOY, and confirmed on the CUBANA manifest, PROCHEAZKA departed Mexico on 10 June 1968, for Havana.

6. Finally, but quite possibly purely coincidental, there were two references to NOVICKY about the same time as his reported arrival. In one of the DISPERSE tapes, #351, in mid-June 1968, Oldrich STANCL (201-777862), in a rambling, slightly drunken speech, refers to one MIKA NOVICKY, as "his brother" (presumably referring to Oldrich NOVICKY). Another even more oblique reference, but again intriguing because of the timing, was a reference in a LIFET report dated 7 June 1968; a local leftist journalist, Jorgo ROSALES Natividad in a conversation with Nikolai LEONOV (201-132139), the Soviet Press Officer, sometime before 1120 hours, said that he "had asked NOVICKI for an opinion on SIEMPRE" (Mexican magazine). There was no further explanation of who "NOVICKI" was, but Station records indicate ROSALES Natividad had been a regular contact of Oldrich NOVICKY, during the latter's tour here as Press Attaché of the Czech Embassy. Just prior to this reference to "NOVICKI", ROSALES had been talking to LEONOV about having gotten a lot of material from Cuba (for an article about Che GUEVARA). Does Headquarters have any information about a possible trip or assignment for NOVICKY in Cuba?

7. In summary, the Station does not know exactly how, or why, NOVICKY, a Czech intelligence officer, might have entered Mexico clandestinely, but believes it was possible.
