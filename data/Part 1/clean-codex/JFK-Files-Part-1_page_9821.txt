27 February 1968

Subject: LIMING/3 (LIKING/3) Report of Conversation with LIMING/2.

1. At 0930 on 27/2/68, LIMING/3 reported to Rowton that he had spoken briefly to Echemendía (Luzmila Suarez de la Paz) on 24/2/68 and to LIMING/2 at some length on 26/2/68 in the LIMING offices. L/3 explained to Rowton that Echemendía had passed a message to him through L/4 that he wanted to talk to him and that he would telephone him to arrange a meeting. L/3 continued that Echemendía finally consented to see him by phone at about noon on 24/2/68 and asked him to come to the LIMING office that afternoon. L/3 told Rowton that when he finally saw Echemendía, the latter was very vague and general in his statements and that all he could gather from his statements was that Echemendía was apologetic for the “misunderstanding” that had arisen as a result of L/4’s request for a re-entry permit. Echemendía stated that “we” are concerned that L/3, whom they considered honest and above reproach in his dealings with PERUMED, might have fallen victim to unscrupulous elements who constantly surround the revolution. L/3 confessed to Rowton that this vague and almost incoherent (incherent) line of Echemendía annoyed him to such an extent that he felt it useless to continue his conversation with him and, as a result, terminated it at the first opportunity.

2. LIMING/3 continued that the meeting with LIMING/2 on 26/2/68 at the latter’s office had much more fruitful results. According to L/3, L/2 began by stating that “they” appreciated the information L/3 had passed to them at various times in the past, specifically the report of Julian Lopez’s arrest, the proposed publication of the arrest in the Guatemalan press, the report concerning the letter of Rico Galán and the Orozco (Crozco) affair, but they were frankly perplexed with regard to the source of such sensitive information. L/3 told Rowton that he explained to L/2 that he had excellent Central American contacts, as well as Mexican sources in good positions, who could furnish such information without naming anyone. He told L/2 that he had no intention of jeopardizing his business contacts by revealing their identity, and he felt that what he had done was in the interest of PERUMED and the revolution, and that L/2 and his friends should not look a gift horse in the mouth and see spies behind every blade of grass, just because they themselves had no capability to check out his reports.

3. L/3 then told Rowton that L/2 asked directly about “el Griego” and his relationship to L/3. L/3 stated that he feigned surprise and asked what “Greek,” adding that he knew several Greeks in Guatemala, Venezuela and Mexico. L/2 clarified by stating that he meant the Greek introduced to him.
