Case 12,667. Report 12,667.

A. Iron Curtain Refugee Campaign of International Rescue Committee.

Re-Czajkowski’s phone request for additional information on Abram J. Becker (Abram J. Becker): (1) Our relationship with Becker. David Martin, then executive director, introduced us to Becker, who had joined IRC staff in April 1955, sometime in autumn 1955, to provide an alternate with the IRC in his own absence.

We did not have any dealings with Becker until he succeeded Martin as executive director in June 1952. At that time, he assured us of his willingness to cooperate with us to the fullest extent possible and immediately supplied proof of this by furnishing detailed biographical information on refugees which the IRC brought to this country. His cooperation in this project was splendid. We had practically no contact with him since summer 1952 because IASO placed him in the “disapproved” category, although a re-evaluation in December 1952 transferred him to “limited” security status.

Becker has never volunteered information of potential interest to us, but this fact may be explained by (a) our own attitude of aloofness toward him and (b) our failure to brief him concerning areas of mutual interest.

(C) Our evaluation of Becker: In view of the above, we have only a limited basis for judging him. He appears honest, straightforward, discreet, and intelligent, more familiar with social welfare practices but considerably less knowledgeable concerning the DP community than his predecessor. We have had no reason of our own, as distinguished from NAS, whose judgment appears to be based on information unrelated to us, to question his character or loyalty. His reputation among other resettlement agents appears to be good. It should be emphasized, however, that we do not have sufficient experience with him to arrive at any conclusiveness of his personality.
