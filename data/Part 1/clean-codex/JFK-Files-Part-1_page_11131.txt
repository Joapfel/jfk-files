2. Considerable time has been spent by Station reviewing with L-9 his access to information and the possibility of increasing his productivity to make his return to Cuba worthwhile.

A. Although L-9 lives in Cuba with his brother and brother's wife, who would logically seem to have access to some interesting information, both are very reserved in what they tell him. Although L-9 believes his brother is basically anti-Communist, the latter has never made any real anti-regime comment or disclosed any sensitive information to him. Brother and sister-in-law seldom discuss their contacts in ministries and generally limit themselves to noncontroversial topics.

B. L-9 lived a very lonely and sheltered life in Cuba and had no real friends. The few good social contacts he had in some embassies refrained from discussing anything sensitive with him. L-9 commented that “although I carefully never say anything against the regime, everyone knows I am a bourgeois intellectual and not a sympathizer.”

C. L-9 sees no hope of transferring to Foreign Affairs, External Trade, or another target ministry. He does not believe the Cuban government would send him with an embassy abroad to fill a post.
