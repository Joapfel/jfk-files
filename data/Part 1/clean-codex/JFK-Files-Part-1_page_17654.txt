Report of Project LIFAT to According to New Standards

1. Product of the Project

The Soviet Embassy

a. Soviet Commercial Activities

Reports submitted during the past year have conveyed a substantial amount of detail concerning Soviet economic and commercial policy in Mexico, and to a lesser extent, in adjacent areas. This information has reflected the types of products the Soviets are selling and purchasing in Mexico, the manner of their intermediaries, details on prices, procedures used for arranging sales and purchases and the manner of affecting shipments. This information has been screened from extensive and prolonged coverage of Soviet industry and commercial traffic and appears to be accurate and detailed. Within the past year traffic concerning the Soviet commercial office in Mexico has increased considerably and remains the station's primary source for information on Soviet commercial activities. Pertinent examples of this type of information may be found in:

HT-2176, dated 1 March 1957, “Tentative deal for Scandinavian Coffee.”

HM-1973, dated 15 October 1956, “Mexican exports to the US.”

Hr-1760, dated 19 October 1956, “Sale of Soviet metal (SovietERRORS'etal) in the U.S.”

b. Soviet Diplomatic Activities

Project LIFAT coverage of Soviet diplomatic activities appears to have been quite thorough and illuminating during the past year. Reports submitted on these activities have indicated some relationships the Soviets are developing with the Mexican Government, with other Latin American governments, and with other foreign diplomatic representatives. LIFAT operations have also established some facts concerning the relations among the Soviets, Poles (Polses) and Czechoslovakia in Mexico City. Some indication has been received on the relations between the Soviets and the Yugoslavs. Reports of this type provide some evidence as to whom the Soviets intend to be enshrined in a drive to establish contacts in various departments of the Mexican Government.
