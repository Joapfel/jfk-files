“Benny” also includes two intelligence reports dated 20 March 1964 in which he says:

“The Air Force Captain, Remigio Gonzalez Espinosa, is being actively sought by the G-2. The charges are that he took apart the controls of five “MIG-21s” on 2 March 1964, one day before practices, in the town of Mariel, Pinar del Rio Province, provoking the crash that killed the pilot, Captain Dominguez Blas, and a woman photographer of the “FAR” aboard the “MIG-21.”

The other report says:

“Soldier Armando Guevara L. was declared a rebel because he did not agree with the Government, was captured by the G-2 in Guaimaro, Oriente Province, at the beginning of March 1964, and was executed on the 14th of the same month.”

Conclusions: This acting officer says that, according to what was reported by Bernardo Nievares, known as “Benny,” in his reports to Nacin, regarding the news that the G-2 set four men free—Ramon Mondejar Cid, known more as “El Diablo,” together with four other men—it is to be noted that Ramon Mondejar Cid and the other four men, Pablo Gilberto Vargas Diaz; Ramon Nieves Guirao, known as “Ramon I”; Jose Aragon Milian, known as “Carragu ao”; and Rodolfo Licor Montesinos arrived in this city on 2 April 1964 clandestinely by boat, after they succeeded in reaching a point 20 miles away from the Cuban coast, where they were picked up by the U.S. Coast Guard and taken to Key West, Florida, where they underwent interrogations by the American Immigration Authorities and are now free in this city of Miami, Florida.

All have been located by the undersigned and interrogated separately.
