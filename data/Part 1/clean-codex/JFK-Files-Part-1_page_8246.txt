Questions: Did you know that you were an object of undue suspicion?

Answers: I presumed that I was. I was an unusual stranger since tourism is not a usual thing in that part of the country. Hence I set about to remove any suspicion. I acted very much American, being all over the place and being curious; but I gave purpose to my curiosity and explained it in each place. Everywhere I engaged people in conversation and gathered other people, explaining my purpose, interests and why I had such interests. I think that in each instance I logically explained my presence and worked into the questions that I asked. I think the idea of going to the Communists accomplished my purpose. I was not a subject of interest to the newspapers, which I might well have been had they had suspicions. I worked out in the open. In dining rooms I would find some excuse to say, “Do you speak English?” and usually someone did. “Could you explain something to me—how is this prepared? We don't have it in the U.S., etc.” Pretty soon we were sitting together, ordering a drink, and invariably the owner of the hotel would come and sit with us and tell me he would write a note to a particular newspaper editor, etc. As a result, I found that about half of the time I was directed to people. I never felt that I was suspect, except by Americans, and I think they thought that I was checking up on them since they tried to avoid me.

Questions: Did you ever find, or have the feeling, that you were followed from the airport, etc.?

Answers: I had that feeling once, but I wasn't being followed. For example, I had the feeling that I had seen the same man in several places, but dismissed it because I didn't see him again. Also, so many of those people look alike that I may have been confused. In the airports I engaged people in conversation, always playing the role of the curious American, but primarily one who is happy with their country.
