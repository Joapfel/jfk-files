The plans for the Nicaragua (Ilicaragua) shipboat fleet this year call for 80 boats. All 40 coastal boats of all types bring the total to 120 boats, which will require repair yard services four times per year (three being normal groundings and one emergency), for a total of 480 annually. The local repair yards are able to service only 100 groundings. This situation has forced many shipboats to seek repairs outside of the country, either in Honduras, Mexico, or the United States, with the resulting foreign-exchange loss of fishing time and profits for Nicaragua.

Initial

Technical Limitations: The lack of local technical personnel and the time required to import various mechanical parts and materials.

Consideration was given to future expansion when designing the shipyard and repair yard. Carpentry and mechanic workshops are also planned. There are two factors, however, that affect the overall operation. The first factor is the lack of technical personnel trained in steel construction of boats, but this will be overcome initially by personnel trained by the Company. The second factor will be resolved only by efficient coordination between the distribution and supply personnel of the Company in Miami. In this way, it will be possible to have any spare part or material forwarded to Bluff within two weeks after the request is made by the local offices via airplane. Furthermore, plans have been prepared to stock the shipyard warehouses with spare parts.
