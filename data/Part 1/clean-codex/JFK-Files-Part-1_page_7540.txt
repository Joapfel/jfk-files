Pertinent Information.

Page 69. The July 30, 1932 issue of the New York Times, under a Shanghai dateline of July 29, 1932, states that Harold R. Isaacs, an American citizen, was warned by the U.S. consul in Shanghai that the U.S. might withdraw its extraterritorial rights and allow him to be tried in a Chinese court. Isaacs, then editor of the China Forum, had violently attacked Chiang Kai-Shek (Chaing Kai Shek), calling him, among other things, a butcher. The article further stated that Isaacs was an associate of Agnes Smedley, a correspondent for American newspapers.

Page 73. The March 7, 1946 issue of the New York Times reports an article captioned “Group of Americans, including writers and journalists,” announcing that they had sent a letter to Chiang Kai Shek (Chaing Kai Shek) concerning the death of Yang Chao, an OWI employee who was arrested by the Nationalists and died in prison.

The signers (sighers) were critical of the treatment received by Chao. The following individuals signed the letter: Maxwell S. Stewart, Elizabeth Ames, Maroline Slade, Alfred Kazin, Eric Sever Reid, Richard Wasts, Jr., N. R. Isaacs, Agnes Smedley, and Theodore White.
