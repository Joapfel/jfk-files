Body of late September 1964. Place: United States, Miami. Date acquired: 10 September 1964.

Field report No. 27G-500.

This is unclassified information. Source data are confidential. Approval of content is necessary.

Source: A member of a group of Cuban exiles trained in the elements of information collection. This group has provided useful reports for about three years. This information was obtained from an individual with first-hand knowledge.

1. On a weekend during the latter part of September 1964, a group of about 250 Cuban exiles were receiving military training at the “Jose Marti Encampment,” which was on a farm owned by a New Jersey councilman or individual of similar political position. The farm is in a mountainous region just off Interstate Route 80, one and a half hours by automobile from Union City, New Jersey. The camp is directed by Marinos Perez (FM) and Bienvenido Ojeda; it is active only on weekends, but there is a permanent armed guard at the camp.

2. Training consists of stoning and exploiting billy redobles, and training in the use of weapons which range from semiautomatic to fully automatic rifles, submachine guns, and .30- and .50-caliber machine guns.

3. Molandó Veracruz being and about 50 of his followers were among those undergoing training. Marinos also are reported to say that this camp was being operated without any financial assistance from the United States Government. Marinos also said that rather severe measures would be effected against any United States Government official who acts or is discovered at the camp.

4. According to some of the individuals who were undertaking training, small groups of Marinos’ men were periodically being sent from the New Jersey area to Miami, and Alfaro (FM) was administering the transfers. After arriving in Miami, they were taken to Sheriff’s Key, or some small key in the vicinity of Sheriff’s Key, where they were being staged prior to invading Miami, as to be the first step in the plan for an eventual landing in Cuba.

5. There was no apparent degree of ill-feeling between many former Castro adherents and the “Marinos’ Tigers” at the camp. Some Puerto Ricans were also in training there.
