Classified message.

DJAK 99. IN 1994.

Imbalance of Indonesia’s pro-Bloc orientation. Whether this new balance will be tipped in favor of the West is problematic and depends on further Western initiatives and the character of the Bloc counteroffensive. Two Indonesian schools of thought prevail. One, consisting of middle-level bureaucracy, apparently sees the outcome of the West Irian issue as propelling Indonesia closer to the West. The other, which reportedly includes elements of the Palace Guard and such professional state philosophers as Ruslan Abdulgani (AEDULGANI), seems to believe some overtures to the Bloc are necessary in order to blunt any impression that the West Irian settlement will compromise the Indonesian version of neutralism to the advantage of the West.

Internally, the vast and disciplined PKI (Indonesia Communist Party) organization is somewhat quiescent and seemingly still awaiting guidelines from Moscow or Peking on the twists its subversion should take in the wake of the West Irian settlement. However, the PKI is observed adding to the momentum for demobilizing the army and lessening army influence, which is the main obstacle to PKI ambitions in internal political affairs. The Indonesian military is countering this momentum by seeking to guide limited demobilization in a manner calculated to preserve its status under Guided Democracy as one of four main functional groups controlling Indonesia’s fortunes.

5. Sukarno gives no impression he intends to liberalize his authoritarian regime. On the military side, his retooling of “liberal” elements from the government bureaucracy is being intensified, and new impetus is being given to the National Front as Sukarno’s catchall and main political instrument for controlling and guiding the energies of emasculated political parties (PKI excepted) and functional groups to consolidate the precepts of Guided Democracy.

(End of message.)
