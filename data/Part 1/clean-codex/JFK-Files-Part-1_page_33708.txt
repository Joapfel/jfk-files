Miss Augustin advised that:

3. So far as she knew, the Socialist Union of Central and Eastern Europe (founded in June 1951) had never approached PCI for support and recognition. In October 1951, the Central European Federation of Christian Trade Unions was formed, and soon afterward approached PCI for support.

Prior to this, and just prior to his departure for Europe, Lessen, who is MCC’s representative of labor and trade union affairs and who is in Paris, where he has responsibility for the International Center of Free Trade Organizations in Paris, stated that it was his impression that the Polish Christian Trade Unions were the only Christian trade unions of any pro-Weary significance in the countries now behind the Iron Curtain and that, if further investigation justified this view, he thought modest support ought to be extended abroad to the Polish Christian Trade Unions. He suggested to Little knowledge of Christian trade unions.

The Central European Federation of Christian Trade Unions is composed of cities residing in the United States or Canada and not abroad, which threw another monkey wrench into the situation. At the time of the approach to PCI of the Christian Trade Union Federation, their representative was told of Benazet’s existence and his existence abroad, and of his need for statistics, information, and Deben’s initial view that support should be abroad and not here. No statistics were forthcoming, but Sieniewicz (Secretary-General of the Christian Democratic) was active, apparently stirring up American Catholic circles by harrying on them that PCI allowed Socialists to mobilize trade union staffs.
