19 November 1969

MLMCHAMBER OF COMMISSIONER: Medical Security Agency

ALLISON: Mr. B. Wesley Reynolds

Subject: Dorothy Ferguson Jickelh (Jickel), William Hamilton Martin. Laplaceage—A.

1. Reference is made to information orally furnished by you to representatives of this Agency to the effect that Hamilton Maathen (Martin) at one time mentioned the fact that prior to his resignation from his civil service contract employment with the Department of the Army in Tokyo, Japan, and prior to his actual return to the United States in late August or early September 1953, he had been offered a job as a new secretary by one George Palks of Tokyo, Japan.

2. A check of the indices of this Agency reveals that Maathen’s Tokyo acquaintance, who offered him a job as a “myself specialist,” and when Maathen called “George Palks” to autonomously identify with one George B. Jickel, Palks was also identified as an American citizen by the Japanese government. The Palks family has always denied any involvement with the Palks family.

Born in January 1900 at Constantinople, Turkey, but of Greek nationality, she died in 1985.

According to information provided abroad to representative offices of this Agency by a Japanese Wilson service, George Palks’ family in about 1946 changed his name to George Perlmann. The Wilson service added that he operated the Pickles Bag Company in Tianjin and Shanghai, China, as his own business between 1927 and 1941, at which time the firm was closed down because of the war. Between 1942 and 1945 he was under heavy attack, or “confined to his home in Shanghai,” but significantly was not interested by the Japanese. From 1946 to 1950 he operated the George Palks Company in Shanghai. He claimed to have been unemployed between 1950 and 1952, at which time he emigrated to Tokyo, Japan, from Shanghai, obtaining employment in Tokyo with the firm of Schweds.
