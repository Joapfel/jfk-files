Executive Secretary of the Czechoslovak National Council of Women, she was a member of the International Women’s Organization, too. She travelled to many countries representing the Czechoslovakian women. Thus, she foresaw the threat of Hitler’s dictatorship, greed, and power, and organized the Women’s Social Movement, popular with its social welfare groups.

Milada Horáková (Honkova) was known and well known in Prague. She was able to recruit an underground army of former social workers to fight the Nazis.

She organized an underground that baffled the Germans. Instead of large meetings, she set up a chain of small groups. Only the head of each group knew the other members. These group heads would meet in different homes or in a doctor’s office. Thus, when the Gestapo arrested an underground woman worker, they would be unable to make a mass arrest.

Milada and her resistance—constantly embarrassed by months. They arranged hiding and led the partisans, helped Jews to escape the clutches of the Gestapo, set up a radio spy network, and distributed leaflets to steel the resistance.

At the same time, Milada helped to write a new constitution to be used when the Nazis were destroyed. She was in constant contact, via secret radio, with the Czechoslovak government-in-exile in London. Thus, she transmitted the progress of her work on the post-war constitution.
