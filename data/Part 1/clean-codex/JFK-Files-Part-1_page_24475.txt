Revolution

1. During the past year, Revolution and its consequences have been of great importance in the history of Africa. Latin America has also experienced some very important changes. For a time during the past few years it was reported that the publication was in financial difficulty, but in the most recent French edition, No. 12, dated October–November, it was announced that the magazine would continue on a new basis, with an enlarged Advisory Board, appearing every two months, not only in an English and a French edition but also in a coming Spanish edition to be published in Mexico for Latin America.

2. The changes which are being made do not in any way alter the pro-Chinese complexion of the magazine. Though the magazine now seems on a much firmer financial basis, there is as yet no evidence that the Chinese are directly intervening in it. They are, however, buying copies and taking advertisements. It has been reported that when Vergou (OCR: Vergou) was in Peking (Teking) in the early part of 1963, the Chinese agreed to take out 3,000 subscriptions to “Africa—1963,” and those were in due course transferred to Africa. In the African Revolution, when the publication sold for five shillings, this was worth £750 a month. Each issue has continued at least one full-page advertisement of Chinese Communist publications.

3. The publication has also had contact with the Chinese through Abdul Eohuman Mohammed Labu (Abduл Eohuman Mohammed Labu), a member of the Advisory Board who is Minister for Commerce and Co-operatives in the Tanzanian Government. Labu visited China in 1960, became the East African correspondent of the New China News Agency in 1961, and went to China again in October 1963.

4. By the end of 1963, Revolution had established contact with the Communist splinter-group in the UK, led by Michael Lomé, which is on the Chinese side in the Sino-Soviet cold war.
