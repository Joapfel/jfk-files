This, Nosenko himself has stated, did not occur to him to tell us because the stakeout had long since been dropped, it had revealed nothing suspicious on the part of Abidian or anyone else, and therefore he had regarded it as insignificant.

This is not implausible. Another possible explanation, however, derived from the already noted inability of Nosenko to pin down a date for the visit, is that he learned of the stationary surveillance post, if not of the visit itself, after his meetings with us in June 1962. It should be noted in this context that with the public exposure of the Penkovskiy case in the fall of 1962, the Pushkin Street deaddrop undoubtedly became the subject of widespread interest within the KGB.

That Nosenko is at a minimum still confused about the visit of Abidian to the Pushkin Street deaddrop and its consequences is clear from the record. While it is entirely possible that Nosenko has consciously exaggerated his involvement with the visit and its aftermath, it is also possible that the evident distortions of his accounts of the affair derive from honest confusion.

Current interviews and a check of the tapes of previous interviews leave no doubt that Nosenko was aware of the visits of Abidian to the upper Gorkiy Street area circa March 1961. These visits by Abidian were for cover purposes and preceded his start of operational.
