Earned first half of baccalaureate in electrotechnology, then expelled.

1938–39, Paris School of Photography. Trained as cine camera operator and movie technician. Left after 1-3/4 years of a 2-year course due to outbreak of WWII in November 1939.

Occupational Specialty: Mechanical electrical engineering; cine or portrait photography. Researcher and innovator of commercial labor- and money-saving devices, electromechanical or photographic in nature. See paragraphs 4, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, and 14 of Employment History for examples.

Military Service:

December 1939, Paris: Volunteered for the French Foreign Legion.

January 1940, Tunisia, North Africa: In 1st Cavalry Regiment, 1st Squadron, 4th Platoon.

February 1940: Sent to NCO training course in the Legion. Pulled out to be reassigned to an all-Georgian unit.

March–August 1940, Barcares: Assigned to Georgian unit.

August 1940, Paris: Discharged after the end of Franco-German hostilities as Private First Class.

Languages and Dialects:

French: Speaks, reads, and writes; native.

Georgian: Fluent spoken; reading and writing good.

German: Good.

English: Reads fluently; writes fair; speaks fair.

Spanish: Made good; speaks and writes poor.

Russian: Poor.

French: Poor.

Employment History:

1. 1942, Paris: Volunteered for work in Germany.

2. Poelitsa: Worked for HIDRYER WERKE on contract for one year in Germany as an installation electrician.

3. 1942, Paris: Self-employed at photo shop; portrait photography.

4. 1942, Berlin: Photographer of metals for a factory that produced electrodes for welding instruments.

5. 1942, Berlin: Assembly-line worker in a Berlin factory producing steel ammunition belts. Received commendation from the factory director for a labor-saving device concerning the manufacture of a device for automatic adjustment of cartridge cases.

5. 1942, Berlin, Muelheim: Drivers’ mechanics course for the NSKK (Nazi Party Motorized Corps). Drove a truck during construction of the Atlantic Wall by the Germans.
