For this reason it is recommended that either one of the two following courses of action may be taken, preferably the first.

1. In very unequivocal language inform subject that his official connection to the U.S. interests in the Congo has been and continues to be so. This continued contact with these interests could result in his apprehension (if for nothing else, for possessing forged documents). Instruct him that he clearly understood that his relationship with this organization could be terminated under the terms of the contract and although we are appreciative of his past efforts, he is now on his own.

2. Bring him back to the U.S. and go forward with the original plan to resettle subject in Mexico. He made preliminary inquiries about this possibility 19 August 1960 before he came with Africa Division and the only thing that stopped the transfer plans was the possibility that he could be used in the Congo.

Solution number one runs the risk of having subject get disgruntled, and then running to the wrong people to tell all he knows. On the other hand, if the man reacts to direction as we are told he does, this may be all that he needs to stay clear of the station. It is apparent that at some point in time he has to be given the gate.

Solution number two runs into the financial problem as well as dealing with an individual who is no longer connected with this organization. However, it does meet one of the “requirements” laid down by the COB in Frankfurt back in 1958, i.e., “Given some money and a job he could be resettled in any South American country without difficulty.” The second solution is inviting, if for no other reason, in terms of getting subject out of a rather difficult area.

3. The 3rd possibility is to bring him directly to a 3rd country and take the documents away from him there—then tell him he is on his own.
