Despite the detailed explanation provided Mr. Singerman, we note in his letter of appeal that he continues to seek a basis for the decision. With regard to these documents, we believe and agree that releasing them to Mr. Singerman would violate protection of sensitive sources and methods. The original denial is correct and should be upheld by the Information Review Committee.

4. Our review of the remaining three documents (items f., g. and h.) has resulted in the following recommendations.

a. TI-539083. A passenger list which the Agency apparently obtained through foreign liaison (Canadian). The nature of the information, the passenger manifest of a Cuban controlled ship, does not permit segregating and releasing parts of the list to Mr. Singerman. We believe the document is exempt under (b)(1), (b)(3) of the FOIA and 5B(1) of B.O. 11652.

b. MEXICO CITY 330. Provides the same information as RMEA-37509 and is, therefore, exempt under the same exemptions cited in the original denial, i.e., (b)(1), (b)(3) and 5B(1).

c. EX-3663. We believe certain parts of this 25-page document can be segregated and released to Mr. Singerman. We agree with ITG on the selected portions that should be extracted for release (see attached marked copy). The balance of the document, however, must be considered exempt under (b)(3)(a) and (b)(6) of the FOIA.

5. In summary, it is recommended that the appeal be denied and the exemptions upheld on all the related documents as listed above except in the case of EX-3663. The selected portions should be sanitized and provided to Mr. Singerman. This recommendation has been discussed with Mr. Makowka of the OCC and he concurs with our decision.
