Revolt was a quick look at the border. Communists were a go-on at the university, the young detective. The Communist had found “Hunt black markers” against him in seven-year record covering all phases of his daily activities, from trips abroad with the fencing team to conversations in restaurants.

In Young Orleat's (Orleat's) view, it was only because he had won the world championship at Cremona, Italy, in 1934 and because he had kept up his studies as a badly needed student of pharmacy. No action had been taken against him.

At the time Orleat and his friend decided to meet for the border, some 60,000,000 Czechoslovakians (Czechuungarians) had already fled to Austria. Those who had lived near Zhouzh (Zhouzh) knew the land near the border and made their way to freedom with little opposition. But others had to make their way from Budapest.

On November 10, Thomas and his friend dressed in workers' clothing and managed to board a train—one of the few still moving to the West. They traveled to the first town outside Budapest without being stopped. There, they hitched a ride with a truck driver who was headed for the border and asked no questions. Although they were stopped “six or seven times” by Soviet soldiers, they traveled with the truck to within 90 miles of the border.

For the final stretch, the two escaping students were joined by a young couple. All four of them traveled by foot for two days to cover their last 90 miles.
