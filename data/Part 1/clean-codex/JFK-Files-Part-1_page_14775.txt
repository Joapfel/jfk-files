3. Minsk Institute of Foreign Languages was located on Ulitsa Zakharova, near Pobedy Square. It had four teacher-training faculties (English, German, French, and Spanish), as well as an interpreter faculty comprised of four departments representing the same languages. Subject claims that about eighty percent of students, especially those of German and Spanish, were drawn from rural areas of Belorussia. Among the other students were some children of Belorussian VIPs.

4. Subject knew several graduates of the interpreter faculty of his institute who were part of a small group eventually circulating on foreign assignments. Subject stated that they travelled abroad under contracts with various Soviet ministries, though in most cases working as military translators/interpreters. Upon return to the USSR, their contracts were terminated and they had to seek other jobs while awaiting possible new assignments abroad. Subject was not informed on the IR CP membership and believed that most of the translators known to him did not belong to the Party.
