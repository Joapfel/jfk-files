Been in close contact with E. E. Kitch when the latter was in China; also lived in this building in the early 1930s. An apartment, 509.

After her arrival in China, Viola Rubinson Isaacs was employed at the DaH Cung Middle School and, by November 1933, was reported as a teacher at the Comparative Law School in Shanghai.

On 7 August 1933, the Isaacs left Shanghai for Japan and, on 16 August 1933, proceeded to Dalian, returning to Shanghai by rail on 26 August 1933. In December 1933, Isaacs left Shanghai for Peking to gather firsthand information on the newly formed People's Revolutionary Government of the Republic of China.

In January 1934, Isaacs is reported to have visited Mr. and Mrs. Noulens while they were on a hunger strike in prison. In the spring of 1934, Isaacs and his wife left for Peking. En route to Peking, at which city they resided at 2 T'iang Pu Chia Tao, East City. His mailing address was 3 of one Mr. Urexann; Grand Hotel, Peking.

As has been set out heretofore, Isaacs is reported to have turned against the Stalinist Government of the USSR, becoming a protégé. During this period he wrote The Tragedy of the Chinese Revolution, allegedly as a single gesture of interpretation and protest to show how, in his opinion, both the Kuaxintang (Kuomintang) and the Communists had gone wrong in China. Also, in April 1936, under the pen name of Le Sen, Isaacs [wrote] an article published in the New Militant, the organ of the BY District Workers' Party. The article is reportedly an attack on Stalin and was entitled, “Stalintona ready to embrace Chiang Kai Shuk (Chiang Kai-shek) ... P. Officer Chinese Red Army in return for resumption of disastrous 1937 bioe.”
