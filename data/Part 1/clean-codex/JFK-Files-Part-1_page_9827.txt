4 February 1968

1. LIRING/4 signed a contract with Rowton (alias), effective 15 February through 30 April 1968, providing payment for information and services. No obligations were specified other than financial, totaling $750.00 for the period specified. LIRING/4 also signed acknowledgement of the alias “Maria Barra-gán,” which was used in the contract and receipts for funds.

2. L/4’s departure, originally scheduled for 16 February 1968, will be delayed until the following Friday to permit intensive care and security training and strengthen the backstop for the objective of the trip. On 12 February, LIRING/2 told L/4 that he would issue a permit to return and, in an expression of apparently genuine sympathy, urged her to take the trip and remain “where she belonged.” On the morning of 13 February, Echemandía (Ismael Suarez de la Paz), who met L/4 by chance inside the LIRING compound, remarked that he was happy to learn she was going back home for a trip and then added, “Go back and stay.” He then invited her to lunch or dinner, but L/4 felt it wise to turn him down the first time. They had lunch in the LIRING dining room. On Rowton’s suggestion, she will, however, accept an outside lunch so she can sound him out further on his reaction to her return.

3. Simultaneously, in order to build up conviction in the minds of LIRING officials that L/4 was returning because of a marital problem with LIRING/3, which she herself stated to Rowton that she hesitated to reveal to anyone, LIRING/3 talked with LIRING/2 in his office on 13 February, at a moment when L/4 was elsewhere in the LIRING offices, and pretended to have just learned of L/4’s request for permission to return. L/3, in an angry tone, alleged that he could not understand why L/4 would want to return, that he had treated her as well as he could, and that in any case he would not permit her to take their daughter. LIRING/2, although obviously somewhat embarrassed, took L/4’s part and urged L/3 to reconsider and give permission for L/4 to take her daughter along, since she would be reluctant to leave as a “good mother.” L/2 went so far as to state that if L/3 insisted, it would be tantamount to “blackmail” against LIRING/4. L/2 continued that he felt obliged as a LIRING official to grant LIRING/4 permission to return, but he did not want to oblige her to depart without her child. LIRING/3 told Rowton that he did not give in and left LIRING/2 with the impression that his mind was made up regarding the child.

4. LIRING/4, who was not aware of the above action by LIRING/3, accepted the child.
