Nikolay Leonov (LONOV)

20 May 1955: Rodolfo Nervo called Subject and was told, “We have found the books and material you requested (re Soviet foreign policy).” Leonov (LEO NOT) said he would take the books to Nervo the next day. Nervo had been active for many years in the Mexican Diplomatic Service, at one time being Chargé of the Mexican Legation in Warsaw, Poland. Now about 75 and inactive in government, he writes articles for the newspaper Excelsior (HEMA 3736 5 Leo 55).

6 January 1956: The daughter of General Heriberto Jara spoke with Subject at the Soviet Embassy, saying she had been told that he was the person who could inform her when she should come by to pick up translations of the “clinical documents” relative to herself and her father. Subject said they were having some trouble getting the documents translated because of the medical terms, but said they should be ready in about five days (HEM 4200).

1 February 1956: Subject called Velia Jara de Nieto, daughter of General Jara, and told her that translations of her father’s clinical history had been made from Russian into Spanish. Mrs. Nieto said she would visit the Embassy the next day and ask Leonov (LEO NOV) for the materials (HEM 4410).

24 February 1956: One Samuel Ramos contacted Subject, who acts as secretary to the Soviet Ambassador, Mexico. Ramos told Leonov (LEO NOV), “We are not going to meet at my house this afternoon, but at Salgo’s, which is at Insurgentes 429-51 at the usual hour.” Subject replied that he thought he could get to the meeting at 1900 hours and sent greetings “to the boys.” Ramos said, “We have some books we want to order through Leonov (LEO NOV)” (HIM 4336).

2 March 1956: Samuel Ramos left a message for Subject that “if he has time I hope he will come to my house today” (HIM 4600).

7 March 1956: Samuel Ramos called the Embassy and asked for data on the possibility of staging an international chess match in Mexico. In this conversation it was indicated that Ramos is in the academic line of work. He stated that when he was “back in classes,” meetings could no longer be held in his home. He told Leonov (LEO NOV) that a tournament featuring a person named “Pomard” would begin Monday, 12 March, and invited Subject to see some of it. Leonov (LEO NOV) said he would attend if he had time. Ramos then asked when he could see Leonov (LEO NOV) to give him a list of books “we want.” Leonov (LEO NOV) told him to come to the Embassy at any time, and Ramos said he would come right away (HIM 4600).

27 June 1956: Subject’s personal calling card was found in the address book of Fidel Castro (GASTRO) Ruz, Cuban revolutionary detained in Mexico (MEXI 839 IN 35356 REF: MEXI 825 IN 34161).

22–29 July 1956: Subject served as translator for Soviet delegates to the Eighth International Radiology Congress (HEM 1752).

1 October 1956: Subject and wife requested U.S. transit visas to leave Mexico on 17 October by rail, entering the U.S. via Laredo; to depart New York on 24 October on the Queen Elizabeth (MEXI 231 IN 34833 6 Oct 56).
