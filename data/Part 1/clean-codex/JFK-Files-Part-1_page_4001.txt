He discussed his civic action training program in Miami, which had just started and in which he said he had 32 men enrolled. This was a 3-month program to prepare men for rural organization work of various kinds. He appealed for help with respect to cooperative training, and especially co-operative leadership selection and preparation.

He spoke of his conversation with Col. Peralta in Guatemala and said that after a four-day field trip he reported to the President that:

a. The Army was old-fashioned, had poor ideas, and was ill-equipped to fight guerrilla warfare or to carry out the civic action program, which was urgently necessary.

b. He said he found serious subversion among the Indians, largely through small radios that Cuban-trained Guatemalans were giving to the Indians and which were tuned in only to Radio Cuba. He knew specifically of 5 Guatemalans engaged in this work.

c. He spoke of the danger of Bosa in the mountains and said that Peralta showed his ignorance by brushing Bosa off as a small-time operator with only a handful of men around him. Artime said he had about 25 men with him and added that this was enough to start a lot of trouble. He said he would like to begin something in Guatemala but had no specific suggestions.

He said he had 3 people working in San Jose, Costa Rica, inside what he referred to as “the Cuban community.”

He had one man in Colombia organizing three cooperatives in the Cali area at the request of a local Senator who owned a sugar mill and the Cali newspaper and whom Artime described as a very good human man. He was deeply concerned about the urgent situation in Colombia.

He was leaving shortly for Bolivia, where he had been invited to spend four days by the President.
