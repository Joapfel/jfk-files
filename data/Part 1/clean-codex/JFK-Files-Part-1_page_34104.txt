November 24, 1958

By the Church A. Edgar, Associated Press writer

In [location], Fla., Nov. 24 (AP)—The first notice of the highly favored Havana cigar is immediately received in the economically declared Communist [country], with a former officer of Cuba’s tobacco industry.

Hail Volts, a defector, launches his former [role], first casting mentally of the highest quality ranks of Cuba’s former No. 2 product.

“The industrial trade ended in direct disruption because of cheapest mismanagement,” Volta, former assistant minister of chemistry [with] the state tobacco exporting company, told Newsday testimony.

Cuban cigars are scarcely [available] in Cuba and searching for [them], Volta said after arriving from London, where he gave up his post as trade counselor in the Cuban Embassy.

The quality, elegance and production value of Cuban cigars, Volta said, have fallen victim to things such as:

—Use of leaf tobacco without proper curing.

—Scarcity of materials such as aluminum, woods and cedar boxes used in packaging.

—A high percentage of poorly made cigars.

—Altering of the traditional blends that give character to cigars.

—“Use of inexperienced workers in making top-quality cigars, resulting in production of undersized, oversized or too-heavy cigars.”

Volta reported leaf production had declined from the former average of 45,000 tons annually to 32,000 tons in 1958.

He added that tobacco in the Vuelta region, famous for wrapper, “has declined more than 50 percent in value and quality due to lack of fertilizer and cash.”

He said cigarettes [made] in tobacco [areas] were diverted to Cuba’s No. 1 crop, sugar, while wheat [was used] to produce [what] he called Cuba’s sharply evil [condition].

The exile added that due to low prices paid tobacco growers by the state, “half of the best efficient producers diverted their lands to other crops.”

In addition, the exile, [with the] Revolutionary Unity, reported after a survey that [conditions] at last seriously affected this season’s tobacco crops.
