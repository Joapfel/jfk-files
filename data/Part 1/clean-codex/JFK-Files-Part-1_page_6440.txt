Subject to two conditions in my office on Thursday:

(a) If necessary, painting out various signs and areas whereby it is not possible to obtain mutual benefit to each other. The proposals were that:

(a) I could find any valuable intelligence sources in Europe such that he would be able to contact the overseas personnel in order to pass this information and request their aid in getting the source to this country. I replied that this was impossible, but that CIG's U.S. firm Hollid office, with which he had already been in contact, would be able to forward any documents of candidates for administration under section J of article 110 to be sent in ten for consideration.

(b) I would intercede with the Department of Affairs for LIC's use of some of its quota for entrance of labor and refugees. I replied that I felt that it should deal directly with the Department of Affairs on this.

(c) U.K. should go to Congress and press for censure against the government set in order to further the intelligence potential of a well-existing mass suffrage movement. I replied that this was a policy matter for this government to decide, that O.B., in his effort to maintain integrity, was wary of getting into Congress rival public interests but would unduly hamper his position if called on by congressional committees to do so.
