3. On 17 Nov 52, the Press Attaché called our attention to State Department Control cable 1437, from Bonn, dated 16 Nov 52, a copy of which is attached hereto for your convenience. Basing our request on the information contained in paragraph 1 above and on the attached cable from Bonn, we submitted queries concerning Subject to both UNION and UNBOUND.

4. On 23 Jun 53, we received the following reply from UNBOUND:

Frits (aka Fred) STRIEM, born 31 May 1903 in Berlin, of German origin, the son of Willy and Eartha TUUERAT, acquired French nationality by naturalization on 1 April 1933. On 19 October 1933 in Paris, he married his competitor, Edith STRIEM, born 24 August 1905 in Berlin. They have one child, born 25 May 1937, of French nationality.

SCHOU joined the Foreign Legion in 1940 and was detailed in February 1941. In August 1942, suffering losses, he was interned in a camp in the unoccupied zone. He decided to go with his wife to Switzerland. Upon his arrival in Switzerland, he was arrested for crossing the border and interned in a camp at Kurtigüy, Valais. Later, he was transferred to a labor camp at Sionnoi. During his stay in Switzerland, he allegedly wrote several articles under the pseudonym “Boual” in the Swiss socialist press, notably in the paper “Libero-Stampa.”

In May 1945, he returned to France and settled at Montreux, Switzerland, where he still resides at 211 Avenue Pierre Brassolatte. Since 1945, he has had a legal office at 63 rue d’Hamboutville, Paris 16, where, according to a statement made public by the Swiss government in 1948, it is reported that he held meetings with Konstantin E. KIESELOFF of the Soviet Commercial Agencies (CIC—e.g., LHA 11555) and with WOCHENH of the Soviet Information Bureau. The activity of this legal office is, in fact, almost negligible.

Since 1945, he has also operated an office for the Carmine and Relief Committee (I.R.A.C.), 35 Rond. des Capitaines, Paris. He is equally administrator of the First “N’ank and Stuit” of Antwerp, Belgium.
