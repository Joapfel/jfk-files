2. Ratings on Performance of Specific Duties

Directions:

a. State in the spaces below up to six of the more important specific duties which you consider the most important first. Do not include minor or unimportant duties. Office administration, mail order.

b. Note performance on each specific duty considering only effectiveness in performance of this specific duty.

c. For supervisors, ability to supervise will always be rated as a specific duty; do not rate as superintendents those who supervise a secretary only.

d. Compare in your mind, when possible, the individual being rated with respect to similar level of responsibility. Same duty at a different level.

e. Two individuals with the same job title may be performing different duties. If so, rate them on different duties.

f. Be specific. Examples of the kind of duties that might be rated are: oral briefing; has and uses area knowledge; giving lectures; develops new programs; connecting seminars; analyzes industrial reports; writing technical reports; manages files; connecting external liaisons; operates radio; tipping; coordinates with other offices; taking dictation; writes regulations; supervising; prepares correspondence. Mailroom interactions. Other interactions.
