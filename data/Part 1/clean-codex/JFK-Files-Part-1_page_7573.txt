ISAACS (ISAAOS)

The July 30, 1932 issue of the New York Times, under a Shanghai headline dated 29 July 1932, stated that Harold R. Isaacs, an American citizen, was warned by the U.S. Consul General in Shanghai that the U.S. might withdraw its extraterritorial rights and allow him to be tried in a Chinese court. Isaacs, then editor of the China Forum, had violently attacked Chiang Kai-shek (Ching E-ai Sheh), calling him, among other things, “a butcher.” The article further stated that Isaacs was an associate of Agnes Sedler, a correspondent for American newspapers (DNB 1461).

Preceded the Graniches as editor of The Voice of China. Wrote an article, “I Break With Stalinism,” which gave a candid elucidation of the Communist financial backing of The Voice of China. DCE-4385, 7 February 1952.

One Harold Robert Isaacs was born 13 September 1910; 5'9" tall, medium build, brown eyes, and black wavy hair brushed back. Born in New York City, American citizen. Editor and publisher of the China Forum. Was secretary of the Noulens Defense Committee and member of the Society of Friends of the USSR. Arrived in China from Manila on 10 December 1930 and joined the editorial staff of the Shanghai Evening Post and Mercury. On 12 June 1931, took a trip on the Yangtze with C. F. Glass, suspected Soviet agent, and wrote his fiancée that he had decided to turn “Red” as a result of talking with Glass. The two returned in August and took up residence at No. 12S Weihaiwei Road. Started printing the Forum later. Manager of Level Printing Co., Inc., in New York State and owned 40 percent of its shares. Employed with Havas News Agency. Isaacs married Miss Viola Robinson in September 1932. Lived in Apartment 204, Embankment Building, Shanghai. On 3 January 1934, subject visited Noulens, who was on hunger strike in prison. Subject’s parents arrived in Shanghai on 14 January 1934 and left on 20 February for Marseilles. Subject published an article in New Militant, organ of the New York District Workers’ Party, attacking Stalin. Isaacs was also lecturing on Japan, China, and the USSR and the war crisis in the Far East.

(ZCS-1911, 16-4-2-37, 5 February 1959; Shanghai Municipal Police Files.)
