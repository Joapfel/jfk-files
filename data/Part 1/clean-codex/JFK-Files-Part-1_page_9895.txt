NOTE

1. By the end of the interview, ROWTON and Subject had made an appointment for a meeting that same evening, indicating that it is quite unlikely that Subject will break contact with ROWTON.

2. It was not really possible for the examiner to determine the extent to which Subject believed the story of the IDEN C and the Italian SGSWIRL examiner. The examiner feels that there were no real flaws in the maintenance of this cover and that the cover's only real weakness is its inherently shaky plausibility. Subject appeared to accept the alias used by the examiner and even made passing references to Italy; whether thought it was the most courteous way to handle the situation is not known. He did make several "hurt" references to the injustice of ROWTON and the examiner demanding to use a SGSWIRL on him when he did not know for sure who ROWTON was. On at least one occasion he mentioned that for all he knew ROWTON might even be working for WOFIRM. This reference did not seem strained, although it is conceivable that its purpose was provocation; ROWTON has told the examiner that Subject recently hypothesized out loud that ROWTON might be a Soviet.

3. The principal misfortune of this interview is probably that Subject was not tested on fabrication of contacts with IDEN D's. This was thought to be the most probable area of extensive deception. It should be pointed out, however, that the station already knows that Subject has fabricated such contacts, and the most likely contribution of SGSWIRL would have been merely a further accumulation of examples of this same basic vice. It might also be pointed out that Subject's "reporting" to date has dealt almost exclusively with his own activity; hence his fabrication has been more an irritation than a threat.

Paul I. NAPOLILLI
