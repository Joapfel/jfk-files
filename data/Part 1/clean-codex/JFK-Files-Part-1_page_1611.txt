Thomas L. Roberts

November 1949: Roberts volunteered his services to the Boston FBI; he was not accepted.

January 1950: Roberts left the United States to study in Paris.

November 1950: From November 1950 until November 1955, Roberts reported on security matters to the Boston FBI Office concerning the Labor Youth League.

August 1955: Itkin hired Roberts to work for Delson, Levin & Gordon. Roberts says he was hired to handle maritime matters in litigation.

November 1955: Roberts moved to New York City.

June 1956: Roberts resumed reporting to the FBI. He was a paid informant of the New York FBI office, reporting on the National Lawyers Guild.

Fall 1956: Roberts and others submitted a profit-sharing plan to Delson, Levin & Gordon; it was not accepted.

April 1957: Roberts resigned from Delson, Levin & Gordon to form a new firm, Roberts, Greenbaum & Itkin. There was no mention of the financial arrangement for setting up the new firm.

6 April 1961: Roberts came to Washington and talked with Robert Amory about employment with the CIA, since Amory had been a former professor of Roberts.

Roberts was sent to an overt CIA personnel recruiter and filled out an application for employment with the CIA.

1 June 1961: Roberts took a leave of absence from the firm of Roberts, Greenbaum & Itkin, which was to last from 1 June 1961 through 31 May 1963.
