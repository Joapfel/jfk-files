At about 6:30 p.m., Jan Peraone published in America his book, Czechoslovakia (International University Press, New York). On page 120 you will find a so-called “Bolshevik danger,” a bogey for any actions taking place in Czechoslovakia because the leader was free of every vestige of feudalism and its democracy has been so all-abducing and successful that there remained no class whose dissatisfaction could lead to the acceptance of Communism or a possible remedy for it.

In the meantime, the “National Front” government was composed of Slovak and Czech patriots, mostly Communist Party candidates, then in “labor camps.” Reports were being circulated in the USA about the role of the White General of Communism.

All these gentlemen would like to change their tone.
