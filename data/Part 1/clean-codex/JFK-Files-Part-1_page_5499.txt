BY: 1948

25 May 1937

Case 41-540 (replaced)

Subject: New Developments in Plans of Haitian Banker, Charles Joseph Charles, to Overthrow the Duvalier Government

1. On 11 May 67, Edmond Wilaire, a Haitian national, who operates a 352-ton converted LC as a cargo ship in Port-au-Prince, arrived in New York City. On 12 May 67, in the company of Louis A. Brown, B & B International Corp., New York City, he stated the following. (Letterhead of the Port-au-Prince firm is attached.)

2. President Duvalier has just transferred Captain Harry Tasy of the Presidential Guard to a rural post and replaced him with Major Coliceau, who happens to be a good friend of Clelland Joseph Charles. Charles claims that through Coliceau, he now has access to arms and ammunition in the basement of the palace and is in a position to carry out a coup “in the near future.”

3. After an explosion near the palace in mid-April 1967, Duvalier quietly embarked upon a major shake-up in the military and civil service. He dismissed or transferred a number of Army, Militia, and Tonton Macoute (Ten Ton execute) officers and civil servants, including those who had previously served in the presidential palace. To date, no official announcements have been made concerning these important changes.

4. In addition to Captain Tasy, the following officers lost their positions in Port-au-Prince:

a. Captain Larouche of the Presidential Guard; transferred to an envoy’s post outside the capital.

b. Major Guy Borgo of the Army; Borgo, a confidant of Duvalier (Duvalie), was in charge of radio propaganda. He used to see the president at least twice a day. Borgo was transferred to a town in the northeastern region of Haiti.

c. Paul Vérité of the TIE.
