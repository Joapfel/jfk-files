Section IV. Office of Personality.

This section is provided as an aid in describing the individual. Your description is not favorable or unfavorable in itself but acquires its meaning in relation to a particular job or assignment. The descriptive words are to be interpreted literally.

On the left hand side of the page below are a series of statements that apply to you as well as to most people. On the right hand side of the page are four major categories of descriptions. The best category for each is divided into three small blocks; this is to allow you to make finer distinctions if you so desire. Look at the statement on the left—then check the category on the right which best tells how much the statement applies to the person you are rating. Placing an “X” in the “Not Observed” column means you have no opinion on whether a phrase applies to an individual. Placing an “X” in the “Does Not Apply” column means you do not share the definite opinion that the description is not at all suited to the individual.

Statements and categories:

A. Able to see another’s point of view. Does not apply.

B. Practical. Applies to a reasonable degree.

1. A good reporter of events. Applies to an outstanding degree.

2. Can make decisions on his own when need arising. Applies to an above average degree.

3. Cautious in action. Applies to a reasonable degree.

4. Has initiative. Applies to an above average degree.

5. Unemotional. Applies to an above average degree.

6. Analytic in his thinking. Applies to an outstanding degree.

7. Constantly striving for new knowledge and ideas. Applies to an outstanding degree.
