The French Media

The first issue of the new edition of *La Revolution* was entitled *Revolution, This Is How It Will Be Published in Paris*, by S.A.P.L. Nouvelles Editions Internationales (Internationalizes), 1920, rue Galande—Paris 5°. The first issue of *La Revolution* in September was published at 40, rue Franche-1er—Paris 8°, printed by “La Cité,” Lausanne, Switzerland, but bound by Artigny, France. Then, in the spring, the director, Jacques H. Yenais, had run into financial difficulties. He found cheaper premises for his editorial office at 52, rue Galande—Paris 5°, and aimed to change his printers to Offret-Press.

It turns out that *La Revolution* had been in financial difficulties for several months. The trouble began, apparently, when the French authorities noted copies of issue No. 7 of the French edition of March 1964 because of an article it contained on French overseas territories. Creditors became alarmed, fearing that its publication might be banned, and demanded settlement of their debts, which were then estimated to exceed £10,000.
