15. Correspondence addressed to Subject at Yarbo 11, San Angel, Mexico 33 (Attachment 1).

a) Letter from Kindler Verlag (Vorloge), Lucile-Grahn-Strasse 37, Munich 8, Germany, dated 15 September 1959 and signed by Dr. P. J. Ramaia (RAMAIA). This letter acknowledges receipt of a letter dated 22 August from Subject. The writer has obtained a copy of The Cross and the Arrow and will inform MAIZZ should he decide to publish a West German edition.

b) In a letter dated 17 September 1959 from PREMIER, of Berlin/Stuttgartency, No. 8462 Boulevard, Hollywood 36, California, the writer discusses financing arrangements for a film production.

c) Letter from Adria Scott (SCOTT), 13341 Wyandotte St., Van Nuys, California, dated 22 September 1959, in which the writer encloses a check, signed by Robert A. Scott for 350 dollars, for payment on a loan MAIZZ made to the writer, apparently several years ago.

d) Letter from Jewish Community, 22 East 17th Street, New York 3, N.Y., dated 27 September 1959 and signed by Morris U. Schappers (SCHAPPERS), editor. The writer acknowledges receipt of a contribution Subject had made while abroad. The editor suggests Subject might write for publication in Jewish Currents an article or travel letter about any one of the countries he visited recently.

e) Letter from Science & Society, 30 East 20th Street, New York 3, N.Y., dated 20 September 1959 and signed by T. E. Mattor (MATTOR), secretary. Swedish press cuttings will be held until the return of Henry Muell (MUELL), who will be abroad until the middle of November.

f) Letter from Mr. Harold Liverman (LIVERMAN), Room 1324 Karl Meyer Hall, Cook County Hospital, Chicago 12, Illinois, dated 1 October 1959. The writer makes reference to A Long Lay in a Short Life and the delivery of Simon McKeever and suggests that they may visit Cook County Hospital in Chicago, a place filled with “communist literature.”

g) Circular from the Committee to Secure Justice for Morton Schell (SCHELL), 9th Precinct, New York 10, N.Y., postmarked 7 October 1959 and entitled To the Freedom for Morton Schell, North Steps in Our History.
