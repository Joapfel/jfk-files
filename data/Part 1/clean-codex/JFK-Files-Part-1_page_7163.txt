On February 21, 1966, Pedro Zarate Marmolejo, Commandant of the Customs Guard, Maritime Customs Office, Mazatlan, Sinaloa, advised that his men had been carefully watching the elements of the Soviet whaling fleet currently visiting Mazatlan. They had been alerted to the importation of any propaganda or contraband, but, as yet, nothing had transpired. When the flagship of the Soviet whaling fleet arrived recently, there was no one aboard who spoke Spanish or English, and the captain seemed to be completely lacking in instructions about documents required to enter the harbor. He did not have the necessary papers prepared. A Soviet Embassy official was sent from Mexico City to straighten matters out.

Zarate said that the purpose of the visit of the whaling ships was to take on fresh water and supplies. The crews were also given liberty.

Also enclosed for reference are copies of the crew list of the Soviet vessel “Iskatel,” which visited Mazatlan on January 9, 1966.

In connection with the visit of the “Iskatel,” it is noted that a Soviet Embassy car, believed to contain Soviet Commercial Consul Georgiy Stephanovich Visko and USSR Embassy chauffeur Vasily Stephanovich Miroshnikov, was observed on the highway leading to Mazatlan on January 11, 1966, and was subsequently observed on January 14, 1966, in Mazatlan.

This office is attempting to obtain the crew lists of the Soviet whaling vessels that visited Mazatlan during February 1966 and will make them available when obtained.
