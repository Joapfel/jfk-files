At the end of second semester, students were graded by the following system:

5 — Otlichno (excellent)  
4 — Khorosho (good)  
3 — Posredstvenno (satisfactory)  
2 — Plokho (bad)  
1 — Ochen plokho (very bad)

The first three were passing grades.

In the spring of 1938, I took the final examinations, oral and written, and was issued a diploma. It was a one-page certificate and indicated that I had attended the technicum for four years. It listed the grades I had received in the various subjects and showed that I had graduated as an electromechanic.

All graduates were assigned to various places of employment where they had to stay for not less than two years. This was a form of repayment for the money which the government had spent on their education. I was sent to the Mikha-Tskhakaya M.T.S. which, in 1958, was converted into an R.T.S.

17. Employment History:

1938–1941 and 1946 until defection — Mikha-Tskhakaya M.T.S. (Machine-Tractor Station), later converted into R.T.S. (Repair Technical Station) — Machino-Traktornaya and Remontno-Tekhnicheskaya Stantsiya, respectively. Mikha-Tskhakaya, referred to by the population simply as Tskhakaya, was the rayon center of Tskhakayskiy Rayon, being renamed after the famous Georgian revolutionary Mikha Tskhakaya. The old name of the town was Akhal-Senaki, and the old name of the rayon was Akhal-Senakskiy Rayon. Mikha Tskhakaya was one of the organizers of the Georgian Communist Party. The town had a population of about 10,000 and was located on the railroad line from Samtredia to Sukhumi, the main connection between Tbilisi and Moscow, with a junction to Poti, the Black Sea resort. The town was located between two large tributaries of the Rion River: the Tsivi and Terakhi Rivers. On the latter, a few kilometers from Mikha Tskhakaya, was a small hydroelectric station where I was a part-time employee upon my return from World War II.
