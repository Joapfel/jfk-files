In his analysis of the type of world economic system which should follow the current American controls and floating of the dollar, von Thadden draws heavily from the 23 September press conference of French President Pompidou. He says that the dollar can no longer be considered the single world currency. The productive capacity of Europe makes it necessary that a European reserve currency be available as a second world currency. This cannot be the British pound as many people at one time would have believed, but must be a currency accepted by both France and Germany and administered by a common German-French bank supported by gold. All European states would use this bank for international currency transactions.

Von Thadden views Pompidou's speech as a signal to the United States that the French are not willing to stay permanently under economic pressure from Washington, and as providing an outline of the type of economic order Europe should have in the future.
