January 1970

Report No. 2/2

A. December 1969

Rains, in addition, with Curbelo and Varela, are moving to the new building. Agreed by the time they are arriving, for an O.F. in Rome, Italy. Rains seems concerned about the maintenance of the building, as it has six floors and a boucher. Haverla will have to send an electrician, who is also a blunderer, and that understands something of bureaucracy. They will need three or four other people to act as doormen, watchmen, etc. The wives of those men will be the chairpersons of the building. Curbelo says once that Haverla approve the building may will have to sit down and tell over what difficulties Spanish personnel (with present president). Rains doesn’t want his own personnel under any circumstances. No special of having two dining rooms, one for the General Office and the other for the Muses; here meals prepared outside will be served. The dining room will be completely isolated so that no visitor or invited guest will be able to place anything in the ceiling nor in the walls without being seen. They are going to install a closed-circuit television so that all entertainment and exits will be controlled. Rains says that a Japanese-type television of the kind they are discussing only costs somewhat over $500, and he adds that most likely there is one in the American Embassy.
