Following released locally today: Release from Prison Free Europe.

Embargoed until 11:30 October 14, 1953.

March, July 14, 1953—Thousands of high-altitude balloons carrying messages of hope to the Czechoslovak people were released on the Czech border last night by the Crusade for Freedom.

Carried by strong winds at altitudes up to 30,000 feet, the balloons were expected to reach as far as the industrial center of Gotava, with Pilsen, Prague, Most, and Sokolov as specific targets.

The “balloon barrage”—the second aimed at Czechoslovakia by the Crusade for Freedom—will continue for several days. A total of more than 12,000,000 messages, or one for every person in the country, will be sent.

Crusade for Freedom launched a similar barrage at Czechoslovakia on August 23, 1951, and another at Poland on August 28, 1951. Therefore, the present operation is four times as large and believed to be the biggest ever attempted.

Messages carried by the balloons released last March and today were of three types: a reproduction of a new one-crown note, delivering a plea to oppose the Communist regime; a pamphlet explaining the recent demonstrations in East Germany and the fall of Soviet policy chief Lavrentiy (Lateoni) Beria; and an aluminum coin stamped with the “Freedom Bell” and the slogan “All Czechs and Slovaks (Slaves) for Freedom—all the Free World for the Czechs and Slovaks.”

The balloons used in the operation called “Winds of Freedom” are of two types, a rubber sphere which explodes at high altitudes, scattering its messages over a wide area, and a plastic balloon which descends on the target.
