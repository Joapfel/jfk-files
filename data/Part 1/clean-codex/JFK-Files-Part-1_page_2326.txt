In summer 1945, the Institute of International Relations was established in Moscow, and in July I entered this institute.

In July, my father went to Germany with a group of engineers, and he took me. I received a temporary rank of senior lieutenant, documents, and a uniform.

In 1945–1950, I studied at the institute. In 1946, I became acquainted with a girl, Shishkov Flavia, a student at the medical institute. I was in close relations with this girl; because of the pregnancy, I married her, and she had an abortion. My parents were against the marriage, and we did not live together. We soon divorced. At the end of 1946, I became acquainted with Telegin Augustine and was going to marry her. I received a flat in 1947 at St. Mira, formerly 1st Uecyehckad, 162/174. In November, her father, General Telegin, was arrested, but I married her. The marriage was not successful. I found out about her close relations with her brother, and a girl child was born with pathological changes. I was not the father of this child. After that, I broke with her, and we lived separately from the end of 1948 to the beginning of 1949.

In spring 1950, before the state examinations at the institute, a commission was working on the future employment of the students in my fifth course. I expressed a wish to work in any military organization.
