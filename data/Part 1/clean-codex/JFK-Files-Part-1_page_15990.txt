POWDER KEG AT POWDER SPRINGS

The flamboyant son of a Cossack colonel, Mitchell WerBell III, has been involved in several Caribbean adventures and misadventures. Now, as the Bahamas nears independence, the wealthy arms dealer has his sights set on Abaco . . .

By William R. Amlong

“Do I look like a guy who has to see himself as a soldier of fortune?”

Mitchell Livingston WerBell III is the son of a cavalry colonel in the Russian Imperial Army. He has stayed, more or less, in the family business: bedeviling Bolsheviks (Boishevis). And now he sees himself not as some ragtag gunrunner, but as a very substantial figure in the shaping of world affairs through their enterprise and politics.

His disclaimer about not looking like a soldier of fortune, however, doesn't quite make us comfortable, for that is indeed why Mitch WerBell lives—or at least what a montage of James Cagney, Al Guzman and Walter Moray would look like. There's the walrus mustache at the Stetson bush jacket with the shoulder patch of the First Airborne Division of the Army of the Republic of Vietnam, a cigarette and a swagger stick are sure to be. The overall impression is that he has just stepped out of the British officers' mess in old Burma (Borma).
