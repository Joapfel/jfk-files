Special Report

I. Subject

Ivan Gavrilovich Alferiev was born in Moscow (U.S.S.R.) on 20 January 1928, diplomatic passport No. 091.477. He is married to Rosa Alekseevna Alferieva, born in Moscow on 20 September 1934, diplomatic passport No. 019.468. The subject is assigned as 2nd Counsellor of the U.S.S.R. Embassy in Buenos Aires. He arrived in this country on 27 January 1968, taking up residence at Uruguay 117b, 5th Floor, Apt. “A,” in the Federal Capital, telephone 41-5405, living at said address until the 6th or 7th of October 1969, when he moved to the Posadas Club, residence for the officers of the Soviet representation.

Alferiev had been in Argentina for a short time during September 1958; in Havana in September 1959; in Quito in January and February 1962; and in Tijuana in September 1963. He was representative of Pravda (Fravda) in Mexico during 1960 and 1964.

Since the subject lived separately from the rest of the Embassy personnel, his mission based on information was thus made easier.

It should be pointed out that during his residence on Uruguay, he was kept under surveillance through a technical penetration carried out by this Section.

As a result of the exploitation of these lines, as well as the “ASADO” case (summer home) and information from the “LORD” case, certain details concerning his personality, private life, and contacts have been ascertained.

Through thorough study of the above-mentioned sources, it is apparent that the Alferievs have certain differences, during which he is the object of vituperation from his wife Rosa (Ross), which Ivan thoroughly ignores, to the great exasperation of his wife.

II. Personality of Ivan Alferiev

He is an introvert, clever, cautious, and distrustful. He knows how to hide his feelings and has excellent self-control, showing violent feelings on very rare occasions. (See Section IV.)

With relation to his cultural level, nothing much is known, as he has never been heard speaking on themes of general culture. When he speaks at social gatherings, he is joking, using double-meaning phrases, usually funny.
