However, it seemed reasonable that Mr. Visdanu (Visدانu) might make the move before going to London, where he would have further consultations with the King. Mr. Visdanu (Visدانu) and Censoum were reported to have come to some basic agreements for the purpose of reaching an understanding between the two factors. Advice was given that General Badaciu would be brought into the consultations eventually.

Mr. More advised of the opinion held by the National Council division of RFF that the Russians should avoid bringing the King into the negotiations, and rather than an agreement in the form of a fait accompli, should present it to the King for his approval.

Mr. Campbell interjected that he had an appointment at that time with Mr. Visdanu (Visدانu), and that in light of this information, he would advise that gentleman not to try to pull any contentious schemes which might disrupt the efforts so far taken. Mr. Barbour requested that Mr. Visdanu (Visدانu) be informed, in strong terms, to avoid further intrigue or unnecessary protracted discussions. In other words, “read him the riot act.”

Hungarian Affairs

Miss Augustin advised the meeting that it had been necessary to make some drastic changes among the Hungarian personnel working for Radio Free Europe in New York, as their output was considered to be inadequate. She advised that efforts were being made to reestablish the Hungarian desk on a more effective basis.

Bulgarian Affairs

Mr. Augustin advised that Mr. Dimitrov was departing for Europe to join the European Movement Conference, and that he was being obliged to come to RFF headquarters to pick up his check for the purpose of the trip.
