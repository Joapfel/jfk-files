Africa, Latin America, Asia Revolution

The following information was received in the first half of October 1964 from a source in close touch with Richard Gibson (CIBSOH), the former editor of “Africa, Latin America, Asia Revolution.”

2. Gibson (CIBSOH) was aware that a number of people had thought that the Chinese Communists had provided some financial backing for “Africa, Latin America, Asia Revolution,” but this was true only to the extent that the Chinese had a standing order for 3,000 copies of each issue of the English edition. If the Chinese had given more general financial assistance, Verges, the publication’s director, would not have had to contend with the present financial difficulties and possible bankruptcy.

3. Verges had had access to a relatively large sum of money which had been held in Switzerland from the time of the Algerian war of independence. When this had dried up, Verges had been unable to repay it. Reports that Verges had stolen this money were not true.

4. The publication owed 4,000 Swiss francs to a businessman, possibly Swiss, named Kessi and 30,000 francs to the French printers. In Gibson’s (CIBSOH’s) view, too much money had been spent on maintaining prestige offices at 40 rue François Ier, Paris.

5. When “Africa, Latin America, Asia Revolution” was banned in Switzerland, all except 100 copies of issue No. 12 were seized by the Swiss authorities. These 100 copies had already been sent to Gibson (CIBSOH).

6. The French edition “Revolution” has not appeared since August 1964.

16 October 1964.

Comment 1) Para. 4

Source could not provide any further particulars about Kessi.
