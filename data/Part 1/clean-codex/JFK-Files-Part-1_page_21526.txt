In summer 1945 there was created the Institute of International Relations in Moscow, and in July I entered this institute.

In July my father went to Germany with the group of engineers, and he took me. I received a temporary rank of senior lieutenant, documents, and a uniform.

In 1945–1950 I studied at the institute. In 1946 I became acquainted with a girl, Shishkov FLAVIA, a student of the medicine institute. I was in close relations with this girl; because of the pregnancy I married her, and she had an abortion. My parents were against the marriage, and we did not live together; we soon divorced. At the end of 1946 I became acquainted with Telegin AUGUSTINE and was going to marry her. I received a flat in 1947 (St. Mira—former 1st Uecyehckad, 162/174). In November her father, General TELEGIN, was arrested, but I married her. The marriage was not successful. I found out about her close relations with her brother, and the child-girl was born with pathological changes. I was not the father of this child. After that I broke with her, and we lived separately (end of 1948–beginning of 1949).

In spring 1950, before the state exams at the institute, a commission was working that was dealing with the future work of the students of my fifth course. I expressed a wish to work in any military organization.
