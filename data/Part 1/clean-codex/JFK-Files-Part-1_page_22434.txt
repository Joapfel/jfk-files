H. Evaluation

The conclusion that KOSENKO is on a KGB mission could carry damaging implications for the American intelligence sources who have supported his bona fides. Unless their statements on KOSENKO can be convincingly explained as innocent repetition of misinformation spread by the KGB within the Soviet services, these sources might be concluded to have been deliberately misleading either as promoters of their own personal interests or as parties to a KGB conspiracy.

Regarding POLITSYN, the opinion of CIA is that he purposefully gave false support for KOSENKO in an attempt to make his opinions more authoritative. This is not a satisfactory explanation for the remarks on KOSENKO by illegible; however, there seem to be no personal interests which their support of KOSENKO's bona fides might have served. The choice thus seems to lie between these three officers being genuine sources of American intelligence but submitting channels of KGB misinformation and, on the other hand, one or more of them being in league with the KGB.

The possibility that illegible are under KGB control was tested further in the context of the KOSENKO operation by reviewing parallels in their reporting and his, and general correlations that appear from one case to another. At the same time, the KOSENKO connections were shown for comparative purposes. Here nothing was found that would eliminate illegible from consideration as possibly being KGB provocation agents.

This examination against the conclusion that KOSENKO is under KGB control has brought the bona fides of illegible into serious question. If KOSENKO is a dispatched agent of the KGB, these other sources seem also to be.
