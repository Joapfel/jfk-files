The source noted that the telegram never reached Consul Hill because Diaz tore it up after the incident in Dr. Reid's house.

The source commented that the interrogators themselves did not believe at this time that the U.S. Department of State was in any way involved with the plot. They thought the disclosures concerning the alleged involvement was simply a method by which Amiama (Amiana) induced General Román to give his approval to the plot. It was the interrogators' opinion at that time that if the North Americans had been involved in this plot, the plot would have been much better organized and the coup would have succeeded.

By the latter part of June 1961, the investigation had pretty well established the identities of all the individuals involved in the plot, many of whom had been arrested and interrogated. The only ones not accounted for were Luis Amiama (Amiana), Tió (Tio), and Antonio Imbert Barrera (Inbert Barreras). As previously related, General Juan Tomás Díaz, Antonio de la Maza, and Lieutenant Amado (Azado) García Guerrero were killed when resisting arrest. At this point the source related that if General Diaz, Lieutenant Garcia, de la Maza, Amiama, and Imbert had been captured and made to talk, it is certain that many more prisoners would have been taken.

As far as the source is aware, the only individuals who were arrested as a result of the assassination of Trujillo and brought before the courts were Tavares and General Román. As far as the source is aware, no other individual arrested in connection with this plot was brought before any Dominican court. “Wimpy,” the North American, was to have been brought before a court to be deported; however, Ramírez ordered that he was not to be molested in any way, and as far as the source is aware, no action was taken against “Wimpy.”
