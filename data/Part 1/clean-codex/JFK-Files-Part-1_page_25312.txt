20 May 1956: Rodolfo (Kodolfo) KANU was called by the Subject and told, “We have found the books and material you requested (the Soviet foreign policy).” LEONOV said he would take the books to HEWRO the next day. HEWRO had been active in the Mexican Diplomatic Service for many years, at one time being Chargé of the Mexican Legation in Barrow, Poland. He was then about 78 and inactive in government, but did write articles for the newspaper Excelsior (HEX: 3736 5).

6 January 1956: The daughter of General Heriberto Jara spoke with the Subject at the Soviet Embassy, saying she had been told that he was the person who could inform her when she should come by to pick up translations of the “clinical documents” relative to herself and her father. The Subject said they were having some trouble getting the documents translated because of the medical terms, but said they should be ready in about five days (HENA 4200).

1 February 1956: The Subject called Velia Jara de NIETO, daughter of General JARA, and told her that translations of her father’s clinical history had been made from Russian into Spanish. Mrs. NIETO said she would visit the Embassy the next day and ask LEONOV (LEO NOV) for the materials (HENA 4410).

24 February 1956: One Samuel RAMOS contacted the Subject, who acted as secretary to the Soviet Ambassador in Mexico. RAMOS told LEONOV, “We are not going to meet at my house this afternoon, but at SALGO’S, which is at Insurgentes 429-51, at the usual hour.” The Subject replied that he thought he could get to the meeting at 1900 hours and sent greetings “to the boys.” RAMOS said, “We have some books we want to order through LEDADV” (HENA 4336).

2 March 1956: Samuel RAMOS left a message for the Subject that “if he has time I hope he will come to my house today” (HENA 4600).

7 March 1956: Samuel RAMOS called the Embassy and asked for data on the possibility of staging an international chess match in Mexico. In this conversation it was indicated that RAMOS was in the academic line of work. He stated that he was “back in classes” and that meetings could no longer be held in his home. He told LEONOV that a tournament featuring a person named “POMMAHD” would begin Monday, 12 March, and invited the Subject to see some of it. LEONOV said he would attend if he had time. RAMOS then asked when he could see LEONOV to give him a list of books “we want.” LEONOV told him to come to the Embassy at any time, and RAMOS said he would come right away (HENA 4600).

27 June 1956: The Subject’s personal calling card was found in the address book of Fidel CASTRO (GASTRO), Cuban revolutionary detailed in Mexico (EXI 839 IN 85366 REP: MEXI 826 IN 84161).

22–29 July 1956: The Subject served as translator for Soviet delegates to the Eighth International Radiology Congress (HEM 1752).

1 October 1956: The Subject and his wife requested U.S. transit visas to leave Mexico on 17 October by rail, entering the U.S. via Laredo; to depart New York on 24 October on the Queen Elizabeth (MEXI 231 IN 34835, 6 October 1956).
