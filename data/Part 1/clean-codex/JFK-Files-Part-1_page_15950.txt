2. Meeting 1-400, Norm Roberts, Mrs. Geery, and Harlowe Duffin.

Mr. Duffin's viewpoint was that he could not prevent us from sending WIROGUE to any of the countries, but did feel some other division besides us should assume the responsibility in case of a flap, as he pointed out that the stations were small enough so it would not be difficult for a fairly clever individual to identify the case and probably the case officers in a station. In this case, should WIROGUE attempt to apply pressure through such knowledge, Mr. Duffin wished someone besides a field officer to take WIROGUE by the scruff of the neck and tell him to move on.

I insisted, however, that no matter how heavy the pressure became, all any field officer could do was to make a denial, plausible or otherwise.

Mrs. Geery suggested that perhaps SR, since they had originally recruited WIROGUE, should assume such responsibility. (She is hardly realistic, but anything is possible.)

A second suggestion made by Mr. Duffin is worth further consideration. He stated that WH has had continual difficulty in establishing contract agents in business throughout SA. However, these countries tended to be much more receptive to people planning agricultural pursuits, and he felt resettlement and the possibility for livelihood would be much greater should WIROGUE approach the country or countries of his choice with some agricultural proposition.

I remember WIROGUE mentioned that he had investigated the possibility of chicken farming in the Congo. Such an approach in SA might (a) make him more acceptable as an immigrant to that country and (b) provide a long-term basis to make him self-supporting.

I did promise to keep Mr. Duffin and Mrs. Geery informed of any further actions taken concerning WIROGUE which would affect their area. It was also at least strongly implied that either Colombia (Columbia) or Brazil might be the best areas, though the final choice essentially rests on what country WIROGUE can enter.
