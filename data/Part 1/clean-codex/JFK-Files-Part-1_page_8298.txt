LOGINOV's general line of conversation was that the essential thing in life is understanding, and to be able to understand others one must get to know them; this in turn calls for having as many friends as possible. The same applies to countries and nations. Before leaving the party, LOGINOV stated that he would like to become friendly with Subject and asked him if he would be willing to meet with him. Subject agreed, and they met the next Friday at the Tecali Restaurant.

b. The conversation at the Tecali was to an extent dealing with the theme of friendship and understanding, with added emphasis that if there was understanding and, consequently, friendship between the U.S. and Soviet Union, we would be living in a much better world. Subject stated his agreement with LOGINOV's (LOG's) views. In a further part of the conversation, LOGINOV was interested in knowing whether Subject had many friends in Mexico. Subject claims to have stated that he had many good friends here. (Note: It is quite likely that he bragged about the number of influential friends he had here.) At the end, LOGINOV told Subject that he would like to see him again, and Subject asked him to come to his house. According to Subject, LOGINOV asked whether the fact of receiving a Soviet official would compromise Subject or lead him into some kind of trouble. Subject assured him that receiving a Soviet or any foreign official could not affect him in any way. Then LOGINOV asked whether they would be alone, and Subject answered affirmatively. (Note: At this point Subject indicated that he received LOGINOV at the apartment at the Icithitz 297.) Finally, LOGINOV agreed to come to Subject's apartment.
