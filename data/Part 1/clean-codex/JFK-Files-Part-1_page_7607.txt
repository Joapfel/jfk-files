11. No data. Apparently has limited income. He has no servant, no TV, no telephone.

12. Loonova. Val sounded somewhat cool about the Loonovs. Naturally he knows them, but doesn't seem to consider them friends.

13. Personality. Does not smoke. He did before.

14. U.S. trip. Val is going to Moscow in July. He wants to spend 2–3 days in New York. He said he had read much about New York but wanted to see it for himself.

April 23, 1967, p.m.

1. Contact. Upon leaving the club, we saw and greeted Val at poolside watching his son swim. He reminded me about our Wednesday date.

April 26, 1967, 1600 hours

1. Contact. Our tennis date.

2. Information.

A. By access? Val asked if I had occasion to go to the U.S. embassy and if I knew anyone in the embassy. I answered no to both.

B. FBI. Val was still concerned about my innocence and lack of fear in acting soon with a Soviet. I joked about not seeing any FBI in the area. He blushed and said he really knew that there were very many FBI agents here.

C. Embassy telephone. Val requested that I not use his embassy phone number on his calling card. He explained that he is all over the embassy and that it is difficult to locate him.

D. American girls. Val asked if I knew any American girls here. No suggested I bring two for our tennis date. He wants to meet U.S. girls to practice his English. He said that in one year he would fail to do so.

This is what a Soviet would say, and that is how the U.S. ambassador here speaks to an American girl.

Goodnight.
