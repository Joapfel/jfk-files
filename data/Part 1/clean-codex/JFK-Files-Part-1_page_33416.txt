Central Bank devalued the escudo by some 60% in an effort to curb import demand and ease pressures on the country's meager foreign exchange reserves. As a result, Chile's cost of living jumped 88% in October, and other price rises pushed inflation to some 770% for the year.

4. The junta has now agreed to follow the stabilization program proposed by an IMF team which visited Chile in late 1979. This program calls for tighter federal spending and for limiting inflation to 100% in 1974. This IMF program is feasible but may be somewhat over-optimistic, especially since the world-wide energy crisis will soon force Chile to raise fuel prices, thus exacerbating the problem of controlling inflation. Adherence to the IMF proposal has, however, improved Chile's credit rating, as has the signing of a bilateral agreement with the United States on Chile's 1971–1972 debt. Chile is thus in a relatively good position for the opening of the Paris Club talks in February. The massive $4 billion foreign debt, however, will cause Chile's balance-of-payments problems for some time.
