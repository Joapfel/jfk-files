The commander-in-chief of 4 Fleet was Admiral Golovko H. G. The chief of my navy intelligence point was Captain Ryzantsev. There were officers: Vingradlov, Bulakh, Demchenko, and others, with sergeants and privates.

Two days before my arrival, this navy intelligence point was transferred from East Germany to the Soviet Union. In Germany, the work of the point was connected with agents’ work; now there was another task: preparation of an agent-observation post in case of a new war, with three or four persons at each post. It was absolutely monkey business.

At the end of December, I took leave, giving my illness as the reason, but having in mind a transfer to another place.

In mid-March 1953, I began to work in the 2nd Chief Directorate of Kyb (KGB; former MOD).
