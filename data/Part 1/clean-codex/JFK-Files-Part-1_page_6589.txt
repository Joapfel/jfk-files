The two bombas, said both the students, anticipated the end, ... of the Soviet ... in early November, despite official claims that the puppet government was negotiating for its victory.

The students prepared for the second round of fighting with small arms collected from the military army and the factories in Budapest. One major source of supply, Orleyn, was a “so-called lamp factory” from which the workers themselves took the arms the freedom fighters needed.

In those five days between November 4 and 6, Orleyn recalled, the sheer might of well over a thousand Soviet tanks turned the tide. He said, “No fighters, no matter how brave, can win out with small arms against heavy armor.”

By November 10, the two freedom fighters and his classmates discovered that the Soviets had taken over many of the railroad stations. He said long experience with the Communists convinced them that deportations had begun, and he and a friend decided to try to escape.

Orleyn said this was a difficult decision for him because of his family. It was especially hard to say goodbye to his father since Orleyn’s two brothers had also fled Hungary in 1947—one to Bolivia and the other to Australia. Thomas’s father was formerly a world champion skilled shooter, a pre-war field marshal in the Hungarian Army, and a leader in the non-Communist ... Party.
