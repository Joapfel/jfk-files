Actual reality clearly made this known to us. Continental opinion supported the Castro regime; Latin American youth beheld Fidel Castro as the legendary leader who would raise the Latin American people toward their total emancipation and development. Little or nothing had been done to face this skillful propaganda. The possibilities of opening a battle front against Castro in the Latin American countries—the fundamental point of our plan—began to be taken into consideration. The first attempts were:

1. In May 1961, our members Zoila Díaz, Marta Klena Rosentrages and Raúl González (Conzales) Simón were sent to São Paulo, Brazil, and stayed there for one month carrying out propaganda work.

2. Immediately after Alberto Müller, Secretary-General of the DRE, was captured, a delegation composed of Jorge Dorticés (Dortices), Rogelio Helu and Joaquín Pérez Ebedriles traveled through Brazil, Uruguay, Argentina and Chile, working to avoid the execution of our Secretary-General by the Castro regime. The work it accomplished, together with the work accomplished in other countries, turned public opinion toward our cause. When the delegation arrived at Montevideo, Uruguay, the “Congresso por la Libertad y la Democracia” was being held in that city, and the delegation was invited to take part in it, holding an outstanding posture in its activities.

3. Immediately after the trial of our Secretary-General, Alberto Müller, and 32 other prisoners with him, one of the strongest and most successful campaigns we had accomplished was carried out. Four delegations were urgently sent to Latin America, organized as follows:

Brazil — Raúl González Simón, Mário García, José Basulto.

Chile — Nelson Alemar, Alejandro Portes, Rafael Oller.

Argentina — Rogelio Helu, Jorge Dortices.

Uruguay — Eduardo Menéz, René Morato, Ramón Machado (Macbado).

At the same time, cables were sent from our main office in Miami to all the governments of the continent, student unions and civic organizations, making use of the contacts we had made by mail from our beginnings. The results of the campaign were really astonishing. All the governments of the continent pleaded in favor of Müller and the other prisoners; almost all the student unions and countless civic organizations did the same. Public demonstrations were held in Mexico, Panama, Chile, Venezuela, Ecuador and Peru. Stones were thrown at the Cuban Embassy in Bolivia. All the liceos (high schools) in Montevideo, Uruguay, went on strike on the day of the trial. The Federación Estudiantil Universitaria (FEUZ) in Honduras also went on strike for one day. Through the streets of San José, Costa Rica, a great student demonstration was held, and the Federación Estudiantil Universitaria (FEUER) proposed exchanging Alberto Müller for one tractor.

The work performed was so effective that Doctor (Braitor) Ernesto Che Guevara, in his interview with President Jânio Quadros during his stop in Brazil while returning from the Conferencia Interamericana Económica y Social (CIES), was asked by the Brazilian government to spare the lives of Müller and the other prisoners.
