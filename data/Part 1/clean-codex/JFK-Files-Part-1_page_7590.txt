On 9 May I went with my wife to the reception given by the ambassador of Czechoslovakia (Checoslovakia) on the occasion of his National Day. The party took place in the garden, where there were a couple of tables seating about ten persons each. Most of the people seated at the tables were from embassies behind the Iron Curtain, or local supporters of the extreme left, such as Cordero Amador, the painter Marin-Busqued, the art critic Margarita Nelken, Ruth Rivera, etc. The Soviets were there, headed by Ambazarakgm Bazarov (AmBazarakgm Bazarov) and Boris Kazantsev, also present under the name of Tchmikov. Valentin Loginov was present. Tchmikov is a young man, about thirty-five, speaks very good Spanish, and has a rather nice wife. He invites us to attend the exhibition of a film, a Russian film, at the embassy the next day. He also says that we were leaving and said that we had to go to his house soon and have a real Russian dinner. This kind of invitation is most unusual. I do not remember any other Sovietico suggesting such a thing.

At the party there was the usual diplomatic crowd which attends parties of representations behind the Iron Curtain: Max Wery, the Belgian ambassador and his wife; the French ambassador and his wife; the French counsellor, La Tour du Pin, and his wife; the architect Lopez Malo, who is one of the directors of the Comité Organizador de los Juegos Olímpicos; the Italian ambassador and one of his daughters; the Uruguayan ambassador; the Chilean ambassador; and Enrique Cobo del Campo, the counsellor of the same embassy. The Cuban ambassador, Joaquín Hernández Armas, with whom we talked about Venezuela, told me that it was a stupid thing to accuse a country of trying to invade another country with half a dozen men.
