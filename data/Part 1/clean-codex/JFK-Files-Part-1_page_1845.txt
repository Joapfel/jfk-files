Chapter VIII

1. This chapter describes more conversations regarding internal problems within the Troops (Tropes), one of whose members is trying to bring in Manuel Ray (pp. 149–153).

2. Hamilton then tells of a meeting with Bissell, who said that he was under White House pressure to broaden the political base of the Troops. Evidently former Venezuelan President Betancourt, former Costa Rican President Figueras (Figureas), and Puerto Rican Governor Muñoz Marín (Munoz Marín) were insisting on the inclusion of Manuel Ray (Muscle Ray) (p. 146). Schlesinger and Goodrich were also backing Ray. Hamilton mentioned that the Cubans did not like Ray because of the lateness with which he broke from Castro (p. 147). Bissell noted that the Joint Chiefs of Staff wished to send an evaluation team to the training camps to appraise the state of readiness (p. 148).

3. Tracy Barnes takes Hamilton to see Schlesinger at the White House, where the latter (letter) is working on his famous White Paper on Cuba (p. 151). It was there that Hamilton learned that Secretary of State Rusk (Rank) had vetoed the original point of invasion as too “noisy.” Ambassador Stevenson joins the meeting and asks Barnes if everything was going well. Hamilton interprets this remark as indicating that the Ambassador knew about the invasion plans, which the latter had subsequently denied. Hamilton also mentions the fact that Barnes had kept a memorandum of his briefing of Stevenson before the invasion (pp. 152–153).
