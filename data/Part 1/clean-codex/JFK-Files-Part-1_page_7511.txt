Shanghai Municipal Files, filed 16-4-2-37.

Isaacs, Harold Robert.

Born 13 September 1910, NYC. Nationality: American.

Description: Height 5'9". Build: medium. Hair: black, wavy, brushed back. Eyes: brown.

Documentation:

In his 30s, was traveling on passport No. 177026.

Arrived in China from Manila on 10 December 1930. Upon arrival, joined the editorial staff of the Shanghai Evening Post and Mercury.

Was a secretary of the Houlens Defense Committee and a member of the Society of Friends of the UCRB.

On 12 June 1931, Isaacs and his friend Glass took a trip up the Yangtze. During this trip, Isaacs wrote his fiancée, Viola Robinson, that he had decided to stay “in China” as a result of conversations with Glass.

The two returned to Shanghai in August 1931 and took up residence at 128 Weihaiwei Road.

Isaacs later started and became the editor and publisher of the China Forum, which at first was job-printed but from October 1932 on was printed by the Level Publishing Co., Inc., 33 Lane 478, Tiandong Road, Shanghai. Isaacs openly stated he was manager of the printing company and owned 40% of its shares.

On 7 August 1933, Isaacs and his wife, Viola Robinson, whom he had married in September 1932, left Shanghai for Japan on the SS Nagasaki Maru. On 16 August, they proceeded to Japan, returning to Shanghai on 26 August.

In addition to his other activities, Isaacs was also employed with the Havantime Agency. He severed connection with this agency around December 1933.

In December 1933, Isaacs was in Poochow to gather firsthand information regarding the newly formed “People’s Revolutionary Government of the Republic of China.”

In the spring of 1934, Isaacs and his wife left for Tianjin en route to Peking, at which city he resided at 2 Tung Fu Chia Tao, East City. Peking mailing address was Mr. Huimiang, Grand Hotel.
