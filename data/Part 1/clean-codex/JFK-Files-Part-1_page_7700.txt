Ralph Wiegandt (WIEGANDT) (P-225-1), German who came to Mexico in 1955 and has a paint factory, member of the French Club.

On 13 March 1967, Mrs. Ruth Wiegandt (WIEGANDT), using the familiar form of address with Subject, invited Subject and his wife to Sunday dinner (1). In April 1967, it was reported that Subject was quite friendly with the Wiegandts (WIEGANDTs) at the French Club (Franch Club) (15).

Douglas H. Wiltshire (WILTSHIRE), American employed with SERVISA Travel Agency.

On 10 May 1967, Wiltshire (WILTSHIRE) planned to pick up some passports from Subject at the Soviet Embassy and mentioned that he would like to invite Subject to his house (1).

Maxx Michael Zarebski (ZAREBSKI) (P-8777, 201-744856), businessman from Cuernavaca, Canadian citizen.

On 11 October 1966, Subject reminded the Polish Consul, Jakowiec (JAKOWIEC), that Zarebski (ZAREBSKI) was going to sell some of his items to the Soviets. On 13 October 1966, Jakowiec (JAKOWIEC) said he could introduce Subject to Zarebski (ZAREBSKI), or Subject could call him directly (1). Subject had originally met Zarebski (ZAREBSKI) at a Polish Embassy reception on 14 December 1965, and they finally got together at another on 4 October 1966, and they finally did get together on 12 January 1967.

Subject asked Zarebski (ZAREBSKI) to help the Soviets find a house for a dacha in Cuernavaca, which he eventually did. On 24 February 1967, Subject visited Zarebski's (ZAREBSKI's) factory and had lunch with him in Cuernavaca. On 20 May 1967, Subject and Boris Aleksandrovich Chmykhov (CHMYKHOV) visited Zarebski (ZAREBSKI) in connection with the dacha, and on 15 July 1967, Subject and Chmykhov (CHMYKHOV) visited the factory (19). Comment: Subject and Chmykhov (CHMYKHOV) had known each other before in Cuba, and both had Mestkom responsibilities for the dacha, so their going to Cuernavaca together is not remarkable, although Chmykhov (CHMYKHOV) is a GRU officer.
