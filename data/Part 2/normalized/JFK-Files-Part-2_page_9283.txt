DE T-1 advised on April 21, 1971, that the Free Breakfast for School Children Program is currently operating five days a week at the following locations:

611 Continental (30 children) 157 Collingwood (15 children) St. Leo's Church (15 children)

DE T-1 advised on April 21, 1971, that the Detroit NCCF scheduled a free community dinner in front of 611 Continen- tal, Detroit, on Saturday, April 24, 1971, at 5:30 p.m. Tables will be set up to serve the food buffet style and there is to be no gathering at the dinner as seating will not be provided.

DE T-3 advised on April 26, 1971, that there were not over 30 people at the NCCF dinner on April 24, 1971, at any one time, and that no incidents occurred,

DE T-1 advised on April 27, 1971, that the NCCF prepared servings of food for about 400 people for the dinner on April 24, 1971, and that all of the food was passed out.

DE T-24 advised on April 28, 1971, that someone is assigned to a desk facing the front door of 2009 Albert, Flint, Michigan, on a daily basis. A loaded M-1 is kept at the desk to prevent anyone from entering the house who is not supposed to. Posters have been removed from some first floor windows so they can see anyone approaching the house. These measures were in- stituted due to the raid by the police in March, 1971, which caused the NCCF in Flint to loose face as the police took over so easily.

III. TEACHING OF THE REVOLUTIONARY PROGRAM

DE T-1 advised on April 24, 1971, that political education classes are held at 611 Continental, Detroit, every Sunday and that the classes consist of reading from and discussing articles appearing in "The Black Panther" newspaper, particularly the center section.

DE T-24 advised on April 28, 1971, that only nine people, mainly young kids, showed up for the political education class on April 18, 1971. Attendance at the classes have fallen off sharply since the police raid in March, 1971.
