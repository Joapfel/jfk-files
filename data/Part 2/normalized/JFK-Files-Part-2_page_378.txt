3. Subject, in his political outlook, is left of center, with a history of revolutionary activity in Cuba from 1961 until his return to Mexico in 1964. Subject has insisted to Rowton that, because of his (existentialist) philosophy, he has never permitted himself to join any organization, including the communist party, although he has given moral and material support to revolutionary movements both in Cuba and in Mexico. Subject has confessed his disillusionment with many of the revolutionary leaders in Cuba and with all of the Mexican leaders, remarking that sooner or later "the real" revolution will come to Mexico to eliminate the corruption and injustice that prevails throughout the country. Subject, however, feels that in the meantime he must live under a more practical philosophy of daily life and is willing to make the necessary compromises to assist Rowton in his business affairs. Subject has shown no reluctance to exploit his friends, and even his wife, to be of specific service to Rowton, and has, on numerous occasions, volunteered to do so with Rowton's approval. This appears to Rowton to be a reflection of the existentialist conviction that his philosophy gives him freedom to choose without dependency, obligation or justification.

5. In summation Rowton feels that Subject is excellent potential agent material, both because of his background and experience as well as his direct access to targets of interest. The problems inherent in his philosophical and psychological make-up will continue to be taken into consideration in developing Subject's full potential. The obvious disadvantages of Subject's unwitting status vis-a-vis [Subject] can be overcome by careful handling of Subject and increased control over him in the future.
