CONCLUSIONS

5. Unless the United States intervene militarily, the Castro government will become more firmly entrenched as its efforts and ability to spread international communism will increase.

6. The United States should intervene militarily in Cuba and could: (a) engineer provocation incidents ostensibly perpetrated by the Castro regime or (b)oment a revolt in Cuba.

7. Engineered provocations would provide greater advantages in control, timing, simplicity and security than would a fomented revolt. It would be most difficult to generate a widespread effective revolt. However, the staging of a local uprising as a contrived incident should not be precluded.

The United States should:

Initiate a coordinated program to create a pretext for overt US military intervention in Cuba.

In support of the above, undertake a flexibly phased effort to build Cuban resistance potential, commencing with the intensification of intelligence collection and sabotage in Cuba, and the creation of a framework for guerrilla activity.

At a propitious time launch appropriate military action to remove the Castro communist government.

9. It would appear necessary that one governmental organization be given the primary responsibility for developing a national plan.

10. The Joint Chiefs of Staff should participate in the development of this national plan.

11. The broad concepts herein and the views of the Joint Chiefs of Staff should be forwarded to the Special Group (5412 Committee) for consideration under the provisions of NSAM-57.
