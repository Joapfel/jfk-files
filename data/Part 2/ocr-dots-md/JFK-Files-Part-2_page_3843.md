15 August 1968

SUBJECT: NAY, Hoke Smith

Subject was granted a covert Security Approval on 22 April 1958 for unwitting use with Project FJINDULGE. Subject was not directly employed by FJINDULGE but was to be used in connection with his position with a private firm which handled work for the Project. At the time of the clearance Subject was employed by Tobis and Company, a public relations firm in Charleston, South Carolina.

A newspaper clipping from the New Orleans STATE-ITEM for 26 May 1967 reported how a letter written by fugitive witness Golden NOVEL (GARRISON investigation into the assassination of former President KENNEDY) came into the hands of Hoke MAY, STATE-ITEM reporter. A copy of this clipping is attached.

Subject was born 11 April 1930 in DeKalb County, Georgia. He attended the University of Georgia, Athens, Georgia, September-December 1947 and again from March-June 1948 but withdrew because of poor scholarship. He was employed as an office boy in the news department of the NEW YORK TIMES in New York City from October 1948 to March 1949. Other employments are as follows: SAVANNAH MORNING NEWS, Savannah, Georgia from March 1949 to January 1950; INTERNATIONAL NEWS SERVICE, Atlanta, Georgia, August 1950 to October 1951; Lockheed Aircraft Corporation, Marietta, Georgia (as publicist), October 1951 to April 1952. From April-June 1952 Subject was political campaign manager for C. Baxter JONES in his unsuccessful campaign for U. S. Congressman from Georgia. From March 1952 to December 1953 Subject was employed by the ANDERSON INDEPENDENT, Anderson, South Carolina; December 1953 to December 1957, THE CHARLOTTE OBSERVER, Charlotte, North Carolina. He won the "Big Story Award" while with the OBSERVER.

Subject is the grandson of former Georgia governor and U. S. Senator,
Hoke MAY.
