**INTELLIGENCE COVERAGE OF FOREIGN COUNTRIES**

- Mr. Belmont
- Mr. Sullivan
- Mr. Evans
- Mr. Donahoe
- Liaison
- Mr. G.C. Moore

On October 13, 1961, I advised you of our discussions with the Department of State relative to expanding our existing program of developing sources within the diplomatic establishments of selected foreign countries so as to include fifteen additional countries in Central and South America. I advised you the Department of State would not grant clearance for the proposed expansion but indicated it would consider individual requests. Our views as to the merits of the program were outcome at that time.

On the above date we directed a letter to Mr. U. Alexander Johnson, Deputy Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs, expressing our views regarding the situation. Under Secretary of State George W. Hall has replied in a letter dated January 10, 1962, explaining and reiterating the views of the Department of State and indicating the Department of State desires to be helpful and cooperative.

For ready reference I am attaching one copy each of the communications mentioned.

The development of sources in diplomatic establishments is admittedly a delicate operation but can produce data of definite value. This is illustrated by several examples during the past year. Sources of this type have provided the first authentic confirmation of the assassination of the Dominican dictator, generalissimo Trujillo; identified the Iranian American Ambassador to Organization of American States as on the payroll of the Dominicans; provided data as to what the President of Rakisten would probably discuss with President Kennedy; furnished a code book utilized by Cuban diplomatic establishments in Cuba; and have provided valuable intelligence data in connection with our inquiries into Dominican Sollying activities in the United States.
