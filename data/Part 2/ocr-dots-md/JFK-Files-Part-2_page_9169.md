RICHARD LEE GRANGER, Security Officer, Security and
Safety Section, United Nations (UN) General Assembly (UNGA)
Building, was interviewed in the vicinity of 43rd Street and
UN Plaza, New York, New York. Security Officer GRANGER
furnished the following information:

On Wednesday, August 7, 1974, he reported to his place
of employment at the UN Building, New York, New York, at approxi-
mately 9:00 a.m. At 9:15 a.m., the guard shift for that day
underwent inspection and he was assigned security post 117,
which is the Meditation Room, First Floor, UN Building. GRANGER
distinguished the Meditation Room as a small, darkened room set
aside for purposes of prayer and meditation for persons of
all religious faiths. He described the room as being almost
rectangular in shape with one end being smaller than the
other, however. The room contains a small square stone,
and the west end of the room contains ten small benches
utilized for the purposes of prayer or meditation. GRANGER
further stated that following his assignment, he obtained the
key to the Meditation Room from the basement Conference Room
at 9:25 a.m. and proceeded directly to the Meditation Room,
which is normally locked, except for the hours of operation
of 9:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., seven days a week. He advised that
after unlocking the room, he undertook the normal procedure
of physically searching the room with a flashlight for packages
or articles of clothing possibly left by visitors to the UN
Building. He advised that he searched the east end of the room
near the large painting first, and then proceeded to search
in the vicinity of the west end, turning over each of the small
benches in the process of searching. He advised that when he
turned over the small bench in the extreme southwest corner of
the room, he saw what appeared to be five sticks of dynamite.
He advised that on looking closer, he observed what appeared
to be two sticks of dynamite taped together, three loose sticks,
an open pack of magnos, a cigarette, and a fuse assembly lying
in the corner of the room. He advised that the cigarette was
not smoking. He advised that he immediately left the room,
without touching any part of the device, locking the door
behind him and notified his immediate superior, Sergeant MITCHELL.
