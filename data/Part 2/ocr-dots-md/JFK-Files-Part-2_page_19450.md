January 16, 1968

DEMONSTRATION PROTESTING UNITED
STATES POLICY IN VIETNAM,
WASHINGTON, D.C., JANUARY 15, 1968

At 7:45 a.m., Eastern Standard Time, on January 15, 1968, a group of women began gathering at the information counter in the 30th Street Station of the Pennsylvania Railroad, Philadelphia, Pa. One sign carried by two women bearing the legend, "JEANNETTE RANKIN BRIGADE," was displayed. A few of the women wore WOMEN STRIKE FOR PEACE (WSP) buttons and a few wore blue overseas caps with VETERANS AND RESERVISTS FOR PEACE in white lettering on them. Nearly all the women wore some article of Black Clothing.

The group was composed predominantly of white, middle-aged women. The group, numbering, approximately 125, was well behaved and orderly.

At 8:10 a.m., Eastern Standard time, the group boarded cars numbered 1582, 1535, and 1509 of Pennsylvania Railroad Train #131, The Congressional, bound for Washington, D.C. The train left 30th Street Station at 8:20 a.m. Inere were no incidents.
