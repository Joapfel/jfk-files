# Translator's Introduction

This dialogue was conducted with "Abu-Amar" by Talal Salman, a Lebanese journalist who visited the fedayeen bases and wrote a series of articles in al-Sayyad, a Lebanese weekly magazine. The first article, "Dialogue with Abu-Amar", appeared in al-Sayyad on January 2, 1969.

The article is an important document because it presents the ideas and thoughts of the leader of al-Fatch in a comprehensive manner. "Abu-Amar" discusses the ideas of the Movement in clear terms, and attempts to answer critics by further explaining the Movement's stand on certain ambiguous questions.

A number of important ideas are clarified in this article. First, the emergence of "Palestinian identity" as an independent force, adopting armed struggle to regain the "land" and confront the Imperialist presence. Second, Fateh's relation to Arab governments is clarified. "Abu-Amar" stresses that the movement does not interfere in the local politics of the Arab governments. It depends on popular support and on the masses for protection. Fateh seeks a certain independence and in that sense it interferes in the affairs of Arab States; by existing on their territory, by moving freely, and by exercising the right to fight for liberation of the occupied territories. Third, Fateh appears as a unifying force, since its bases exist in most Arab states and its men move freely from one state to another. Fateh, therefore, works against regionalism and promotes Arab unity in action. Fourth, the question of ideology or leftist vs. rightist controversy is clarified. "Abu-Amar" stresses that all Fateh men are of the wretched of the earth since they lack the means for existence.
