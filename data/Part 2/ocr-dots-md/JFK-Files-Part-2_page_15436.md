The increasing political instability in the countries
of Central and South America has been a serious cause for
concern in view of the imports made by communism, the efforts
of the Castro regime in Cuba to export its revolutionary
concepts and the increased efforts of the Sino-Soviet bloc
to extend its influence in that area. In recognition of this
situation we sought clearance from the Department of State
for the designation of fifteen countries in that area as target
countries under this program.

We consulted Mr. U. Alexis Johnson, Deputy Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs, and Mr. Francis B. Heley, his special Assistant. We were advised by Mr. Johnson in September, 1961, that careful consideration had been given the matter. We advised, however, that clearance would not be granted for the proposed expansion of this program although consideration would be given to any request we might make with regard to any specific country or individual. Mr. Johnson indicated it was felt that the diplomatic establishments in the United States of these countries were strictly on the outside with regard to pertinent developments in the home countries. He felt no real intelligence could be gained and the risk of developing sources outweighed any possible gain.

Our past experience has shown this program can produce
intelligence data of value as well as investigative data of
assistance in handling our internal security responsibilities.
The development of informants in diplomatic establishments is
a delicate and tedious process which requires careful planning.
Thus most propitious time to implement such a program is prior
to the point that the diplomatic establishment of a particular
country is staffed solely by individuals who have no sympathy
with the position or views of the United States.
