OBSERVATIONS:

It cannot be said with any assurance that all of this coverage will be productive of valuable intelligence data. The diplomatic representatives in the U.S. of some countries are much more active and knowledgeable than those of others. With regard to some countries we may run into definite translation problems.

If the coverage were installed at the embassy and United Nations Delegation of each of the 16 countries mentioned above simultaneously, this would increase our number of technical surveillances by 32. We presently have 84 technical surveillances and one additional one is being installed. Of these 85, 81 are in Bureau cases and four are exclusively maintained at the request of the National Security Agency (NSA). We are also installing coverage of teletype facilities of seven additional establishments at NSA request, which will increase our count from 85 to 92. The addition of technical surveillances in instant matter will probably raise the total to 100 or more, of which 11 will be at NSA request. We have proposed that in the future we not include in our over-all count those technical surveillances which are maintained at the request of NSA.

The best way to test the potential in instant matter is to install coverage on six or eight establishments, see what we get, replace the unproductive coverage with new coverage of new establishments, and, in general, continually probe the potential of this coverage by changing our targets selectively.

RECOMMENDATIONS:

1. Attached for approval is a letter to the Attorney General specifying the additional countries coverage of which we feel might be productive. We are advising the Attorney General that if he approves we will explore the feasibility of coverage of the embassy as well as the United Nations Delegation of each country. Thereafter, if the coverage is feasible, we will clear each proposed installation with State and with the Attorney General, as we have in the past.
