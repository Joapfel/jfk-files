My memorandum 12/21/59 pointed out twelve field offices, directly or potentially involved under captioned program in the development of live sources within foreign official establishments in the U. S., had been requested to submit evaluations and suggestions regarding possible expansion of this phase of program. As a result three specific questions were raised. These were considered at Internal Security - Espionage Conference June 9-10, 1960, with results set forth hereinafter:

First question related to whether we should attempt source developments in establishments outside Washington, New York City, and San Francisco, where all existing sources currently operate, and whether we should alter present instructions that potential sources be limited almost exclusively to employees of foreign establishments who are U.S. citizens. Conference decided that official establishments outside Washington and New York City for the most part are of a commercial nature with honorary consuls, most of whom in fact are U.S. citizens. For this reason, in absence of a showing that valuable intelligence data might exist in establishments removed from these two cities, it would not be feasible to undertake extensive program to develop sources therein. With regard to expanding our targets to include for potential development individuals who are not U.S. citizens, Conference was of the opinion we should continue as in the past concentrating on U.S. citizens but giving consideration (taking into account any special circumstances involved) to making discreet approaches after thorough investigation to foreigners employed in these diplomatic establishments, this to be done on a highly selective basis.
