Convert to Markdown5. How to Establish Policy Control? Here I wonder whether the British experience might not be of value. The notable feature of the British intelligence system is the determination to keep clandestine activity under strict Foreign Office control. This control is achieved in a number of ways:

a) Secret Intelligence Service (MI-6) itself operates under the direction of the Joint Intelligence Committee, which has a Foreign Office chairman (until recently Sir Patrick Dean, who is now the British representative at the UN) and which includes the Service Intelligence directors and representatives of the Colonial Office and the Commonwealth Relations Office as well as the Chief of SIS.

b) basic political warfare directives are originated, not by SIS, but by the Information Research Department in the Foreign Office, often in consultation with an interdepartmental Working Group on International Communist Fronts, and under the ultimate control of the Supervintending Under-Secretary of the Permanent Under-Secretary's Department in the Foreign Office,

c) SIS cover political action campaigns must not only conform to Foreign Office directives but must be cleared with the appropriate Foreign Office geographical desk.

d) working groups under IRB chairmanship govern operations in special areas, such as, for example, Sino-Soviet relations by the World Youth Festival.

e) a Foreign Office Staff Liaison Office sits next to the SIS Chief, and Foreign Office officials serve tours of duty in SIS sections.
