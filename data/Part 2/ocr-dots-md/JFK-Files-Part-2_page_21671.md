Mr. Houston. Oh, no, there's a number of times. For instance, I think it was back in the '50's, there's a lot of them, but a couple might illustrate -- in those days we were doing a good deal of political action abroad in the sense of giving guidance to factions in foreign countries that U.S. policy favored in organizing elections. I don't mean in rigging them, but how to get down to the grass roots, the old-fashioned political advice on how to run a political campaign.

We didn't have many trained officers on this. We had one or two who were excellent and they wanted more, and they came to me with the proposition that -- this was an election year, which one I have forgotten -- that we put officers in both the Republican and Democratic campaign parties to get the practical experience, and I advised -- actually, I went over and talked with Malcolm Wilkie, who was then the legal counsel in the Department of Justice, and this was most ill-advisable, and I went back and they dropped the project.

More recently, our communication fellows down in Virginia found they could easily monitor long-distance telephone conversations from Latin American to Canada, and in some cases to New York, and these frequently carried conversations involving narcotics in great detail.
