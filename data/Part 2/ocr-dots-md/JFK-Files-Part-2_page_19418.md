DEMONSTRATIONS PROTESTING
UNITED STATES INTERVENTION
IN VIETNAM

On January 15, 1968, Edward Higgins, Commission on Subversive Activities, Legislature, State of Hawaii, at Honolulu, advised as follows:

A short time after noon that date, some fifteen women gathered on the lawn in front of the Main United States Post Office Building in Honolulu and passed out a leaflet, "Hawaii Women Support the Jeannette Rankin Brigade," a photostat of which is appended hereto. The women's group which included Ada Laurenty, Aiko Reinecke, Jean King, and Bette Johnson, were interviewed by local press and television representatives.

The January 15, 1968, edition of the Honolulu Advertiser, a daily newspaper of Honolulu, reported that the Jeannette Rankin Brigade plans a march today in Washington, D.C., to petition Congress to end the war in Vietnam.

On January 4, 1963, Honolulu T-1 identified Ada Laurenty as a Communist Party (CP) member in San Diego in 1934 and as being active in the CP in the San Francisco, California, area in 1943.

On November 30, 1953, Robert McBurney Kempa, a former member of the CP in Honolulu from 1947 to 1952, identified Aiko Reinecke as a CP member in 1952.

On October 8, 1953, Kempa identified Jean King as a CP member from 1950 to 1952.
