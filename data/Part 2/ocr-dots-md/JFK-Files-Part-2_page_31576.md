Announcement was also made at the press conference that the purpose of the march was to oppose American intervention in Vietnam, and demand an end to Federal and local suppression of the protest movement. Mention was also made that a team of lawyers would take every possible legal step to force the City of Oakland to permit the march.

Records of the U.S. District Court, San Francisco, revealed that on November 1, 1965, a petition was filed by the VDC which asked for an injunction against the City of Oakland, the Mayor, the Chief of Police and the Acting City Manager. U.S. District Judge WILLIAM T. SWEIGERT filed an order for the defendants to show cause by November 12, 1965, why they should not permit a parade through the City of Oakland on November 20, 1965, by the VDC.

In the November 12, 1965, issue of the "San Francisco Examiner", there appeared an article captioned, "Berkeley OKs VDC March, but Oakland Sticks to Ban."

The article informed that the City of Berkeley had approved a parade permit, however, the City of Oakland was denying the parade permit on the grounds it could cause violence. Oakland City Manager JOHN A. MORIN denied the permit for several reasons.

1. "The proposed 7-1/2 mile march to a point near OAT starting at 1:00 PM would still be going on after dark."

2. The fact he had received 3 parade applications and one amended petition, all signed by different individuals in the VDC. This led him to believe the VDC was a "loosely-knit organization which might not be able to control itself."

3. "It is not reasonable to expect local taxpayers to suffer inconvenience and exposure to possible violence, as well as having to pay the high cost of protection."

On November 17, 1965, U.S. District Judge SWEIGERT issued a preliminary injunction asked by the plaintiff (VDC)
