There is a general misunderstanding concerning the use of Army Special Forces units. Navy SEAL teams, Air Force Jungle Jim organizations or Marine Corps special units in covert and clandestine operations. While it is entirely possible and feasible to covert individuals or units for clandestine or covert operations, the basic mission of Special Forces originally was oriented toward the organization of resistance forces in support of overt military operations. Subsequently, the charter of Special Forces was expanded to incorporate counter-insurgency operations in friendly territory. Both of these missions are essentially overt in nature and there is a lack of experience and doctrine for the commitment of Special Forces in clandestine or covert operations. This generally applies to units of the other military services. Any major reëvaluation of the mission of these units will detract from their current requirements and could jeopardize, for example, the ability of our Special Forces to assist in the organization of a resistance in Cuba in support of a conventional military operation there.

It is essential to an understanding of the problem to realise that since late 1961, all U. S. intelligence estimates have indicated that there is little likelihood of an internally sparked Cuban resistance to be of any significance.
