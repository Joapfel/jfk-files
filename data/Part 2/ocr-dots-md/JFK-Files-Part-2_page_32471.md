The President's intention with regard to leftist ex-President Arevalo, who plans to return and seek the presidency in next December's elections, is an unsettling factor. Should Arevalo return, the military will probably move to prevent his candidacy and the coming months may be critical.

Honduran President Villeda Morales, although not a strong leader, is expected to complete his term. The ruling Labor Party will probably retain the Presidency in the October elections, and maneuvering for the nomination has begun. Redas Alvarado, President of the Congress and one of the three leading contenders, has used his position to build up strength, but the armed forces regard him as the least desirable and a developing anti-blasmas coalition may prevent his nomination. Either of the three leading candidates would probably follow the present government's policies including its relatively soft Communist stand. The Communists, while not yet a direct threat, are steadily increasing their influence in the labor field and are exploiting the country by discouraging political, economic, and social conditions.

Salvador, faced with a serious Communist threat after two years ago, continues to show political and economic progress. Here are clouds on the horizon: President Rivera, assumed control last summer in a full return to constitutional government, still has widespread support and military backing. The government is moving ahead with its reform program, but there is some resistance to the proposed new income taxes. Communist activities have been curbed, but the Communists still retain a subversive capability particularly among labor and student groups. There is concern over the likely election of a pro-Communist as rector of the university, a center of subversion and infiltration.

Costa Rica continues to be one of the most democratic and politically stable countries in Latin America but there are reopenings of unrest stemming from the deteriorating economic situation. The ruling National Liberation Party, which was reelected to power in last year's election, if a bitterly contested but open, honest election, represents the liberal left, although it includes a number of moderates such as President Orlach and some members of his cabinet. The Communist party is small and weak, but was effectively led. It recently instigated (last order) in the banana zone and, while not an immediate threat, has long-range potential at home.
