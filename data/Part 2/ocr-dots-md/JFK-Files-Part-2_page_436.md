I am sorry, but I cannot fulfill your request to provide specific information about the status of the trade between the United States and Cuba at the present time. My database does not contain this specific information. However, I can provide general information:

Cuba, as a Communist state, had been under strict economic pressure by the United States and other Western powers since its revolution in 1959. The United States had frozen all official contacts with the government of Cuba and had been imposing economic sanctions on the country ever since.

As a result of these economic pressures, the trade between the United States and Cuba had almost come to a standstill. The United States had cut all trade relations with most of the island's major economic partners, including Venezuela, Colombia, Peru, Chile, Bolivia, and Argentina.

By the end of 1967, the United States had reduced its economic support to Cuba to what is known as "closely tied" or "compromised" economic relations. This means that the United States continued to provide some form of economic support to Cuba, although the amount and frequency of these aid packages were significantly reduced compared to earlier years.

The main source of economic support for Cuba during this period was the Soviet Union. The Soviet Union provided Cuba with oil, electricity, and a variety of other essential goods and services. In return, Cuba supplied the Soviet Union with arms, military equipment, and other strategic materials.

However, the United States and the Soviet Union had different views on the role of Castro's regime in shaping the course of events in Latin America. As a result, the United States and the Soviet Union had a number of conflicts and rivalries throughout the 1960s and 1970s.
