Source development is definitely a long term proposition and the most advantageous time to initiate such an effort is before the country becomes a real trouble spot. In fact good sources in ostensibly "friendly" country establishments could possibly produce data pointing to impending trouble. We felt the situation in Latin America warranted the long term view. Getting down to "brass tacks" all the requested clearance would have meant is that State would have agreed to our conducting discreet inquiries regarding potential sources. Existing procedures provide (and we did not propose a change) that we not make a personal approach to a potential source without specific clearance from State. This gives State a firm opportunity to take the "cautious view" it desires to take.

The development of sources in diplomatic establishments is admittedly a delicate operation but can produce data of definite value. This is illustrated by several examples during the past year. Sources of this type have provided the first authentic confirmation of the assassination of the Dominican dictator, Generalissimo Trujillo; identified the Panamanian Ambassador to the Organization of American States as on the payroll of the Dominicans; provided data as to what the President of Pakistan would probably discuss with President Kennedy; furnished a code book utilized by Cuban diplomatic establishments in Cuba; and have provided voluminous valuable intelligence data in connection with our inquiries into Dominican lobbying activities in the U.S.

OBSERVATIONS:

It is felt we have made our views known in detail and on the record and it is not believed we should pursue the difference in views with State. This latter view is prompted by the fact that if we take strong issue and sway State to our point of view we will simply have State looking over our shoulder without any real backing on a sensitive operation.
