4. **Paramilitary Warfare.** I gather, is regarded in some quarters as a purely technical matter, easily detachable from policy and therefore a proper function of the Department of Defense. Yet there is almost no CIA function more peculiarly dependent on the political context than paramilitary warfare.

There are several reasons for this. For one thing, a paramilitary operation is in its nature a large and attributable operation and thereby, no suggested additions, clashes with the presuppositions of our own society. (These considerations need not apply, however, to the training of, say, the South Vietnamese in guerrilla tactics or to the support of already existing guerrilla activities.) For another, the moral and political price of direct paramilitary failure is acute for us. Communists, when they stimulate paramilitary activity, are doing what the world expects from them: when we do it, we appear to betray our own professional principles and therefore cannot afford to compound delinquency by defeat. Moreover, as the recent Algerian episode shows, once we convince the world that we are committed to a paramilitary endeavor, we will be blamed for all sorts of things. And, as the recent tracts far-prizeone's episode showed, when we do end men to possible death, we commit lightly where we call and close the books. The Communists, on the other hand, have no compulsion about liquidating a losing foe.
