SECRET

COUNTRY: Panama/El Salvador/Chile

SUBJECT: Panamanian National, Julian NINO Ruiz, Deported from El Salvador/ Limited Biographies

XMDN OAB(S) 322/06120-70

13 March 1970

1. On 5 and 6 Mar 70 guard personnel from The Wackenhat Corporation✓ were contracted to provide guard services for the subject to insure his departure from the US while changing plans at Miami International Airport. While supervising the guard service, the following information was obtained.

2. Subject is a Panamanian national, an alleged Communist, deported from El Salvador for political reasons. Government of Panama refused to accept entry of subject; Chile agreed to accept him. Subject had no passport or visa, only various "emergency" papers. Physical description: Black male, 5'6", 140 pounds, about 35 years of age, gold filled teeth.

3. Subject was being transported from El Salvador to Chile via La Chilean Airlines and arrived at the Miami International Airport at 2211 hours on 5 Mar 70. He was taken to the La Chile VIP room where he was guarded until his time of departure at 0545 on 6 Mar 70 for his destination in Chile. There was to be a layover in Lima, Peru, where it was realized that subject might attempt an escape.

4. At the Miami International Airport subject was met by the Panaman Consul General (name unknown) and the El Salvadoran Consul General, Ing. Ricardo Dutra, 208 Pan American Bank Building, 150 EE Third Avenue, Miami, Florida, Telephone 371-8850. Both Consuls stated subject was being deported for political reasons. El Salvadoran Consul stated that subject is a criminal and a Communist; Panamanian Consul stated he was a criminal. Both considered subject to be dangerous and both felt that he might attempt an escape.

5. Subject's luggage contained a black beret with a red medal if it shaped like a shield with gold stitching on it which was not real.

6. Subject allegedly was a member of a gang charged with murder and performing perverted acts on 20 people in El Salvador.

7. The attached paper contains a list of names, addresses, & numbers of individuals in Miami who subject was supposed to contact his arrival in the Miami area. When asked if he would do so, his response was denied by officials. However, the El Salvadoran Consul General a copy of the document and I complied with his request. Subject was aware of a copy being made as nothing had been said in front of him when the document was returned to him. He immediately smelled if there was an odor of copier fluid; he was apparently satisfied the copy had not been made as he said nothing and just quietly left.
