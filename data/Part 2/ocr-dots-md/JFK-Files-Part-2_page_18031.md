DATE 10/18/75 BY SPAM CHG

Former Assistant Director Charles D. Brennan telephonically contacted SA Paul V. Daly of the Legal Counsel Division on 9/22/75 to advise that he had been requested by the Senate Select Committee to appear before that Committee at 5 p.m. on 9/23/75 to testify under oath concerning his knowledge of the "Huston Plan." Brennan was further informed that he was being requested to testify during open session of the Committee on Thursday, 9/25/75, regarding the same topic. He was advised generally of his legal rights and of the appropriate provisions of the rules of procedure concerning the Senate Select Committee. He was informed that the Bureau was cooperating with the Committee and had furnished considerable information in the form of briefings and documents some of which directly relate to the Bureau's knowledge and participation in the "Huston Plan."

Brennan expressed concern as to exactly what topics he might discuss during an open session of the Senate Select Committee and it was explained to him that he should contact Section Chief William O. Cregar of the Intelligence Division who would appropriately advise him concerning this area. Brennan asked if SA Daly would have Cregar contact him concerning this matter and stated he could be reached at telephone number 790-6132. Cregar was appropriately advised of the aforementioned and indicated he would contact Brennan.

RECOMMENDATION:

ST. 114 REC-20 62 116 370-805 OCT 1 1975

That former Assistant Director Charles D. Brennan be released from his employment agreement and the applicable sections of the Code of Federal Regulations for purposes of giving testimony before the Senate Select Committee.

The Department of Justice has issued a warning against investing in high-yield corporate bonds called "jumbo" or "ultra-high-yield," or "ehyd." These are known as "securitization investments" because they convert a loan or other security into a pool of loans and then securitize them into securities that can be bought and sold to investors.

Investors in these "securities" typically buy them through a broker-dealer such asamu mkt.com, ampmkt.com, bnpartners.com, mon Financial, roan, uofc, zbuphilly or zellerwesley.com. Investors can also purchase them directly from banks or other financial institutions.

Investing in these securities carries certain risks, including the risk of default by the underlying loans or securities. Other risks include the risk of inflation, changes in interest rates, credit losses, and possible loss of principal.

If you are considering investing in these securities, it is important to understand your own investment goals and risks before making any investment decisions.

Before investing, read the "Risk Factors" section of the prospectus or statement of information that comes with the offer. The "Risk Factors" will help you understand the potential risks and benefits of investing in these securities.

You should consider your own financial situation and objectives before investing in these securities. This warning does not mean that all investments in these securities will fail. Some investments may do well over time, while others may fail completely. It is important to understand the risks and benefits of investing in these securities carefully before investing.

Investing in these securities involves a lot of research and due diligence. You should do your own research before investing in any new investment. This includes learning about the issuer, its history, its business, its financial condition, its cash flow, its debt structure, its earnings history, and its prospects for the future.
