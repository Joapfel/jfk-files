A. Alberto LLEGRAS Casargo (201-102576) was born in Bogota, 3 July 1908. He left school at the age of seventeen to become a journalist. He traveled to Argentina and lived in Buenos Aires for three years (1926-29) writing for various Argentine periodicals. His political career began upon his return to Colombia in 1939, when he was appointed secretary of the Liberal Party (1930-33) and received tremendous influence under the regime of Alfonso LOPEZ, Liberal Party leader and former President of Colombia (1934-38 and 1942-45). Subsequently, LLEGRAS served in the National House of Representatives (1941-44). When alfonso LOPEZ was inaugurated President in 1944, he took LLEGRAS into his government as Secretary General of the Presidency (1934-35), and later made him Minister of Government from 1945 to 1948. The latter year he was made Minister of Foreign Affairs, as well as First Vice President of Colombia. His public service culminated then in his accession to the Presidency on the resignation of President LOPEZ in 1948. After discontinuing the duties of Chief Executive for a year (1948-49), LLEGRAS was elected Secretary General of the Pan American Union in 1949, a post which he held until 1954. He then resigned and returned to Colombia to accept the position of Rector (President) of the University of The Hague (1955-56). Devoting himself once again to politics, LLEGRAS began writing a column in the Liberal Daily El Respectador. In his columns he strongly criticized the Bojas regime (1953-57) and called for the return of constitutional government in Colombia. In March 1958, he was elected President of the Liberal Party and shortly thereafter (June 1958) travelled to Spain and had conversations with Laurosano COMEDES, a leader of the Conservative Party and former President of Colombia (1930-33).
