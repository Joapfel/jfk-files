Further contact, if any, depends upon our assessment of the attitude and reliability of the individual and the degree of cooperation volunteered.

As a result of this program, which has proceeded on a most careful and discreet basis, we have developed or are attempting to develop sources with regard to a number of countries in the Middle East, Africa and Asia as well as the countries of Cuba, Venezuela and Brazil.

The increasing political instability in the countries of Central and South America has been a serious cause for concern in view of the inroads made by communism, the efforts of the Castro regime in Cuba to export its revolutionary concepts and the increased efforts of the Sino-Soviet bloc to extend its influence in that area. This situation prompted us to initiate contact with the Department of State and to request clearance for the designation of fifteen countries in that area as target countries under this program.

Following his conversation with you and with Mr. Meloy on September 12, 1961, Mr. Bartlett informed me that after careful consideration you had advised clearance would not be granted for the proposed expansion although consideration would be given to any request we might make with regard to any specific country or individual. I understand that you feel the diplomatic establishments in the United States of these countries are strictly on the outside with regard to pertinent developments in the home countries and no real intelligence could be gained. It was the opinion of the Department of State that the risk of developing sources outweighed any possible gain.

Our past experience has shown this program can produce intelligence data of value as well as investigative data of assistance in handling our internal security responsibilities.
