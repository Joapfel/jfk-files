Education:

Entered Harvard University in September 1940; completed requirements for a degree, cum laude, in history, government, and economics, February 1943; awarded degree while in service, November 1943.

History: 30 hours, including American, modern European, ancient, and medieval history.

Honors thesis: Andrew Jackson and the Presidency.

Government: 12 hours, including American federal government, comparative government, administration, and political theory.

Economics: 12 hours, including principles, banking, and international trade.

Further: Yale University, first year, ASTP, Far Eastern Area and Japanese, including Chinese and Japanese history, Pacific islands, society, Japanese political parties and society, and spoken Japanese.

University of Michigan: HHS Language School, including spoken and written Japanese, Japanese political, social, and military organization; awarded 12 hours graduate credit, 1944–45.

Graduate:

Training: Johns Hopkins University, 1947–48, 6 hours, international relations and political geography, including economic geography and seminar study of Korea.

University of Pennsylvania, 1948–50, 31 hours, history and political science.

History: 30 hours, including American social and diplomatic history, development and organization of the British Empire, British foreign policy 1919–1949, American colonial history, political history since 1665, the United States, and Latin America.

Political Science: 12 hours, government and politics of Latin America.

Diplomas: A.B., Harvard; A.M., University of Pennsylvania. Doctorates awarded in history, University of Pennsylvania, 1951.

Military Service: U.S. Army, 1943–1945, graduate corporal, unit: ARS, Japanese specialist.

Experience:

Instructor in History, Dickinson College, Carlisle, Pennsylvania, 1946–48.

Assistant Professor of History, Dickinson College, Carlisle, Pennsylvania, 1949. Subjects taught: world history, medieval history, American history, Far East, international relations, Indian geography, and historical geography.

Publications:

“A Frontier Expedition in Eighteen Years,” Pennsylvania History, Pennsylvania Historical Association, January 1949. A study of the social problems in the establishment of Dickinson College in Carlisle, Pennsylvania, 1703–1868.

“The Horizons and Their Living Spaces,” Human Review, Human Cultural Association, September 1949. An attempt to analyze the Korean transition in terms of political and economic geography.
