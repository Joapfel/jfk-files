April–May 1945: In Sofia in contact with G. W. Dimitrov, Nikola Petkov, and Wawa Racheva.

Obtained illegal identity card in the name of Boris Stolkov, a member of the 2nd Belgrade Shock Brigade. Toured southern and western Yugoslavia, “organizing Agrarian posts.” Captured by Yugoslavians.

Sent back to Bulgaria; escaped during transfer; reached Sofia, where he was helped by Peter Belbashki, Colonel Bakobbo, an American sergeant named “Bucky,” General Craig, and Mrs. Minnie Bala-Barova. Travelled through Bulgaria organizing “secret posts”—some 250.

18 November 1945: During elections, spoke at secret meetings in Vratisa, urging people to defeat Communism.

16 August 1946: Captured by police in Sofia; 18 days without bread; 12 days without water; 4 days on an electric battery machine. He believes he was not executed because Communists wished to hold him as a witness in preparation for the trial of Nikola Petkov.
