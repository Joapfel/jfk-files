Moskalev, a nerve physiologist and party man, worked with Catsch and subsequently succeeded him on research on curing or preventing radiation damage. Source is Dr. Wilhelm Henke (STIB), a returned German scientist who gave information on Soviet personalities at the Nuclear Research Institutes at Sukhumi between 1945 and 1955. Source: CIA, CS-G-82811, 1 March 1956.

Moskalev, Yuriy Ivanovich, worked as a scientific assistant in the source's laboratory at Sungul. He took the position of chief of the pathophysiological laboratory when the source left in October 1953. He was not well qualified. He was supposedly imprisoned during the war because of his German descent or for black market dealings. He became a Party member in 1951. He had one month's training at the Biophysical Institute, Moscow, during the last half of 1952. Upon his return, he informed the source that the institute was doing intensive work on the hematological effects on dogs of radiation exposure. The institute was also working on radiation effects on the nervous systems of dogs, similar to the conditioned reflex experiments of Pavlov. He also stated that the institute was doing parallel work to Sungul on increasing the excretion of incorporated radioactive isotopes, using diuretic substances and hormones, particularly parathyrotropic hormone. No special results were achieved. He was married to Vera Nikolayevna Streltsova. Age 35. Medium height. Dark blond.

Source: CIA, CS-G-EG-1550, 24 April 1956.
