13. Radio Habana, 5 April 1967, p.m.

Juan Mari Brás, Secretary-General of the Movement for the Independence of Puerto Rico, made the following statements in a long-distance telephone interview: “Dr. Luther King’s assassination will undoubtedly precipitate the rebellion of the Negro people of the United States against all the abuses of which they have been the object.”

GKM, Radio Habana, 5 April 1967, p.m.

The Executive Secretariat of the Asian, African, and Latin American Peoples Solidarity Organization (AALAPSO) sent a message of condolence to the SNCC on the assassination of Luther King. The AALAPSO’s message to the American pacifist organization ends as follows: “We beg you to express to the family of Martin Luther King and to all Afro-Americans our deepest sorrow over his death, our decision to support your struggle, and our deep conviction that this terrible crime—which shows us that armed revolutionary violence is the only way of achieving genuine liberation—will help strengthen the struggle of the Afro-American people, which is already aimed in the direction of a total confrontation.”

5. GTM, Radio Habana, 2 April 1967, p.m.

Agustin Gonzalez Martinez (JAgustin Gonzalez Martinez), Bishop of the Episcopal Church of Cuba, made the following statements in a special interview: “May the tragic disappearance of this great leader at least help to speed up the arrival of the day when every man in his nation and in all other nations will be treated with the respect which is due to them as human beings.”

16. KKM, Radio Habana, 7 April 1967, p.m.

Reverend (Joverend) Rafael Pastor, of the Presbyterian Church in Luyano, Havana, made the following statements in a special interview: “These brothers, the American Negroes, will learn much from his death. We can almost assure that they, the American Negroes whose dignity and decorum has been crushed, will come back to life after centuries of lethargy.”
