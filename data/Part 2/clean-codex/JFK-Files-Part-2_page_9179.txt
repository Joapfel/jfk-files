BROWN advised that he was going to place the explosives at the United Nations building whether or not JAY STAFFORD and his brother wanted to go along. BROWN gave the impression that he was committed to do this sometime around the first of August. BROWN told the STAFFORD brothers that it would have to be done and that he would do it alone if necessary. BROWN told the STAFFORDS that he had proposed this scheme to some of his associates whom he had known for ten years and that they had refused to go along with it. BROWN discussed the fact that he had been charged on a number of counts in the state of California, but had never been convicted. BROWN also alleged that he was using an alias and that if he used his true name, he would not be able to get employment. BROWN also exhibited to the STAFFORD brothers a motorcycle magazine, which BROWN suggested he was publishing or somehow involved in the publication of. JAY STAFFORD further advised that he observed four high-powered rifles in one of the upstairs rooms of the BROWN residence. STAFFORD described the upstairs of BROWN's residence as an attic that had been converted into utility space containing two rooms, one of these rooms being walled with a paneling material and unfinished, and the other room used to store what appeared to be personal items.

JAY STAFFORD advised that neither he nor his brother LARRY considered BROWN's proposal to be legitimate at the time and that their visit with BROWN was terminated with the understanding or feeling that JAY and LARRY would recontact him if and when they were interested in involving themselves in the type of activity which BROWN had described. JAY STAFFORD stated that he felt that BROWN trusted him and his brother, probably basing this trust on the fact that JAY and LARRY had been involved in a burglary-safe-cracking attempt.
