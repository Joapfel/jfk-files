Major Long also revealed that an MSS agent penetration of the VC, of untested reliability, had reported on 1 August that his VC superior in Chau Hiep, thirty kilometers west of Saigon, had instructed him on 31 July to be alert for a coup, and to meet his VC superior in Saigon four hours after the coup began.

Major Long also stated that a hand grenade had exploded in the VAP area of Saigon on the night of 1 August, injuring seven Vietnamese women and children. The relevance of the explosion to a coup attempt is not clear, but Major Long believed that the events might be connected.

At the same time, the Presidential Security Service (SEPES) intercepted coded radio messages on 1 August on unusual frequencies. The text of the messages, when decoded, was in Vietnamese and French and stated that a coup would occur on the night of 1–2 August. ARVN units have been alerted in the Saigon area.

Chau is considered to be a fairly reliable source, who has previously stated that, of all the ARVN officers, General Duong Van Minh was the most likely to lead a coup attempt. General Minh has been a vocal critic of the Diem regime insofar as Diem’s efficacy against the VC is concerned. General Mai Huu Xuan, frequently rumored to be a member of various coup groups, is also involved.
