Bombing of Kehilath Israel Synagogue, Kansas City, Missouri

January 28, 1960

On January 28, 1960, approximately 10:30 p.m., an explosion occurred in the front courtyard of the Kehilath Israel Synagogue, 800 East Meyer Boulevard, Kansas City, Missouri. Property damage consisting of 51 plate-glass windows shattered, a broken stained-glass window, and a dented door amounted to approximately $5,000. The only occupants of the synagogue were the janitor and his wife, neither of whom was injured. FBI Laboratory examination indicated characteristics of a dynamite explosion. A laboratory examiner was flown to Kansas City to assist in examination of evidence.

Witnesses observed two automobiles leaving the area at a high rate of speed immediately after the explosion. Identification was made of the occupants of one automobile. Interviews are being conducted. Other witnesses saw four youths running from the vicinity of the synagogue immediately after the explosion. A high school student stated that he and another student were responsible for the bombing; however, he later denied that they were involved. Students are being interviewed. Approximately one month ago, several thousand blasting caps were stolen from a company in Kansas City. Half of the stolen caps were recovered by Kansas City police in the possession of teenagers who are being considered as suspects in the bombing.

Investigation determined that a number of high school students in the Kansas City area had recently formed a Nazi-type group. Members of the group were identified and interviewed. Two juvenile members are prime suspects.

The FBI immediately offered the services of the Laboratory and the Identification Division to local authorities and is continuing to actively render every possible assistance to them.

A special squad of Special Agents has been formed, and this case is being afforded intensive investigation by the Kansas City Division.
