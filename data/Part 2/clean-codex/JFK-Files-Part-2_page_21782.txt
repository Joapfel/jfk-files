Mr. Wides. Like Alpha-66?

Mr. Sarno. Alpha-66, there were many of them.

And we usually had one of our outside types, either recruit or get next to an individual of the group and he would penetrate the group himself to report to us what they were doing.

Mr. Wides. Do you recall the flights of the Diaz, or one of the Diz brothers, over Havana dropping leaflets?

Mr. Sarno. What date was it?

Mr. Wides. I believe it was quite early, and it was not in connection with the AMUTS.

Mr. Sarno. I know the Diaz brothers.

Mr. Wides. Do you recall hearing of flights with—one of them with Frank Sturgis over Havana dropping leaflets?

Mr. Sarno. No, sir.

Mr. Wides. It was fairly widely known.

Mr. Sarno. Well, I didn't get into Cubans until maybe it was June of '60, but the only leaflets I ever saw fly over Cuba was the day before the invasion. Those were the only ones I saw.

Mr. Wides. When you were in Miami, did you have any contact with Frank Sturgis, Frank Fiorini?

Mr. Sarno. Never, never. To my knowledge, he never worked for us.
