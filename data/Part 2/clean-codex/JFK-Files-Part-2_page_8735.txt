Ships and cargoes. Now appears AMLEG agents generally unsuited for violent sabotage.

Director 39186, dated 11 May 1963.

Restriction on sabotage applies only to explosive and incendiary type actions such as limpets and shape charges. Wish to explore possibility of non-violent methods of sabotaging Cuban ships. Wish to minimize USG sponsorship of sabotage acts whenever possible.

This program is to be held on a strict need-to-know basis.

Therefore, no briefing of State personnel without prior Hqs. approval. Request for approval in most cases will be denied. Rationale for revision is that State fully represented in approval of this program and may brief their Chiefs of Mission through their own channels if they wish.

Researcher comment: In view of the large number of persons briefed on the program in a short period of time, it appears likely that the Government of Cuba could have possibly learned of the program by June 1963, some four months before the assassination of President Kennedy. The State Department approved the operation and ambassadors were to be informed on a strictly “need-to-know” basis. There was a minor flap when it was learned that Ambassador McArthur (Belgium) was informed of the program.
