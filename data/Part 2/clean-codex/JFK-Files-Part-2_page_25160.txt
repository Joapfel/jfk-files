Persons/Entities: Diem.

Dates: August 21, 1963.

Categories: Diem.

Occurrence (card 1):

Higgins characterizes the raids on the Xa Loi and August 21, 1963, as the turning point in American policy toward Viet Nam. She states that this crystallizes the feelings of a powerful action inside the Kennedy Administration that Diem had to be opposed.

Raised stories from the Saigon scene that had all the inventiveness of an Oriental Ian Fleming. Soldiers in battle dress were wearing gasmasks, “stormed” into shortly after midnight. A of bells, a blood-curdling scream shattered the spirits’ serenity. One eyewitness professed to see two monks thrown twenty feet from a balcony. Extravagant rumors about the number of dead and wounded spread like a wild malignancy.

“When the United Nations in Viet Nam investigated the rumors three months later, he could find no evidence that anyone had been killed!”

Source: Our Vietnam Nightmare by Marguerite Higgins, Harper and Rowe. The investigators talked at length with those who had been present during the entire affair at Xa Loi and elsewhere. (80–81)

Staff: Dawson. Date: July 2, 1975.

Persons/Entities: Diem; Ball, Undersecretary of State; Helms, Richard; Colby, Win. Date: 8/21/63. Categories: Diem.

Occurrence:

On 21 August 1963, South Vietnamese troops and police attacked the occupied Buddhist pagodas throughout the country. Diem imposed nationwide martial law. At a presidential meeting on Vietnam on this same date, the participants from the CIA, including the DCI, DDP, and Chief of Far Eastern Division, were told, based on notes taken by the Chief of the Far Eastern Division, that “on the matter of possible alternate leadership in Vietnam, it was pointed out that there was no indication that Diem was not in full control.” (2)

Source: 1967 I.G. Report, Part C.
