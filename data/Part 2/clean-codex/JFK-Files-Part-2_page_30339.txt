1. Mr. Edgar. Have you ever been in a conversation with any of the agents of the CIA about who might have been responsible for having you detained on April 4th, 1964?

2. Mr. Nosenko. No.

3. Mr. Edgar. 1964.

4. Mr. Nosenko. No.

5. Mr. Edgar. You indicated earlier you thought it was a man by the name of Murphy.

6. Mr. Nosenko. Murphy, Chief of Soviet, former Chief of Soviet Russian Division, and Chief of Counterintelligence, former Chief of Counterintelligence of the CIA, Mr. Angleton, because it cannot without him be decided.

7. Mr. Edgar. Do you know of any other KGB agents or other defectors who were held in those very same conditions?

8. Mr. Nosenko. No, I do not know.

9. Mr. Edgar. Were there any other prisoners held nearby that you could hear or—

10. Mr. Nosenko. No, sir, I was in solitary, absolutely confinement, no prisoner, nobody I could see.

11. Mr. Edgar. Yet you are still willing to be a consultant to the CIA?

12. Mr. Nosenko. I am—no, sir. I am consultant only on the base, on my knowledge, what I know about counterintelligence work of the KGB.

13. Mr. Edgar. Well, you have suggested to the Committee that your statements given to the CIA in 1964 cannot be considered.
