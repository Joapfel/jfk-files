Headline “Pal Claims Panther Chief Murdered in Bed,” a reporter described a tour given newsmen by Rush showing the only bullet holes “in the spread and mattress of Hampton’s blood-splattered bedroom.” Rush was quoted as charging, “Hampton was murdered in bed while he slept. Since he was a light sleeper, some pig [policeman] must have come in the back door and murdered him with a silencer” (Chicago Today, Dec. 4, 1969).

The basic controversy continued to receive extensive coverage in every media. On December 7, defense attorney Frances Andrew was quoted as saying a private autopsy showed that Hampton was “murdered while he was asleep,” and Renault Robinson, head of the Afro-American Patrolmen’s League, reportedly said after touring the site that it appeared the men were killed “for no reason in a police set-up” (Chicago Sun-Times, 12/7).

By December 8, Mayor Chaalala of suburban Maywood, where Hampton had grown up, announced that three of the Villages Trustees, seven of the nine from the Relations Commission, issued a statement demanding that State Attorney all the shooting.

(Tim-Times, 12/8)
