Your Revolver

All of the revolvers listed at the beginning of this article can use a .38-caliber cleaning rod, bore brush tip, cleaning solvent, and gun oil. For cleaning, you can use material like flannel and cotton, any material that does not ravel. Cut any linty material into circles about 2 inches in diameter. All of this cleaning material can be purchased at any gun store.

Your revolver should be cleaned twice a week, and each time after firing. Keep it free of dust and dampness to avoid rusting. If you do not clean your revolver after firing, it can cause corrosion in the barrel and eat away at it, causing your shooting to become inaccurate because of the powder eating away at the barrel and rust from a lack of cleaning and dampness.

After firing, your barrel and chambers in the cylinder have to be scrubbed with your bore brush and solvent every time. Attach the brush to the rod, dip the brush in solvent, and use a brisk movement in and out of the barrel and chambers in the cylinder, cleaning out all of the particles. Hold the barrel up to the light to see if it shines, and you will be able to see any particles still holding on.

Take each bore brush and put the tip on the end of a patch through the tip and mop the cylinder until the patch comes out clean. Use each patch about 10 times. You will use more than one patch to mop the barrel clean.

Attach a clean patch to the rod and add 2 drops of oil on the patch, and mop the already dried barrel with oil. Do not soak the patch in oil.
