United States Government

Memorandum

To: W. C. Sullivan

Date: April 30, 1963

From: W. R. Wannall

Subject: Technical Surveillance Coverage of Diplomatic Establishments

With approval of the Attorney General on January 31, 1962, we instituted a program to expand use of technical surveillance on diplomatic establishments of countries outside the Soviet bloc. Following is an analysis of this program and its current status.

We advised the Attorney General on January 26, 1962, that on a selective and probing basis we would explore coverage of the following countries under this program by instituting coverage on six or eight establishments at a time: Argentina, Brazil, Republic of Congo, Guinea, India, Indonesia, Japan, Laos, Mali, Mexico, Morocco, Panama, Portugal, Thailand, Venezuela, and South Vietnam.

After necessary surveys and State clearance in each instance, initial coverage was initiated in early March 1962, and to date coverage on the following establishments has been instituted:

Embassy of Argentina  
Embassy of Brazil  
Brazilian Delegation to United Nations (UN)  
Congolese Delegation to UN  
Embassy of Guinea  
Guinea Delegation to UN  
Embassy of India  
Indian Delegation to UN  
Embassy of Indonesia  
Indonesian Delegation to UN  
Embassy of Japan  
Embassy of Mali  
Embassy of Mexico  
Mexican Delegation to UN  
Embassy of Panama  
Embassy of Portugal  
Portuguese Delegation to UN  
Embassy of Venezuela  
Venezuelan Delegation to UN

Of the above-listed establishments, as of this date we have productive coverage on the following; others were discontinued as unproductive:

Embassy of Argentina  
Embassy of Brazil  
Embassy of Mexico  
Embassy of Panama  
Embassy of Venezuela
