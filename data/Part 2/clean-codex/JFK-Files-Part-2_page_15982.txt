On May 9, 1969, it was learned that Chicago BPP had issued an ultimatum to the BR that they should join the BPP or else. BR had no intention of joining Chicago BPP.

Chicago BPP and Young Socialist Alliance (YSA)

The Young Socialist Alliance (YSA) held its National Convention in Chicago, Illinois, from November 28, 1968, to December 1, 1968.

On November 29, 1968, Robert Brown, leader of Chicago BPP, spoke to the YSA convention. Brown spoke for over one hour on the importance of winning “the black masses to black socialism.”

From November 28, 1968, to December 1, 1968, the 8th National Convention of the YSA was held in Chicago, Illinois. Of the 791 persons registered, Robert Brown of Chicago was the only individual who identified himself as representing the BPP from Chicago.

YSA speakers stated at the convention, “The YSA supports the BPP as a black political party. Groups like this (BPP) will unite the Black People in a struggle against the policies of today’s government and will be essential in the formation of the Socialist Revolution.”

On January 29, 1969, at a meeting of the Socialist Workers Party (SWP) in Chicago, Illinois, one of the speakers stated that the YSA and SWP had bad communication with the BPP.

On February 25, 1969, YSA at Northern Illinois University, DeKalb, Illinois, sponsored a panel-type discussion entitled “Black Liberation Front.”

The moderator of the panel was Paul Boutelle, a member of the New York Socialist Workers Party.
