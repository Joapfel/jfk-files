Incoming Telegram. Department of State.

From: Kingston. To: Secretary of State. No. 567, February 11, 6 p.m. Control: 7384. Received: February 11, 1963, 9:11 p.m.

Permanent Secretary, External Affairs Ministry, told Embassy today that GOJ was concerned over reports it had received that the USSR was furnishing Cubans not only fishing vessels, but also torpedo boats and submarine chasers. He foresaw Soviet-supplied ships as having potential adverse effect on the Jamaican fishing industry, as well as presenting a possible security problem. He asked whether the US could provide more detailed information on these ships, which would operate from a Soviet-built “fishing base” in Cuba.

In view of the reports, he planned to urge Cabinet to take early action on deciding and publicizing limits of Jamaican territorial waters in order to avoid incidents with Soviets and Cubans. He is considering recommending to Cabinet that Jamaica claim territorial waters of six-and-six miles. Jamaica now apparently claims a three-mile limit.

Although GOJ is obviously counting on receipt of US military equipment, it seems unlikely that this expression of GOJ concern was motivated primarily by a desire to speed up delivery. Embassy is inclined to view the Jamaican approach as a healthsome indication that GOJ at the technical level is beginning to appreciate the ramifications of the Cuban problem.

Action: State (Army ACSI). Info: DCSOP, OSA, JCS, USDEL IADB. DA IN 16465.
