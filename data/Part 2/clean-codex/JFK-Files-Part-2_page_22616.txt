For example, we had certain political overtones where we were used in an affirmative way under President Eisenhower.

He wanted to make a real attempt to see if there could ever be a peace in the Middle East. And with Allen Dulles (Alan Dulles), his brothers, and myself, and Kermit Roosevelt, we put up a plan to bring in Robert B. Anderson, who had been Assistant Secretary of Defense.

And he was accredited as special emissary from the President. And I handled his base to Israel, which was secret, black, as we call them, to see Ben-Gurion—and Shaffetz, and later Mayor Kollek, who was in the Prime Minister's office.

And we took him back to Rome. And Kermit Roosevelt would take him over to see Nasser. And this went on back and forth on many trips, until the conclusions were reached that there could not be any peace.

But those types of initiatives could be done without publicizing or without destroying it.
