The risk, or if there were security problems, would be handled through MHMUTUAL.

What we would do when it came to aviation and marine was go to one of the cooperating companies and see if they wanted the insurance for their own accounts, because we had made arrangements that if there were operations security problems in any one claim, they could handle the investigation so it would not be a problem if they wanted to take it for their own account. If they did not want to take it for their own account, or did not want to take it all for their own account, they might then cede back to us what they did not want. It would then be underwritten technically out of MHMUTUAL's account, although they would be the ostensible insurers.

Several of the aviation entities, other than Air America, which dealt almost exclusively with regular commercial insurance companies, did not feel quite secure with regular insurance. They turned to MHMUTUAL. MHMUTUAL would in turn offer the risk to one of the cooperating insurance companies. If they would take the risk, they would then negotiate the premium with the proprietary. If they did not want the risk, then they would cede back to us and we would take the portion of the premium that should come back. Usually, if it went through a company, they would take a small underwriting premium to pay their costs, 3 percent or something like that. The rest of the premium would go back into MHMUTUAL if they were actually taking the risk.

In 1970, the Inspector General conducted a survey of the securities held by MHMUTUAL. He concluded that the project seemed to be secure in its operations in the fields of insurance and investment.
