Oral instructions conveyed to Assistant Secretary of State Woodward, a plan is desired for the indicated contingency.

Related documents indicate that the subject matter of the contingency referred to in NSAM 100 was the “possible removal of Castro from the Cuban scene.”

(Bundy Ex 3A, Minutes of Special Group Meeting, October 6, 1961, p. 1.)

The Minutes of the Special Group meeting on October 6, 1961, (the day after the issuance of NSAM 100) state that the Group was told that in addition to an overall plan for Cuban covert operations, a contingency plan in connection with the possible removal of Castro from the Cuban scene was in preparation. (Bundy, Ex 3A, Memorandum for the Record of Special Group meeting, October 6, 1961, p. 1.)

In addition, a Memorandum for the Record by Parrott on October 5, 1961, states that Parrott informed Assistant Secretary Woodward’s Deputy that “what was wanted was a plan against the contingency that Castro would in some way or other be removed from the Cuban scene.” (Bundy, Ex 3B, p. 1.) Parrott’s memorandum further stated that in preparing the plan, “the presence and positions of Raul (Castro) and Che Guevara must be taken into account,” and that General Taylor had told Parrott he preferred “the President’s interest in the matter not be mentioned” to Woodward. (Bundy, Ex 3B.) Parrott’s memorandum also stated that “on the covert side, I talked to Tracy Barnes in CIA and asked that an up-to-date report be furnished as soon as possible on what is going on and what is being planned.”

(Bundy, Ex 3B, p. 1.)

Woodward at that time was Assistant Secretary of State for Latin American Affairs.
