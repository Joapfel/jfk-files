Anybody. It was to provide information to them because their name would come up somewhere in a case.

Mr. Wides. What kind of assistance? You mean more than just a chance to interview them?

Mr. Sarno. No, just interview. They never used them to run a man on the street or anything like that, no, sir.

They tried to get one individual one time and they didn't know that he worked for us and they tried to hire him and he came back and reported it. We had to advise them that he was already working for us.

Mr. Wides. And you had 115 men and all—

Mr. Sarno. There were women also, sir.

Mr. Wides. And women, and all they were doing was interviewing refugees and writing reports on exile potential activities against Cuba?

Mr. Sarno. I would say that was the biggest majority of their job.

Mr. Wides. What was the rest of it?

Mr. Sarno. They also went out, if our station was going to run an operation and they needed X amount of bodies, certain specialized personnel, the MINS would go and locate the personnel, talk to them, spot them, get their personal history from them, send it in, and these people would be traced out and checked and cleared and eventually introduced to the station case officer who would use them in our operations.
