4. GOHAVEN:

a. GOHAVEN was contacted only once on 9 January. He was told that, in recognition for his past services and his excellent behavior at DIRABALO, those concerned with his case were making an effort to obtain his entry into the United States or the U.S. Army. He was reminded several times during the discussion that this effort might not be successful. He was also told that his transfer from DIRABALO was necessary in the very near future and that, in the event that his entry into the United States or the U.S. Army was still pending, he would be moved to Syros. He was told that his transfer to Syros would be certain if he was not permitted to enter the United States or the U.S. Army. At this point he was asked to sign the Syros agreement; he refused, stating that he could not sign any agreement which indicated a voluntary severance of relations with the Americans. However, GOHAVEN added that he would not resist his transfer to Greece should the Americans desire to send him there. He pointed out that living conditions at Syros were such that his health would certainly deteriorate and, being a Bulgarian, he could not look forward to fair treatment at the hands of the Greeks.

b. GOHAVEN was instructed not to reveal to anyone what took place during the discussion and was told that he would without question be sent to Syros if he violated these instructions. He stated that he would follow instructions and would gladly sign a statement to that effect.

CHRISTIAN M. FREEH,  
Acting Chief, SE

Athens (Athenas): 3 with attachment under separate cover. Two copies of the Syros agreement in Greek; one copy of the Syros agreement in English.
