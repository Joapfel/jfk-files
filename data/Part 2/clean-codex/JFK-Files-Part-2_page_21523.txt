Mr. Harvey. Well, very briefly, that is an operation that Frank Rollett (Frank Rowlett) and I conceived some time before I went to Berlin.

Senator Tower. You and who?

Mr. Harvey. Frank Rollett (Frank Rowlett), who for many years was one of the, if not the most, knowledgeable senior officers in the cryptologic group. And one of the reasons I went to Berlin was to attempt to mount this operation.

In substance, what it amounted to was setting up a fairly complex cover within a cover installation in West Berlin and driving a tunnel approximately 500 meters, well underground, across the sector boundary, and tapping into three main telephone, telegraph and teleprinter cables.

I think we had a total of around 900 circuits of these, which were, among other things, the main circuit between the Zossen-Lowersdorf (Zossen-Wünsdorf) and command headquarters of the Soviet Group in Bunsdorf, and the outlying commands in Germany and the headquarters in Moscow.

We also tapped into a number of Soviet Embassy circuits in Unter den Frund and Unter den Linden in East Berlin, including a number of intelligence circuits which unfortunately were not really readable.

Senator Tower. Were not what?

Mr. Harvey. Were not readable.
