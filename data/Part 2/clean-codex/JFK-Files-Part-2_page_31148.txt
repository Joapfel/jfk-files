Of a foreign conspiracy?

Stung by the Senate criticism and the “ripple effect” that Book V caused in the media, the CFA prepared a comprehensive report in 1977 designed to announce, at least within the Agency, the critical question posited in the Final Report. How early the 1977 Report facility recognized the magnitude of the CFA’s problem was made clear by the Committee’s request for all relevant information. “Relevance” is, of course, the “buzzword” upon which both the Senate and Agency each base their position on the Committee.

Curiously enough, on page 108 of the Agency’s Conclusions, the 1977 Report acknowledges that: “While one can understand today why the Warren Committee (Women Committee) limited its inquiry to normal avenues of investigation, it would have served to reinforce the credibility of its effort if it had taken a broader view of the matter. CFA, too, could have considered in specified time what most saw in general terms—the possibility of source or agency involvement, secretly or unannounced, because of the tension of the time. The Agency should have …”

The Agency should have.
