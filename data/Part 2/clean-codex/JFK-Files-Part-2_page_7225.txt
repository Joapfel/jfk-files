Looking back on the record of the past twenty-five years, the Ambassador said he concludes that in fact the ambassadors in Bonn had been kept appropriately advised and the absence of flaps would substantiate the judgment exercised by the respective station chiefs over the years.

5. Mr. Aaron discussed at some length with the Ambassador the feasibility of charging the Ambassador with greater direct responsibility for supervision of the clandestine effort.

Ambassador stated that if it were assigned to him as a mission we would manifestly have to discharge it and would, as he saw it, probably need a staff of at least two competent officers to carry out the practical staff work on his behalf. Mr. Aaron then raised the question of the British system, about which the Ambassador made the pertinent observation that it was a question for the government to decide.
