Dear Sir:

I wrote a letter on February 15, 1978, to Mr. James Callaghan, Prime Minister. I enclosed registered mail No. 251067.

On July 18, 1963, I was in London, England, at the airport. Immigration officer No. 175—I had no visa—placed me under house arrest. Mr. Smith, in police uniform, secret service, bought me a steak dinner, and a police officer interrogated me. He asked me what the Embassy of the U.S.S.R. said. What did I hear in Vienna, Austria? I said I had heard that one American defector, Mr. Lee Harvey Oswald, went to Russia and returned to the United States of America. This man was preparing to kill—to assassinate—President John F. Kennedy.

Mr. Smith, police officer, asked me, “Do you know his name?” I said his name started with “O.” His name was published one time in the Washington Post. Mr. Smith, police officer, said it was easy to find.

I am searching for this file in British police, British intelligence, and British immigration records. I am asking your Embassy to help find the truth, and I ask your government to forward my record of July 18, 1963, to President Jimmy Carter and Senator Daniel K. Inouye, Chairman of the U.S. Senate Select Committee on Intelligence. I enclose a copy of his letter to me. I also enclose a copy of the letter I sent on February 17, 1978, to the Russian Ambassador.

Your justice will always be appreciated. My incoming mail is controlled by F.B.I. agents. Some of the mail I do not get. It is not forwarded to me. The F.B.I. suppresses my information and tortures me with drugs. Twenty-seven years of anesthesia gas and freezing me—pushing drugs into my mouth.
