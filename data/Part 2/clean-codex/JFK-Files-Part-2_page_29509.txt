Application at that time had been to remain in USSR and for temporary extension of his tourist visa pending outcome of his request. This application, according to Oswald, contained no reference to Soviet citizenship. Oswald stated that he had been employed since 13 January 1960 in Belorussian Radio and TV Factory in Minsk, where he worked as a metal worker in the research shop. Oswald was married on 30 April 1961 to Marina Nikolaevna Pusakova, a dental technician born in July 1941 in the USSR. No HDQS traces. He attempted to arrange for his wife to join him in Moscow so she could appear at the Embassy for a visa interview. His American passport was returned to him. The U.S. Embassy in Moscow stated that twenty months of realities of life in the Soviet Union had clearly had a maturing effect on Oswald.

Latest HDQS information was a State Department report dated May 1962 saying that the State Department had determined Oswald was still a U.S. citizen and that both he and his Soviet wife had exit permits. The Department of State had given approval for their travel with their infant child to the USA.

4. Station should pass information in the reference and paragraph one to the U.S. Embassy, Federal Bureau of Investigation, Navy, and Immigration and Naturalization locally. Information in paragraphs two and three originates with the State Department.

5. Reference and possible identification being disseminated to HDQS of the FBI, State Department, Navy, and I&NS. Please keep HDQS advised on any further contacts or positive identification of Oswald.
