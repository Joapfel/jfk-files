Intelligence Coverage of Friendly Foreign Countries

File: 05-63118 (Butile: 05-63118)

I. Background, Scope and Objectives

The program was inaugurated in 1954 and aimed at developing positive intelligence information, as well as data of value to FBI investigations. Expansion of the program took place in 1955 and 1956, and during 1957 and 1958, as well as in 1970, additional coverage was approved.

The program has three principal phases as follows:

a. Regular notification to the FBI by either U.S. agencies regarding the arrival in the U.S. of intelligence personnel from non-Soviet bloc countries. This phase consists chiefly of file reviews at the State Department (State of Government) at nominal expense.

b. Maintenance of special coverage on official establishments of selected non-Soviet bloc countries with the approval of the State Department and the Attorney General. We currently have special coverage on eleven diplomatic embassies of seven non-Soviet bloc countries and three additional installations as recently approved by the Attorney General. Countries currently covered are Pakistan, Israel, Jordan, the United Arab Republic, Chile, Bolivia, and Peru. Our cost in operating this phase of the program is related to translation of the intelligence product and salary of personnel who are given special coverage equipment. Costs vary, analyzed on an individual basis each time the special coverage is justified. This justification is based on the value of the data developed.

c. This phase relates to the development of live sources within diplomatic embassies of non-Soviet bloc countries, as well as the development of individuals so closely connected with official establishments that they are able to provide accurate, up-to-date intelligence information. We now have 47 actual and 22 potential live sources.
