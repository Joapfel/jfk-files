Women's National March on the Pentagon, April 10, 1971; Internal Security—Information Concerning.

On April 10, 1971, at 10:40 a.m., special agents of the FBI observed about two hundred white female individuals in their early twenties begin to assemble on the grassy area at the southeast corner of Ninth and Constitution Avenue, N.W., Washington, D.C. (WDC). The group then waited around, with the size of the group increasing steadily. Many of the females had women liberation emblems painted on their faces. Others had “FREEDOM” printed along with emblems. On their clothing, some had “NOTHING LEFT TO LOSE.” Entertainment was provided by the participants, which consisted of singing and skits rendered in front of an open truck with an amplifier.
