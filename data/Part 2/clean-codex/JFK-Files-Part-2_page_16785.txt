Report of the Laboratory, Federal Bureau of Investigation, Washington, D.C. 20535.

To: FBI, Chicago.

Re: Sgt. Daniel R. Groth, et al.; Chicago police officers; Fred A. Hampton (deceased), et al.—victims. Summary punishment—CR.

Date: 3/4/70.

Specimens received: 2/11/70.

Q162: Double bed mattress, Wonder Rest, FBI No. 301.

Q163: Box springs, twin size, blue striped, FBI No. 302.

Q164: Mattress, Slumber Industries No. 97765, blue-white, FBI No. 303.

Q165: Box springs, Slumber Industries No. 97929, blue-white design, twin size, FBI No. 306.

Q166: Mattress, Posture Firm, twin size, FBI No. 307.

Q167: Mattress, yellow and gray, twin size, FBI No. 308.

Results of examination:

A radiograph of the Q162 mattress revealed the presence of a bullet in the center of the mattress, 10 inches from the end opposite the label “Restonic II.” Fuller.” A probe of the mattress in this area was made, and a .30 Carbine Norma bullet was recovered. The absence of sufficient individual characteristics on this specimen precluded the possibility of identifying it with the particular weapon firing it. The general rifling characteristics on this bullet, consisting of twelve lands and grooves, right twist, are the same as those on test bullets fired from the .30 caliber Universal Carbine, Serial Number 66174, specimen K27.

A radiograph of the Q162 mattress revealed the presence of small metallic particles in the lower left side of the mattress. A probe of this area was made, and three small lead fragments, the sources of which are not known, were found. Corresponding holes of entrance in the mattress cover were noted.

No other metal fragments in this mattress were detected in the radiographs.

No foreign metal particles were found in the radiographs of the Q163 specimen.
