On December 6, 1969, Officer James Neilson, Chicago PD, advised that Robert Rush, Deputy Minister of Defense, Illinois BPP, publicly surrendered at noon to Commanders Robert Harness and George Sims of the Second and 11th Police Districts, respectively. Rush was charged with failure to register a firearm as a result of a raid on his residence, 2030 South State Street, on December 5, 1969. The surrender took place on the stage of the Capital Theatre, 79th and South Halsted Streets, during an Operation Breadbasket meeting that was in progress and attended mostly by Negroes. No incidents were reported or arrests made as a result of the above arrest, and those at the theatre remained calm.

Sources advised that various memorial services were being held in the ghetto area of Chicago for Hampton, but no incidents or arrests were reported. The PD source also advised that Hampton was waked at the A. A. Rayner and Sons Funeral Parlor, 3654 West Roosevelt Road, during the evening of December 7, 1969.

Chief of Police Dominic Cimino, Melrose Park, Illinois, PD, advised on December 6, 1969, that services for Hampton were to be held from 7:00 to 9:00 p.m. on December 8, 1969, at the First Baptist Church, 2141 Main Street, Melrose Park. Following the services, the body would be shipped to an unknown address in Louisiana.

Captain Wilbert Samuel, Maywood, Illinois, PD, advised on December 8, 1969, that Hampton would be waked an additional day at the funeral parlor and the services would be held during the same time and at the same location on December 9, 1969.
