Mr. Wides. Until roughly?

Mr. Sarno. Until roughly the arrival of Mr. Dimmer. You have the date there. I think Mr. Dimmer's the one who brought down the fellow from Headquarters.

Mr. Wides. And these people received some training regarding the use of intercept equipment even if they had some experience as radio operators, isn't that true?

Mr. Sarno. Sir, I really don't know.

I know that I never brought an instructor down to train them in the use of radio equipment. These individuals had worked in this work in Cuba before they came out—

Mr. Wides. In intercept work like this?

Mr. Sarno. In intercept work, yes. We had two very brilliant fellows who were the top people in the communications field in Cuba.

And most of the radio operators had had up to twenty years experience, they were elderly people, and they had been in radio and they could read and they could send it out very well.

Mr. Wides. So your testimony is that—

Mr. Sarno. I do not recall them ever having been trained by anybody, in any sense.

Mr. Wides. Or communications in general?

Mr. Sarno. Now communications generally, I cannot say that.
