Vice-Intelligence Division, Montgomery County Police Department.

Continuing Investigations Reports.

Carrafa, Eugene Michael.

Date: 08-08-76. Source advises that Carrafa's brother arrived in Maryland on 08-07-76 from Connecticut; same operating 1975 Buick, 4-door sedan, green, Connecticut registration KE 9712; listed to Matthew Carrafa, 271 Citizens Avenue, Waterbury, Connecticut. Matthew Carrafa allegedly stated that if the police did not find his brother's murderer in sixty days, he would have things taken care of.

1800 hours — Telephone interview with Stanley Tashoff, Esq., wherein Tashoff related the following: Incorporated Knohl & Carrafa about a year ago. Knohl & Carrafa was in the office Tuesday, 07-27-76, regarding a breaking and entering at Gino's Classic Cars, and police wanted Carrafa to take a polygraph. He did not admit to making a false report, but Tashoff says, “regardless of my feelings on the subject which wouldn't make any difference one way or the other,” he said the place was broken into on Friday. Knohl was cordial towards Gino, always cordial, and asked Tashoff to arrange with police to stop hassling Gino about the break-in. Tashoff heard from Jay Knohl regarding whether Carol Carrafa was going to New York alone to identify the body; he asked Tashoff to work something out where Knohl and his brother would make the identification. Regarding freezing proceeds or the company, Jay can sell the business tomorrow if he wants. Asked whether they both own an equal share in the business, Tashoff replied, “Can't tell you that.” Mrs. Carrafa told Tashoff she owned 20%; he told her he thought she owned 20%. He stated, “doesn't really matter, Jay owned 50%, I as trustee (Tashoff) owned other 50%—of that Gino had 20% and he could purchase the rest at his option.”
