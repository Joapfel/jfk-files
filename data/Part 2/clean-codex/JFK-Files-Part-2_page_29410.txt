After the assassination of John Kennedy, this procedure was changed and the film was cleared from the camera on a daily basis.

The CIA contract agent outside of the United States Embassy who was in charge of the photographic bases was Ramon-Joseph Alvarez Durant. Alvarez picked up the film, prior to the assassination, from the photo bases three times a week. Alvarez then took the film to his brother-in-law, who worked at night, to develop it. The brother-in-law also printed the film into eight-by-ten contact prints. After the film was developed and printed, Alvarez turned over the negatives and contact prints to Thomas Keenan.

Ms. Ann Goodpasture picked up the photo production if Mr. Keenan was not available. Mr. Keenan or Ms. Goodpasture would then bring the photographic production back to the Mexico City Station in the American Embassy. Mr. Keenan did not remember with certainty to whom he turned over the material, but believed it was either Ms. Goodpasture or Herbert Manell.
