Albany, New York

January 9, 1963

Demonstration Protesting United States Policy in Viet Nam, Washington, D.C., January 8, 1968

A source who has furnished reliable information in the past stated that the Women's International League for Peace and Freedom had circulated postcards in the Syracuse, New York, area, announcing the captioned demonstration, which would be under the sponsorship of the Jeannette Rankin Brigade.

The source noted that, according to the postcard, the date of the march had been changed to January 15, 1968. The source advised that the card instructed participants to meet at Union Station, Washington, D.C., at 11 a.m., January 15, 1968, and then walk to the Capitol. The source stated that no known organizations or individuals were planning to attend from the Syracuse, New York, area at this time.

A source who had furnished reliable information in the past advised that the Women's International League for Peace and Freedom is a movement which has its national headquarters at the Jane Addams House, 2006 Walnut Street, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania; its legislative office at 120 Maryland Avenue, Northeast, in Washington, D.C.; and its international headquarters in Geneva. It is an international, interracial organization striving for peace and freedom by nonviolent, democratic methods. It was founded in 1915, and its first president, Jane Addams, was the first American woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize. It keeps its membership informed on current problems through its legislative office in Washington and issues its own pamphlets, study kits, and fact sheets. It sponsors public discussions on such topics as disarmament, nuclear testing, and civil defense through its local branches, and initiates peace walks, vigils, and meetings.
