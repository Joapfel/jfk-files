Manuel Artine, MRR.

Eusebio Mujal, labor leader.

Jose Bosch, Cuban industrialist.

On July 26, 1963, Santiago Alvarez Rodriguez advised he had talked with Luis Somoza for about four hours while Somoza was in Miami during the middle of July 1963. Alvarez said that it was his opinion Somoza was acting as an intermediary between the United States Government and the Cuban exiles.

On August 20, 1963, Alvarez advised that nothing had yet developed concerning the Somoza plan; it was now not clear what Somoza was offering, or what the United States Government's position or attitude was in this matter. Alvarez said it was obvious Somoza could not afford, either as a private individual or a representative of the Nicaraguan Government, to back such a military program by Cuban exiles, and would have to have the backing and support of the United States Government to go on with his plan.

On July 30, 1963, Fermin Fleites, member of the Executive Committee of the MDC, advised that about a week earlier, a Nicaraguan Army officer had visited the MDC Headquarters and, in the name of Somoza, offered camps, military equipment, and bases in Nicaragua. This individual also offered to pay the expenses of moving the MDC operations to Nicaragua.

Fleites explained that the MDC had taken no positive action concerning this matter and had adopted a “wait and see” policy to ascertain what attitude the United States Government would take concerning this matter.
