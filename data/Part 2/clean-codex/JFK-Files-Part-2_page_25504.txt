ML: So, I was as surprised, I was very surprised. (Continued.)

FS: How long was it—did you leave New York to go to Havana?

ML: Fidel said to me, I said, “Is this really you, Fidel?” and he said, “Yes. I am in Havana Hilton. You call me back in room 2408.” And I said, “No, all right, I believe you,” I said, because how else would you have my number? He said, “I want you to come back. I think about you and I will send a plane, my plane, and you come back.” And it was a joke, really. I liked the Cocoa Glace; it's an ice cream in a coconut shell, and he had sent to my father's ship a case of it so I would have enough for the return trip home because, I don't know, I just talked about Havana, the island in general. So I still didn't believe he would send the plane.

FS: Did he send the airplane?

ML: Yes.

FS: What kind of an airplane did he send?

ML: A Cubana airliner with...

FS: Four engines?

ML: Yes, with two men, and two men came to my door. I was living at 344 West 87th Street at the time, on the second floor, and my brother was at a UN party. I was home alone studying. Oh no, in fact, I was reading a Herbert L. Mathews article on Fidel Castro and Cuba when the doorbell rang, and it was Jesus and Pedro. They showed me identification: one came in military uniform, the other in civilian clothes. One smiled, one didn't. One said, “Are you ready?” and one said, “We go now. Fidel want very much to see you.”

FS: (Laughter).

ML: And I said, “I didn't finish my homework yet.” I was in shock. I didn't know what to do. So they said they were staying in a hotel, that the plane would leave tomorrow, and asked me to be ready. Meanwhile, I hadn't even unpacked from the cruise.
