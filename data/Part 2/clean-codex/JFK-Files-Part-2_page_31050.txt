Betsy Palmer

Review of Files on Manuel Ray y Rivero, Volumes I–XVIII

April 24, 1978

Volume XI – October 1963–December 1963

1. Field Information Report dated 1 October 1963. Notes that a source, a former Cuban diplomat who is a reliable source, said Ray was in Tegucigalpa, Honduras, about a week or 10 days ago and had spoken with Foreign Minister Roberto Perdomo Paredes, who has a 201#). A radio station manager says Ray was there about 20 September.

2. Memo for the Record, 2 October 1963, from Alfonso Rodriguez. Met with Ray in San Juan on 24–25 September 1963. Said Ray was departing Puerto Rico on 7 October 1963 for Venezuela and perhaps Mexico. Ray and JURE were involved in infiltrating Cuba and subsequently invading.

3. Dispatch, 3 October 1963, to Chief, WHD, from CS/Panama. On 26 September 1963, HYSAGE-1 learned that Jose Puente Blanco had recently met with Ray in Costa Rica for a three-day meeting.

4. Draft, dated 4 October 1963, by Rodriguez. Subject: Manuel Ray Rivero.

Ray, unlike many Latin American political leaders, is convinced that organization and work alone can assure lasting political success. Ray’s philosophy is a way of life. He is a prodigious worker, given to studying details; training at the University of Utah was mentioned. Ray was devoting full time to JURE.

5. Contact Report, 15, dated 8 October 1963. Contact with an MRR member by CO Warren K. Guertler. Would like to know what position to take regarding Ray if A-19 asks about U.S. backing. A-19 considers Ray part of a Kremlin conspiracy to put someone in power in place of Castro. CO has told A-19 that MRR are CIA’s “chosen people,” but if someone in the U.S., perhaps the State Department, wants Ray, CIA will have to accept him.

6. Cable to Director from JMWAVE, dated 11 October 1963. DIR info, BARR, MEXI. Cite WAVE 5656. Persecution of JURE’s Mexico City delegation by local secret police. DOI: 26 September 1963.

Information received from Rogelio Cisneros Diaz. Cisneros advises the delegation to work clandestinely, operate in cells, and not maintain public offices as they had in the past.
