Summer 1965. According to a sensitive source:

As a result of Nosenko’s (NOSENKO’s) defection, many KGB officers were expelled from the Party and from the KGB. These included KGB Chairman Serov (SEROV), and Second Chief Directorate Chief Gribanov (GRIBANOV) had been expelled from the Party and from the KGB. Gribanov had been especially friendly with Nosenko, had given him many privileges, and they were “buddies and night buddies.”

Comment: Semichastnyy (SEMICHAŞTNYY), not Serov, was KGB Chairman in 1965. Serov became head of the GRU in 1958.

Winter 1965–1966

Source: A self-professed former KGB Second Chief Directorate agent, from KGB officer V.G. Svirin (SVIRIN), a colleague of her husband at the State Committee for Science and Technology (GKKNR) in 1960–1962. Source said that Svirin was later head of a KGB unit conducting operations against the American Embassy in Moscow.

Nosenko’s (NOSENKO’s) father was a Minister or General and his mother was Jewish and “always involved in some blackmarketeering.” Nosenko was not a KGB officer but a “civilian” connected somehow with the KGB. When Nosenko defected, everyone said he was detestable. Nosenko told the Americans about the microphones and things (sic) in the Embassy.
