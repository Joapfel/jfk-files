13 December 1968

Memorandum for the Record

Subject: NAGELL, Richard Case — #264170

Reference: State Department Airgram No. A-903 from Berlin — Subject: Psychiatrist's Report on Richard Case NAGELL, U.S. Citizen Released by GDR

Reference airgram furnishes background information on the subject and fills in gaps in the chronology of events in the subject's life of interest to the Agency. Of particular note is the fact that the subject was acquitted on 29 April 1968 after his claim of temporary insanity at the time of the crime found support in the expert testimony of a noted American neurologist, Dr. Edwin WEINSTEIN. An article in the January 1968 issue of RAMPARTS by William W. TURNER implied that NAGELL had been involved in the assassination plot against President KENNEDY and that he had been “salted away.” The subject denied any involvement in any conspiracy and denied that he had any information of use to GARRISON. The subject alleged that his fear that the CIA might try to “eliminate” him was planted in his mind by Mr. GARRISON. (See page 2 of interview report.) On page 3 of the interview report, the subject states he no longer believes that it is realistic to think that the CIA was trying to kill him.

George R. BABINEAU, Capt., MC, the psychiatrist at the U.S. Army Hospital, Berlin, who interviewed the subject, was granted a continuing liaison clearance on 17 October 1968 at the request of DDP/E/G/IS.

Dr. Edwin A. WEINSTEIN was security approved as a continuing contact through the Medical Staff on 13 July 1965. WEINSTEIN is with the Walter Reed Army Medical Center, Washington, D.C.

Nicey S. NEW, Jr. (Ricey S. NEW, Jr.), NAGELL's attorney, who was present during the interview, is with the Washington, D.C., law firm of NEW, MACKEY and GEORGE. Newspaper clippings from the WASHINGTON STAR for 17 March 1966 and the NEW YORK TIMES for 29 August 1965 reflect that NICEY has been associated with East Berlin attorney Wolfgang VOGEL and West Berlin attorney Juergen STANGE in a number of cases involving the release of Americans from East Germany. According to an FBI report dated 3 February 1967, these releases usually involved the payment of specified sums of money, and VOGEL and STANGE were allegedly reaping large monetary benefits for their services.
