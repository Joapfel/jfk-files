To: Washington, Honolulu.

From: Saigon 0966, 17 September 1963.

1. After making and breaking four appointments with CAS officer, General Tran Tu Oai sent message on 5 September via Le Trung Thanh that he considered it best in present climate that CAS officer not come to General Oai's headquarters owing to heavy surveillance.

Transparently, this excuse was indicated by assurances that General Oai thought it perfectly in order to continue visits to his headquarters' so-called clandestine enclosure, the gate to which is not more than fifty feet from the main gate. One surveillance would cover both.

2. In an hour conversation, Thanh and CAS officer went over various subjects, the first being an article on CAS in Vietnam Times, and CAS officer asked Thanh to give General Oai a full account of the conversation. CAS officer pointed out that, like most exposés, this was a mishmash of fact and fancy; that, to the extent it was accurate, the writer discussed joint projects which had been approved by the U.S. and GVN, in most cases by Counselor Nhu himself. It would therefore be the height of illogic for the U.S. to spend large sums and efforts on behalf of a government which we were simultaneously attempting to overthrow. CAS officer told Thanh to tell Oai that in such circumstances CAS had no choice but to review all projects.

3. Thanh brought various messages on which Oai asked for CAS officer's opinion. First was a query whether CAS officer agreed with General Oai that President Kennedy erred in stating GVN out of touch with people and also that Buddhists were being repressed.

CAS officer said he disagreed with Oai on both counts and concurred.
