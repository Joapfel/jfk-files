In April 1985, the Senate Select Committee to Study Government Operations with Respect to Intelligence Information published this Final Report (Volume II), which in essence faulted the CIA (CFA), and to some extent for its error of commission in not informing the Warren Commission of the Agency’s majority plot against the revolutionary government in Cuba and its purpose in attempts on the life of its premier, Fidel Castro. The Committee informed us that on pages 6–7 of its Findings, and one paragraph, sufficiency seems to sum up their position.

Senior CIA (CFA) officials should have realized that their agency was not utilizing its full capability to investigate Oswald’s (Owald’s) post-Castro and any Castro assassinations. They should have shown that CIA operatives against Cuba, particularly operations involving the assassination of Castro, needed to be considered in the investigation. Yet they failed to subordinate themselves to conduct an inquiry into whether they had been told of these vital facts. These officials, whom the Warren Commission had no right to infer, added to the Warren Commission that the CFR had no.
