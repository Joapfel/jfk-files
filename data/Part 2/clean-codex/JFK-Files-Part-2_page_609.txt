He said he knew everyone at the Cuban Embassy—Mexico (MEXI)—and could go to Cuba for Rowton.

4. Rowton slowed matters down by saying he had to go to Europe for about three weeks but that they would talk more about it upon his return. In turning conversation to his wife, Rowton learned she had a father in New York who left Cuba to get away from the revolution. Father and daughter are on good terms, and he writes her every week. His name is Chinese for “peach blossom,” and Chichay is the Cuban attempt at transliteration. Jurado spoke the Chinese name, but Rowton was unable to remember the phonetic pronunciation and could not ask for the spelling. Chichay’s mother is still in Cuba with her brothers and sisters.

5. LITAIN-T-2 has been cultivating Chichay for several months. He saw a letter on her desk one day in the consulate and asked if she knew Cuban stamps. Chichay said no, that it was from her father in New York. She is unsalaried at the consulate, and Curbelo, who according to LIENVOY was reported to be a friend of Jurado, has delayed sending her PHS to Cuba for employment on a salary basis. The reason for the delay is not known. She is friendly with Curbelo but not with LIRING-I. When their daughter was sick, LITAIN-T-2 gave Chichay money to take the child to the doctor.
