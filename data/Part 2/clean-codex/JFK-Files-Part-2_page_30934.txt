6. Intelligence Information Cable, dated 28 June 1966.

Source: qualified American observer.

On 15 June 1966, Rafael Garcia-Bango, Cuban exile in Spain, received a call in Madrid from Ray in Puerto Rico regarding Ray's plans to assume power in Cuba. Garcia-Bango told Ray it was essential that he come to Madrid before 25 June and return to San Juan in order to talk to a member of the Cuban delegation for the 10th Caribbean and Central American games in Puerto Rico, prior to the delegation's return to Cuba. Ray agreed to do so.

Ray was enthusiastic over the possibility of overthrowing Castro in the next several months. He knows Garcia-Bango has a bad reputation but feels he could prove useful. Also useful would be Garcia-Bango's brother, Jorge Garcia-Bango Dirube, who has a more respectable reputation. Ray also stated that he had indirectly received a message from Jose Llanusa Gobel and considers him a potential ally.

7. Harry Mahoney debriefs Ray, as described in a memorandum for the record dated 8 July 1966. Mentions Julian Gallo at this time, and Ray indicates he knows and respects him.

8. Memorandum No. 771, dated 1 September 1966, for Chief, OPS Cuba/WH Division/DDP, attention Mr. Harry Mahoney, subject: MIG-21. Attaching communication from DIA authorizing $100,000 should the MIG-21 operation succeed. Funds to come from the Air Force escrow fund now held by CIA. Other memoranda refer to MIG, which Ray said he knew a Cuban pilot would fly out of Cuba. Ray never comes through with any usable information and finally says he cannot contact the pilot.

9. Cable dated 4 January 1969, CITE WH/Miami 2233. Reference: Caracas 11490.

AMOT reports on 9 August 1968 indicate Ray plans to dissolve JURE and hold a congress of Cuban exiles in 1969 to create a new organization to fight Castro. Ray is working with Haitian groups and hopes that, if a new invasion of Haiti is successful, he will be granted operational bases and a radio station in Haiti.

10. Memorandum dated 8 February 1971 to the Director, FBI, from DD/P indicates that CIA has no current operational interest in Manuel Ray Rivero.
