McDonald showed a photo of Saul to a man. The man asked if McDonald's interest had to do with the murder of the President in 1963. McDonald said no; he wanted to talk to Saul about business.

By now, McDonald was sure that “Saul” was reasonably well-known in highly specialized circles. Moskalev is known in international scientific circles on the European continent.

McDonald says that the correlation between activity on Vozrozdenya and new types of influenza in the United States is disturbingly exact.

In the summer of 1970, on the outskirts of Gormisch, a man told McDonald that he was dying from radiation sickness. He was flown to Vozrozdenya, where he was led to believe there was a hospital that specialized in cases of radiation sickness and that he would be looked after by famous doctors.

He was interviewed by three Russian specialists: Emile Kroptate, Peter Gorsach, and Marion Lentzen.

Moskalev was a delegate to the Second International Congress on Radiation Protection, Brighton, U.K., 3–8 May 1970.
