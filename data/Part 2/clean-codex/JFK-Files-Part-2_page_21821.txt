Mr. DeMarco. What were these two people doing in Cuba prior to the time they were turned over to you and you met them?

Mr. Sarno. Collection of intelligence.

Mr. DeMarco. For the Agency?

Mr. Sarno. Yes, for the Agency. They were agents of the Agency, as was I. But they were residing in Cuba.

Mr. DeMarco. At any time while you were in Cuba, for that matter, at any time, did you become aware of any Agency relationships with—I'm going to use the term generically—organized crime. Do you understand what I mean by that?

Mr. Sarno. Yes, certainly.

Mr. DeMarco. Whether or not organized, people who you've read about in the paper, or you would consider to be involved in that kind of business, are you aware of any relationships between any Agency personnel in Cuba or elsewhere?

Mr. Sarno. None whatsoever.

Mr. DeMarco. During the period of time that you were in the casinos, playing blackjack and roulette and so forth, did you get an opportunity to meet the people that were running the casinos?

Mr. Sarno. No.
