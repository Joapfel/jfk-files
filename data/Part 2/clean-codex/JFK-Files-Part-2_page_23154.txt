Mr. Wallach. I realize again you may not have been familiar with it at that time, or even before the assassination, but what—and I'm not asking specifically for what facts, but you have testified previously that—I don't want to paraphrase your testimony, it speaks for itself—that you had no reason to believe that the Cubans were involved in the assassination of President Kennedy.

Mr. Halley. Yes, at the time that the incident occurred, and when Oswald was apprehended, I had no reason to believe that the Cubans were involved.

Mr. Wallach. Taking the other side of the coin, what evidence was there in your mind that they were not involved?

Mr. Halley. Well, because we didn't have any evidence that they were.

Mr. Wallach. Did you conduct any investigation at all to determine if there was any involvement?

Mr. Halley. I didn't have any charter for that. People were arrested. The FBI and the authorities in Dallas were pursuing the case. I had no charter to pursue that.
