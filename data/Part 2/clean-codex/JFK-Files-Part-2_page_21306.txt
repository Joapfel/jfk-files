Gleichauf, covert CIA representative, Miami, advised that about two months ago he went to Merola's home after Merola pestered him with phone calls offering to rent a plane or helicopter to CIA for use by Frente Revolucionario Democratico, an anti-Castro organization utilized by CLIP. Merola showed Gleichauf the boat “Terry Knott” (“Terry Knaut”) docked behind his home and implied this was his boat and that it had already been used by Frente. Merola seemed eager to get out of the U.S. and suggested CIA use him on some mission to Brazil, where Merola planned to work out a deal to ship whiskey to Brazil duty-free through a Brazilian politician. Gleichauf told Merola he was not interested.

After further checking, Gleichauf advised Miami that this boat had been used on one or more operations by Frente, but was not likely to be used again because it was in bad shape. The boat is 63 feet long and bears registration number 270404. Gleichauf stated that Merola was not working for CIA or Frente, so far as could be determined.

On April 5, David Glass, covert CIA agent, furnished Miami a list of boats being used in various operations in which CIA has an interest. “Terry Knott” was on this list. After several unsuccessful attempts to contact Varona personally, on April 13 Jorge Moran, secretary to Manuel Antonio de Varona Loredo, general coordinator of Frente, confidentially advised that Merola was working on the boat “Terry Knott,” owned by Movimiento de Recuperacion de la Revolucion, MRR, which is a member organization of Frente. Moran stated the boat was operated by Santiago Babum on April 8 and Jose “Pepe” Ariola was in charge of “Terry Knott.”
