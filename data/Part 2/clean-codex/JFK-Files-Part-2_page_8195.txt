Schedule B (Form 1040) 1987, Page 2.

Part V—Retirement Income Credit

A. General Rule

If separate return, use column B only. If joint return, use columns A and B for wife and husband.

Did you receive earned income in excess of $600 in each of any 10 calendar years before 1987 (and owned or widowed unless instructions, page 8-3)? Yes or No.

If answer above is “Yes” in either column, furnish all information below in that column.

1. Retirement Income for taxable year:

(a) For taxpayers under 65 years of age: Enter only income received from pensions and annuities under public retirement systems (e.g., Federal, State governments, etc.) included on page 1, line 9, Form 1040.

(b) For taxpayers 65 years of age or older: Enter total of pensions and annuities, interest, and dividends included on page 1, line 9, Form 1040, and gross rents from Part II, column 2 of this schedule.

2. Maximum amount of retirement income for credit computation.

3. Deduct:

(a) Amounts received as pensions or annuities under the Social Security Act, the Railroad Retirement Acts, and certain other exclusions from gross income. Column A: $1,524. Column B: $1,524.

(b) Earned income received (Does not apply to persons 72 years of age or over):

(1) Taxpayers under 62 years of age, enter amount in excess of $300.

(2) Taxpayers 62 or over but under 72, enter amount determined as follows:

If $1,200 or less, enter zero.

If over $1,200 but not over $1,700, enter ½ of amount over $1,200.

Or if over $1,700, enter excess over $1,450.

4. Total of lines 3(a) and 3(b).

5. Balance (subtract line 4 from line 2).

6. Line 5 or line 1, whichever is smaller.

7. (a) Total (add amounts on lines 6, columns A and B).

If line 7(a) is less than $2,296 and this is a joint return and both husbands and wives are age 65 or over, complete the Alternative Computation in B below which may result in a larger credit.
